26. PUBLIC HEARING regarding the proposed Ready Wyoming
Transmission Lines Project.
24 - 325
Public Hearing, Ready Wyoming Tranmission Lines
Project
Attachments:
Justin Arnold, Planning, said Electrical Consultants, Inc, on behalf of Cheyenne Light,
Fuel and Power, submitted several permitting applications to approve large
high-power transmission line projects. He referred to a project map and narrative from
the developer. Under Laramie County Land Use Regulations 2-2-127, the board is
required to hold a public hearing for every proposal to construct high-power
transmission lines in Laramie County to discern the public’s position on the project
and to assure that the project will be located to minimize disruption of existing county
residents and land users. Arnold said while the board does not have the discretion to
disapprove the applications, this hearing provides the developer a chance to hear
public concerns, answer questions, and make any changes deemed appropriate.

Arnold said the current use of the properties being crossed is primarily agricultural
land situated across Laramie County. There are a total of four lines proposed: 1) A
115kV line to connect the Cheyenne Prairie Generation Station west of Campstool Rd
exit to the Allison Draw substation about halfway between I-80 and Chalk Bluff Rd; 2)
A 230kV line to connect Sweetgrass substation and head in a generally northeasterly
direction across the county, into Goshen County, and eventually into Nebraska; 3) A
230kV line to connect the West Cheyenne substation and travel generally north across
the county into Platte and then Converse County, ending at the Windstar substation
outside of Glenrock; and 4) A 230kV line to connect the West Cheyenne substation
just outside of the North Range Business Park to the proposed Sweetgrass
substation on the east side of South Greeley Hwy.
Chairman Lovett opened the hearing for PUBLIC COMMENT. Art Van Nostrand,
resident, had three questions: 1) Why was the electric line off of Chalk Bluff Rd being
run onto properties and the need for an additional maintenance road? 2) What do the
