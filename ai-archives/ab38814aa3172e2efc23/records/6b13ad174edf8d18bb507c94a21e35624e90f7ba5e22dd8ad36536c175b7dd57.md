```text
WYOMING:
Section 1. That the owner(s) of the property described herein has (have) applied for a
zoning map amendment (“zone change”) for said property in accordance with Section 2.2.1,
Zoning Map Amendment, of the Cheyenne Unified Development Code (“UDC”).
Section 2. That on August 1, 2022, the City of Cheyenne Planning Commission conducted

Section 4. That, in accordance with Section 2.2.1, Zoning Map Amendment, Section 5.1.2,
Zoning Districts Established, and Section 5.1.3, Official Zoning Map, of the UDC, the

described as Lot 1 and Lot 1½ , Block 18, Re-subdivision of Lake Minnehaha Addition, Cheyenne,
Wyoming, is hereby changed to NR-2 Neighborhood Residential – Medium Density.
Section 5. That the City Engineer is hereby authorized and directed to alter and amend the

described in Section 4 above when this ordinance takes effect in accordance with Section 7 below.
```

```text
UDC-22-00248
August 1, 2022
The Future Land Use Designation for this property is and the surrounding area is Urban
Residential. According to comprehensive plan the optimal City zones for the

future land use designation is any one of the residential zones, including NR-2.

Based on the policies and future land use map, NR-2 zoning is appropriate at this location.

The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 1.

2) The application is compatible with nearby property, specifically considering existing zoning,
the Comprehensive Plan, and any other official plans and policies created under the guidance
of that plan for these areas; (UDC 2.2.1.d.2)

All surrounding properties are zoned MR. Both the MR and NR-2 zones are medium-density
residential zones. The difference is that NR-2 zoning anticipates alley-loaded development,
allows smaller lots, and has a higher design standard. NR-2 zoning is compatible with
surrounding zoning.

The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 2.

3) The suitability of the subject property for development according to the standards and uses of
the existing zoning district, compared to the suitability of the subject property for
development according to the standards and uses of the proposed zoning district (UDC
2.2.1.d.3)

The MR, as put forward in the UDC, is intended for larger-block, front-loaded, suburban style
development patterns (like Saddle Ridge or The Pointe). NR-2 zoning is intended for smaller-
block, rear-loaded, compact development patterns (like what is found in the Avenues).
Unfortunately, after the adoption of the UDC, a comprehensive map amendment was not
pursued, or neighborhoods like this one may have been changed to the new NR-2 zone. In short,
the proposed NR-2 zone is more consistent with the existing development pattern of this
neighborhood than the current MR zone.

The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 3.

4) The ability of the subject property and surrounding vicinity to be developed according to the
Subdivision Standards in Article 4 considering the proposed zoning (UDC 2.2.1.d.4)

The zone change is occurring in a pre-existing neighborhood. No changes to the street network
are proposed. The neighborhood meets the smaller block sizes anticipated for NR-2 zoning.

Accesses will not be changed with the zone change.

Infrastructure (streets and utilities) exist in the area.

The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 4.

5) The ability of the application to support the Intent and Applicability statements of the
proposed zoning district and achieve the Design Objectives of any specific standards. (UDC
2.2.1.d.5)
```

```text
The NR-2 zone is intended for a medium density and mix of dwelling types that promote a
consistent neighborhood character, with a compact development pattern and highly connected
street network. The existing neighborhood meets these objectives.

The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 5.

6) The ability of all potential future development to meet all applicable standards for
development in the proposed zoning district, including other development options if any
specific development proposed in association with the application is not executed after
approval. (UDC 2.2.1.d.6)

The NR-2 zone allows a variety of residential uses (as does the current MR zone). Should this
particular project require a different style of redevelopment, NR zoning provides a few more
options for infill residential development.

The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 6.

7) The review and recommendations of other reviewing bodies. (UDC 2.2.1.d.7)

No reviewing agencies provided recommendations regarding the proposed zone change.

8) Whether the application will harm the public health, safety, or general welfare. (UDC
2.2.1.d.8)

City staff is satisfied that this proposed zone change would not adversely affect the public
health, safety, or general welfare.

The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 8.
```
