RECORD OF PROCEEDINGS FOR THE GOVERNING BODY
OF THE CITY OF CHEYENNE
Office of City Clerk
 February 24, 2014
The Governing Body of the City of Cheyenne met in regular session on this date beginning
at 6:00 p.m. in Council Chambers.  Present were: MAYOR - Richard L. Kaysen; COUNCIL
MEMBERS - Mr. Allen, Mr. Brown, Ms. Broyles, Mr. Cook, Mr. Luna, Dr. Rinne, Mr. Shanor, Mr.

normal sequence on the agenda.)  Upon request by Mr. Brown, and pursuant to support received by

Reflections and Progress) on the Consent Agenda that was referred to Committee of the Whole would
be reviewed and discussed at the Committee meeting, open to the public for comments, scheduled

ORDINANCE - Amending the Cheyenne Unified Development Code (UDC), to adopt
amendments to increase the maximum Administrative Adjustment from 10% to 50% for infill and
redevelopment as initiated from the 2013 annual review.  Albert Simpson expressed his concern with
reducing standards and suggested the authority be left to the landowner on what they want to do
within the 50% limit.  Reporting for Public Services Committee, Mr. Shanor moved to approve on
third and final reading, seconded by Ms. Williams.  Mr. Shanor, seconded by Ms. Broyles, moved
to amend by substitute ordinance by renumbering the current Section 7 to 8, and inserting a new
Section 7 with the following language:  “In cases where more than 25% of administrative adjustment
is exercised, the developer will be required to notify all property owners within 140 feet of the subject
area boundaries, excluding rights-of-way.”.  Boyd Wiggam, Wyoming Liberty Group, spoke in
opposition to the amendment, commenting on the current process involving sign notification placed
on the property, that the amendment potentially promotes neighbor activism, and the need to protect
use of property for owners.  Jim Weaver, #1 Properties and commercial real estate broker, questioned
the intent and purpose of the proposed mailing notice requirement by the developer, and what the
next process would be by staff if responses are received as a result of the notice.  In response,
Brandon Cammarata, Planning and Development Director, expressed that the mailed notice
requirement may seem disproportionate to the minor adjustments being attempted to be addressed;
that the notice may increase approval times, and that there is nothing specified regarding a staff
and/or Council process relative to responses being received as a result of the notices.  Jan Nelson-
Schroll commented on property and neighborhood aesthetics, and balancing the rights and interests
of developing property owners and those of neighboring owners.  Comments followed on the intent
of the proposed amendment involving mailed notices; provisions of Wyo. Statute Section 15-1-603,
and wording contained in UDC Section 2.3.4 b. 3.  Upon inquiry, Dan White, City Attorney, advised
it was his understanding that the 140 ft. notification referenced in state statute concerns zone changes,
and commented that the purpose (i.e. ordinance topic generally) is a less significant change, dealing
with an administrative change during a site plan process.  Motion to amend by substitute failed.
