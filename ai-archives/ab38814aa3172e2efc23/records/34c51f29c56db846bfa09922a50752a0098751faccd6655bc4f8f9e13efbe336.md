Here are Engineering's comments on including Campstool Road and the UPRR right-of-way in the
Lummis/Read annexation:
1.
The proposed Lummis/Read Annexation includes railroad and public road rights of way for the
UPRR and Campstool Road.  It is our understanding that a portion of the Campstool Road right of way is
in the fee title ownership of the UPRR and was established under a lease arrangement for a County Road
(Campstool Road).  To the best of our knowledge, the lease has expired and is under renegotiation
between the UPRR and Laramie County.  In previous discussions with Laramie County, the City of
Cheyenne had tentatively agreed to take maintenance responsibilities for Campstool Road once a new
lease agreement had been established.  However, no such documentation has been provided to the City
of Cheyenne thus far to demonstrate that the issues surrounding this public road right of way had been
resolved and a new lease established.  With this in mind it would not be appropriate for the City of
Cheyenne to take jurisdiction of this public road right of way without a clearly defined establishment of the
road and conditions applicable.  Excluding Campstool Road would not affect the contiguity of this
proposed annexation as W.S.15-1-402(b) specifically provides for the exclusion of “… a public or private
right of way, a public or private transportation right of way…” for contiguity purposes .
2.
The proposed Lummis/Read Annexation would require further boundary survey to include the
UPRR and Campstool Road rights of way Westward to the Original City of Cheyenne boundary.  The cost
and applicability of these survey requirements by this applicant do not appear to be justified .
3.
The UPRR right of way further to the East has not been annexed, while areas to either side have
been annexed.  This demonstrating the precedence that UPRR right of way has not historically been
annexed as a part of the adjacent area annexations.  Annexing UPRR right of way could also put the City
in violation of W.S.15-1-412 which requires the written consent of the landowner of any parcel or
contiguous parcels in excess of 40 acres and $40,000 in assessed valuation.  UPRR appears to be a
landowner meeting that threshold, especially if the additional area requested to be included by Laramie
County is added.
CONCLUSION:  Until such a time as proper documentation is provided to show a clearly defined
establishment and continuing agreement with the UPRR for the Campstool Road (public road) right of
way, the City of Cheyenne should not annex said road to the City.  And to remain consistent, the railroad
right of way should not be annexed unless consent is received from the UPRR to be a part thereof.
Nathan Beauheim, P.E.
Assistant City Engineer
