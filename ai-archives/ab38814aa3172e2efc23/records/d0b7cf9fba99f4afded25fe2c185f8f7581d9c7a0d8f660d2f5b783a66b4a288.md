14. Consideration of three separate resolutions approving Cowboy
Racing, LLC, to conduct parimutuel wagering on live horse racing,
historic horse racing and simulcast events at 1720 Cleveland Ave,
1734 Meadowland Dr, and 714 W 20th St, Cheyenne, Laramie
County, WY. (Postponed from the Dec 17, 2024 meeting)
25 - 015
Resolution, Cowboy Racing, LLC, Parimutuel
Wagering, 1720 Cleveland Ave
Resolution, Cowboy Racing, LLC, Parimutuel
Wagering, 1734 Meadowland Dr
Resolution, Cowboy Racing, LLC, Parimutuel
Wagering, 714 W 20th St
Attachments:
Ryan Clement, applicant, gave a presentation on the three locations. He said Thunder
Plains is focused on Laramie County and the full-length track has been completed. He
said there are 312 acres in the Hillsdale area, half of which will be dedicated to
first-class horse racing. He informed they have partnered with 1/ST Racing, an
industry leader in safety and integrity.
Clement said they will use Historic Horse Racing (HHR) revenue to build the best
equestrian center in the region, rivaling that of any other equestrian center. He said the
facility will have the best horse racing in the Mountain West, drawing people from
Colorado, Nebraska and throughout the region.
Clement noted there are currently two operators for Off-Track Betting (OTB) and HHR,
and a third operator would open a competitive market.
Clement said OTB helps support the track, horseracing, and raises revenue for
breeders, award funds, and staff. He added the horse track will create job
opportunities and promote local businesses.

In response to a question from Chairman Malm, Clement said he had contacted local
residents about their concerns and the meeting was very positive. He expressed his
commitment to holding neighborhood meetings.
Commissioner Hollingshead asked if Clement had contacted local law enforcement
about the OTB establishments. Scott Homar, attorney for Cowboy Racing, LLC, said
they talked with Cheyenne Police Chief Francisco, who indicated that the impact on
law enforcement is negligible. According to Homar, the chief did not see a need for
additional officers because of the OTB facilities. Commissioner Hollingshead asked
about the economic impact on the county. Clement believes that after about five years,
the track will run from Memorial Day until Labor Day. He said the park's future plans
will bring in customers and create jobs for hotels, retail, gaming, and the sports
complex.
Commissioner Zwonitzer asked about on-site security. Clement explained that the WY
Gaming Commission requires every facility to meet the security requirements
stipulated in their rules. Commissioner Zwonitzer asked if the three locations Cowboy
Racing, LLC, is requesting will be the only ones. Clement said he would share his
sustainable OTB expansion plan with the board, but would not like to discuss it
publicly. He added they need to see if their business model is functioning as intended
before planning further expansion.
Chairman Malm explained that the location at 1720 Cleveland Ave was a fire station,
sold by the city of Cheyenne in Oct 2022 in order to build a new fire station approved
by the voters. Chairman Malm requested public comments not address the sale of that
property.
Chairman Malm opened the hearing for PUBLIC COMMENT.
Darin Westby, Dave DeLancey, and John Hess, residents, spoke in favor of the
locations. Residents expressed that the OTBs will help improve facilities, boost
economic development, and benefit the equine community.
Heather, Travis and Cardon Burnham, residents, spoke against the 1720 Cleveland
Ave location. They expressed concerns about facilities in residential areas, increased
traffic, and increased number of intoxicated people.
Mark Moody, City Council Ward 3, spoke against the facility at 1720 Cleveland Ave.
Commissioner Malm pointed out the city could have exercised jurisdiction on the
matter through zoning, but the city chose not to use it. He also noted legislation has
been proposed to give more authority to local government. In response to
commissioners’ questions about plans for the 1720 Cleveland Ave facility, Will
Edwards, Cowboy Racing, LLC, said the location is currently zoned commercial and
the building is in disrepair. He addressed questions about conducting a traffic study
and continuing community engagement. Commissioner Health asked if the location

ROW. Arnold said under the Laramie County Land Use Regulations § 2-2-127, the
Laramie County Board of County Commissioners is required to hold a public hearing
for every proposal to extend main lines in excess of 12” in order to discern the public’s
position on the project and to assure that the project will be located so as to minimize
disruption of existing county residents and land users. He said while the Board does
not have the discretion to disapprove the application, the hearing provides the
developer a chance to hear the public’s concerns, answer any questions and make
appropriate changes.
