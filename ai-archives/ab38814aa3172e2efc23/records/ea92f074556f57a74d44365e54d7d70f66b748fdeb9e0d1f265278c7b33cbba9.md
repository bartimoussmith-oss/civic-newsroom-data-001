Move to recommend the Governing Body approve text amendments to the Unified Development Code
Sections 1.4.4.c, 5.1.4 Table 5-1, 5.7.4, and 6.2.4 Table 6-2 as outlined in this staff report and

BACKGROUND:

The City’s Unified Development Code (UDC) was adopted by the Governing Body on January 23, 2012, by
Ordinance Number 3943 with an effective date of April 30, 2012.  The Unified Development Code is the
regulatory document carrying out the vision of PlanCheyenne and is reviewed annually pursuant to UDC
1.1.7 [Annual Review Requirement] to determine if any particular standard or regulation is timely,
relevant and adequately addresses current market conditions. In addition to the annual review, the
Planning and Development Director and City Engineer are authorized under UDC 2.4.1.b.3 to bring
forward text amendments on their own initiative.

The proposed text amendments are to the Child Care requirements of the UDC. The proposed text
amendments are to align UDC requirements with the Department of Family Services (DFS). The reason for
the changes is due to the confusion as to what is required when someone wants to open up a new child
care. The City’s regulations did not match what DFS required and made the process more difficult for new
child cares.

The first proposed changes are to the definitions. DFS has three types of Child Cares. Family Child Care
Home, Family Child Care Center, and Child Care Center. Staffs proposed amendment is to match DFS with
the definition of each. The UDC has four definitions for Child Cares which were similar to DFS but had
different names and broke Child Care Centers into two, a minor and a major. Staff proposes changing
Child Care, In-home-Minor to Family Child Care Home, change Child Care, In home-Major to Family Child
Care Center, and change Child Care, Center – Minor and Major to just Child Care Center. DFS does not

The second recommended amendment is to allow Child Care Centers as Conditional Uses in the LR, MR,
HR, NR-1, NR-2, and NR-3 zone districts. Staff’s reasoning is that the UDC allows Primary and Secondary
schools as conditional uses within these zone districts and there only slight difference between a school
and a child care. This would open up new opportunities for child cares in more locations while also having
them go through a public process to determine if the location is suitable. Churches can often be found in
residential zones, and a child care can be a weekday use of a church or a re-use opportunity of a church
location should the church cease operation.

The third recommended amendment is to 5.7.4, Child Care Uses. Staff proposes removing the table within
this section and replacing it with standards that match DFS requirements. DFS has specific standards that
all child cares are required to meet. Current UDC requirements add additional burdens to child cares that
DFS does not require or allows alternatives to. For instance, the UDC requires an outdoor play area, where
DFS allows for an increased indoor play area in place of not providing an outdoor play area. Staff feels that
if an applicant is meeting all DFS requirements, the only child care specific zoning requirements we should
really look at are the parking requirements (requirements standard to all uses in the zone district would
still apply – such as applicable landscaping and pedestrian connections). The proposed amendments to
Child Care Uses in 5.7.4 would also allow child care as a permitted accessory use to any permitted
commercial or industrial use. This would allow a large-scale business to have a child care on-site for
employees despite being in a zone which may not otherwise allow child care uses.

The last recommended amendment is to add the parking requirements into UDC Section 6.2.4 Table 6-2.
By placing the parking requirements into this section it would allow for additional ways to receive parking
credits and flexibility. Staff proposes that Family Child Care Centers (in home) be required to have 1 space
per employee and 1 visitor space. Staff proposes that Child Care Centers be required to have 1 space per
employee, 1 space for each 20 children at maximum capacity, and 2 required spaces. As an example, if a
Child Care Center has 60 children and 10 employees, the current UDC requirement would be 20 parking
spaces. The ITE analysis would suggest only 14 parking spaces are needed. Staff’s proposed changes would
require 15 parking spaces. A parking worksheet can be accessed here.

PLTA-24-5

May 20, 2024
This proposed text amendment is necessary to address an issue not anticipated in the
Comprehensive Plan when the Unified Development Code (UDC) was adopted. DFS has changed
its requirements over the years and the UDC has not been updated to match what changes have
been made. Plan Cheyenne encourages changes that align with market demand and child cares
are in demand, but the current regulations can make them challenging to fit into locations
throughout the City.

2. STATE STATUTORY REQUIREMENTS:
The amendment is consistent with the Wyoming Statutes, and specifically the Municipal Planning
and Zoning statutes. The following Articles are all listed verbatim from Wyoming State Statute.

WYO 15-1-601.
The governing body of any city or town, by ordinance, may:

(d) All regulations shall be made:
(i) In accordance with a comprehensive plan and designed to:

(B) Secure safety from fire, panic and other dangers;
(C) Promote health and general welfare;

(E) Prevent the overcrowding of land;
(F) Avoid undue concentration of population; and
(G) Facilitate adequate provisions for transportation,

Assessment: This amendment proposes modifying the Unified Development Code to align with
DFS regulations and make things clearer for those looking to operate a child care.

3. HEALTH, SAFETY, AND PUBLIC WELFARE:
“The amendment promotes the general health, safety and public welfare.”

This amendment provides aligning regulations with DFS and reducing parking requirements
slightly to help promote more child cares throughout the community. These changes will make it
easier for those looking to start up a child care as it would match DFS requirements, making the
process less confusing.

CONCLUSION:

Staff concludes that this text amendment meets the review criteria of UDC 2.4.1.d.
