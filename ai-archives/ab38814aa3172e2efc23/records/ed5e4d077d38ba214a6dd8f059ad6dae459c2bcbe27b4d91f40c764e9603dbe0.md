Ayres Associates was originally retained in late 2009 to design and provide construction
engineering services for the E. Pershing Blvd. Reconstruction Project.  The original project scope
was to rehabilitate E. Pershing Blvd. between Dunn Ave. and Rayor Ave. and reconstruct
Pershing between Rayor Ave. and Converse Ave., including the construction of the roundabout at
the Pershing Blvd. / Converse Ave. / 19th St. intersection.  The scope was subsequently expanded
to include rehabilitating Pershing between Seymour Ave. and Dunn Ave. due to bids on another
project coming in better than expected and freeing up additional funding.

The reconstruction of Pershing Blvd. between I-25 and Converse Ave. was originally master
planned by AVI in 1987.  They recommended splitting the reconstruction into five phases.  The
segment between Dunn Ave. and Converse Ave. was identified as Phase IV of the larger project.
 At the time, broken sidewalks/curb & gutter, drainage problems, excessive crowns, steep grades,
and very high on-going maintenance requirements were identified.  These problems have not
been fixed on this segment and have become increasingly worse.  Meanwhile traffic volumes
have continued to increase of this segment, growing from 12,700 vehicles per day when the study
was done to 15,000 vehicles per day in 2010.

At the time the master plan was done, Phase IV was planned for construction in 1995-96.  In fact,
Phase IV was not actually funded until the 2007-2010 1% Sales Tax with a budget of
$2,117,000.00.  Rather than base a major reconstruction project on a plan that was nearly twenty
years old, the Cheyenne Metropolitan Planning Organization hired Ayres Associates in 2008 to
update the corridor plan, as well as to develop a final recommendation for the reconfiguration of
the Pershing/Converse/19th intersections.

The Pershing/Converse/19th intersections have themselves been the subject of at least three major

to split the E. Pershing Blvd. Reconstruction
Project into two separate projects.  Each of the
two projects will be bid and awarded separately.   I
have attached a map showing the limits of the
various types of work and projects.

The first project is to rehabilitate Pershing between Seymour Ave. and Rayor Ave. and to
reconstruct the corridor between Rayor Ave. and Carbon Ave.  This includes replacing more than
3100 feet of water main as well as sanitary and storm sewer improvements.  This first project
was recently awarded to Reiman Corp. for construction in the summer of 2013 using 5th Penny,
Board of Public Utilities (BOPU) and Federal Highway Administration funding.  This project is
commonly being called the corridor project.

The second project will be to construct the multi-lane roundabout at the intersection of Pershing /
Converse / 19th.  The second project also includes reconstructing the block of Pershing between
Converse and Carbon; as well as water main and storm sewer upgrades.  This second project is
scheduled to be out to bid in the first quarter of 2013 for construction in the summer of 2013.
The second project will be financed with 5th Penny and BOPU funding.  This project is
commonly being called the intersection project or just the roundabout.

One advantage of splitting the project into two was that it allows the corridor project to proceed
independently of the right-of-way acquisition for a portion of the property at 1900 Converse,
which turned out to be far more difficult then ever envisioned.  This in turn is important in that
there are significant maintenance issues for both the City and BOPU that need to be addressed in
the reconstruction portion of the corridor project.

The additional design costs of expanding the project and splitting it into two separate contracts
was covered in Contract Modification #2 to Ayres’ contract, however the additional construction
administration time was not.  (Contract Modification #1 dealt with some design elements that
were not anticipated in the original scope of work.)

Task 1 – Prepare Documents for Condemnation.

This task includes money for time that was spent by Ayres and their subconsultants preparing
expert witness statements and collecting documents for routine discovery in the condemnation
case against 1900 Converse Ave.  Condemnation was never anticipated in the original scope of
work and no provisions were originally made for this type of work.

Task 2 – Hollywood Video Revisions

This task consists of designing a parking lot, including all necessary construction drawings, for
the construction of a parking lot on the City-owned land on the northwest corner of the
intersection of Pershing & Converse.  Under the proposed settlement with the owners of 1900
Converse, this parking lot will be conveyed to the 1900 Converse owners in partial exchange for
the property the City wishes to acquire.

Task 3 – Misc. Roundabout Design

This task adds additional design to add rectangular
rapid flash beacons to the crosswalks at the
roundabout and to change some details on the water
mains to reflect updated standards.  The portion
dealing with the water main changes is being paid by
BOPU.

Task 4 – Safeway Revisions

Throughout this project, the City has endeavored to
work closely with the surrounding property owners to
coordinate the City’s work with other things going on
in the neighborhood.  Nowhere has this been more
important, and more time consuming, than with
Safeway’s redevelopment of the Cole Shopping
Center.  One key item of coordination of plans is the
location of access points along E. Pershing.
Unfortunately, this changed after this portion of the
plans was substantially complete.  This change affected not only the main driveway into the
Safeway property, but also the medians (and their landscaping), water connections, striping,
concrete pavement joint layout, cross sections, and storm sewers.
