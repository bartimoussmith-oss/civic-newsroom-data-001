18. Consideration of a resolution to prohibit open carry for all county
buildings and facilities, effective July 1, 2025.
25 - 305
Resolution, Prohibit Open Carry for All County
Buildings and Facilities
Attachments:
Mark Voss, County Attorney, presented the proposed policy. He noted the policy is
due to legislative changes (HB172 - Repeal gun free zones and preemption
amendments) and modifications to WS 6-8-105.
Chairman Malm opened the meeting for PUBLIC COMMENT. Bill Weaver, resident,
asked if the resolution, under paragraph 5, would prohibit citizens from carrying guns
on county roads. Chairman Malm said that was not the intent or understanding of the
board. Mark Voss, County Attorney, said the county does not "own" roads nor does it
prohibit open carry on county roads.  He added the county does have an easement
interest, and he would not like the policy to be misinterpreted.
A motion was made by Vice Chairman Thompson, seconded by
Commissioner Heath, that this agenda item be approved.
A motion was made by Vice Chairman Thompson, seconded by
Commissioner Hollingshead, to amend paragraph 5 to read "including but
not limited to buildings and structures and their corresponding curtilage
owned and controlled by the county shall be prohibited."
Commissioner Thompson thanked Weaver for the comment.
Brad Lund, senior deputy county attorney, said the definition of property is borrowed
from areas of the statutes that provide more specific meanings consistent with the
policy approved in the previous item. Lund requested the item be tabled temporarily to
fine-tune the language, address the board's concerns, and ensure consistency in the
two policies for employees and for government buildings.  He noted the policies and
resolutions were developed in conjunction with law enforcement and their involvement
is critical as they will be ones tasked with enforcement.
Commissioner Thompson agreed to withdraw his amendment and motion.
The motion to amend was withdrawn.
The main motion was withdrawn.

Greg Golovanoff, Cesar Garcia, Clarence Fisher, Kevin Scott, Frank Waters,
Jeannette Kahler, and Patrick Formby were among those providing comments.
Comments ranged from concerns about proximity of gaming facilities to schools,
churches and residential neighbors, to the number of applications and permits
required for home businesses, to the need for affordable housing, and concerns about
annexations by the city of Cheyenne.
Most comments came from those concerned about annexation of their property into
the city of Cheyenne and requesting the county's assistance. Chairman Malm
explained there was no action commissioners could take on annexation, and the
proposed land use regulations would not affect annexation. He advised residents to
contact their legislators to address their concerns.
Commissioner Thompson said there is a purpose for land use regulations and
attempt to strike a balance between freedom of property owners to do what they want
with their land and those seeking to protect and preserve their property.
Arnold, said the proposed regulations provide more accommodations for landowners
by removing certain requirements and having better enforcement of nuisance
ordinances. He said the county has taken painstaking measures to involve the public
in the rulemaking process.
Hearing no further PUBLIC COMMENT, Chairman Malm closed the hearing.
