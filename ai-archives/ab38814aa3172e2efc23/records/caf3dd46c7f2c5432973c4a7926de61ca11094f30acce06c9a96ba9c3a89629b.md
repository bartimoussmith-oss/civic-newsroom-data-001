BACKGROUND:

The applicant is requesting approval of a preliminary plat for a 94.06-acre tract of land to create 161 lots for
future residential development. 149 of the lots will be for residential development, one lot will be dedicated
for a new school, one lot will be dedicated for a future joint Police and Fire Station and one lot will be for
future Multi-family residential, while the remainder of the lots are dedicated as open space tracks for the
integrated trail system. The land is currently vacant.

SURROUNDING LAND USE AND ZONING:

The property is zoned as part of the Sweetgrass PUD.  The Sweetgrass PUD is generally described as “a
±2,349.5-acre community envisioned as a distinctive mixed-use master planned village, integrating
retail, entertainment, lodging, employment opportunities, and variety of intimately scaled residential
neighborhoods.”  The district generally allows a broad range of uses. The MDR is intended to provide for
residential development allowing for a variety of single-family detached and attached dwelling units,
located nearby to schools and commercial centers. An extensive open space network should be
provided with within each MDR parcel. To promote greater diversity, lower density development, such
as that permitted within LDR districts, is permitted within MDR districts.

This development is in conformance with the principles and future land uses identified in
PlanCheyenne. The property is within the Urban Service Boundary and has access to all utilities.

The future land use map within PlanCheyenne identifies this area as developing with a
residential use. This plat proposes 149 residential lots with an incorporated trail system
connecting this development to existing and future developments.

Foundation 1 of PlanCheyenne encourages community growth. This development is in response
to market demands and is within the Urban Service Boundary. The development patterns
proposed by this plat are compatible with the designations found on the future land use map.

Foundation 2 of PlanCheyenne encourages new residential development to be compatible and
connected with existing residential neighborhoods. The proposed plat ties into the adjacent
platted neighborhoods.

Foundation 4 of PlanCheyenne encourages a connected, multi-model transportation system that
can accommodate future growth. The proposed plat connects to existing developments but is
constrained by topography to the north. A proposed trail system increases the potential for
pedestrian and cyclist connectivity.

Foundation 6 of PlanCheyenne encourages the creation of open space and trails and the
preservation of significant natural resources. The plat proposes dedicated open space lots that
will be a part of an integrated trail system throughout Sweetgrass.

The application complies with this review criterion.

and potential future development in the vicinity, including consideration of current and future
streets, open spaces, blocks and lots, and public utilities or facilities. (UDC 2.1.2.d.2)

Transportation Network:
The plat proposes dividing land Southeast of an existing street network and adding to the
network. The plat proposes adding nine new streets with three connections into the existing
Sweetgrass Drive and a connection into Sweetgrass 5th Filings subdivision. The proposed street
network is in compliance with the network proposed by the Sweetgrass development.

With the development continuing to move further south, a second major connection to the west
will be required with future subdivisions. Currently all traffic is being funneled to Sweetgrass
Drive and up to College Drive. An Emergency secondary access has been provided to the west
that will be built with this development.

Maximum and minimum block dimensions are established by the Unified Development Code.
Block 17, 19, 21, and 26 within this proposed subdivision do not meet the block standards put
forth in the code; however, the oversized blocks appear to qualify for exceptions and the overall
pattern still provides an interconnected system of streets. Staff recommends that block 17, 19,
and 26 qualify for the Natural Features and Open Space System exception (see exception 1).

Public Safety:

the City.   Recent preliminary plats have been required to contribute a monetary amount on a
per unit basis for public safety fees to be placed in the Development Impact Fund earmarked for
the purpose of enhancing the City’s fire rescue infrastructure. Staff has recommended a
(Condition 1.c) that the developer enter into a development agreement for this public safety
prior to final plat approval.  This dollar amount utilized in $756 per unit based on January 2021
costs.   The City will utilize that base fee and adjust for inflation utilizing the  construction cost
index for twenty (20) cities, as issued by the current Engineering News Record which is similarly
used for calculating infrastructure reimbursements pursuant to Cheyenne Municipal Code
subsection 1.16.070.b at the time the plat is scheduled for the Governing Body.  Based on
January 2021 numbers, the fee for 149 units is $112,644.  When adjusted for 3.78% inflation
through June 2021, the estimated total public safety fees is $127,279.94 ($112,644 * 03.78%).
This number will be finalized in the agreement for the time the plat is scheduled for Governing
Body consideration.

The Fire Department also asks that the applicant provide a temporary fire access to the west
with their future Engineering Plan Review submittal and subdivision construction. The applicant
has provided a document showing the proposed secondary access.
