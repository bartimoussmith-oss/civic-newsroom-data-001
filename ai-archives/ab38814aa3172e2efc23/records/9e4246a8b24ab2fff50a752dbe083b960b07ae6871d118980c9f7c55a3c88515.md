RECORD OF PROCEEDINGS FOR THE GOVERNING BODY
OF THE CITY OF CHEYENNE
Office of City Clerk
April 10, 2017
The Governing Body of the City of Cheyenne met in regular session on this date
beginning at 6:00 p.m. in Council Chambers.  Present were:  MAYOR – Marian J. Orr;
COUNCIL MEMBERS – Rocky Case, Bryan Cook (arriving at 6:03 p.m.), Richard Johnson,
Pete Laybourn, Mike Luna, Dr. Mark Rinne, Scott Roybal, Dicky Shanor and Jeff White.  Also
present:  Carol Intlekofer, City Clerk, and Sylvia Hackl, City Attorney.  The pledge of allegiance
was recited.
Consent Agenda.  (All agenda items listed with the designation of [CA] are considered to
be routine items by the governing body and will be enacted by one motion. There will be no
separate discussion on these items unless a member of the governing body so requests and
support by two other members is received. Any item removed from the Consent Agenda will be
considered in its normal sequence on the agenda.)  Mr. Johnson moved to approve, seconded by
Mr. Roybal.  Motion carried.  Voting “yes” -- all members of the governing body present.
 [CA]
Minutes from Regular Meeting of the Governing Body on March 27, 2017.  Motion (per
Consent Agenda) carried.
Mr. Roybal declared a conflict of interest (business interest in voucher payable to Tyrrell
Chevrolet) and vacated Council Chambers.  Mr. Luna moved to approve payment of vouchers as
presented, seconded by Mr. Johnson.  Motion carried.  Voting “yes” -- all members of the
governing body.  Following announcement of the vote, Mr. Roybal returned to Council
Chambers.
Mayor Orr opened a public hearing to receive comments and input for a transfer of
ownership for a retail liquor license application filed with the City Clerk’s Office for Evergreen
Restaurant LLC #5011, d/b/a Outback Steakhouse #5011, 1626 Fleischli Parkway, Cheyenne,
Wyoming (License currently held by Outback Steakhouse of Florida, LLC, d/b/a Outback
Steakhouse, 1626 Fleischli Parkway, Cheyenne, Wyoming).  Brian Proffitt, attorney representing
Evergreen Restaurant Group, explained the transfer request is due to franchise restructuring.
There being no further comments or input, Mayor Orr closed the hearing and referred the
application to Finance Committee.
Mayor Orr opened a public hearing to receive comments and input for a retail liquor
license transfer of ownership application filed with the City Clerk’s Office for Deep South, LLC,
d/b/a Redwood Lounge, 2105 East Lincolnway, Cheyenne, Wyoming (License currently held by
High Plains Entertainment, Inc., d/b/a Redwood Lounge and Package Liquor, 2105 East
Lincolnway, Cheyenne, Wyoming).  Wayne Layton, applicant, commented in support of the
application and his desire to make business improvements.  There being no further comments or
input, Mayor Orr closed the hearing and referred the application to Finance Committee.

ORDINANCE – 3 READING – Creating the Civic Center Commons Project
rd
Management Team and providing procedures for the operation thereof.  Jack Speight spoke in
opposition of the ordinance.  Topics included in his comments were: Future developments and
improvements in the West Edge area; private investors; funding sources; FEMA grant and 6th
penny funds; site remediation, environmental studies, and developing the area in phases.  During
his comments, Mr. Speight displayed and referenced an aerial map, and submitted informational
handouts (on file with City Clerk’s office).  Gene MacDonald, Ayres Associates, provided
detailed information on the project.  Topics included in his comments were: Expertise of Ayres
staff involved in the project; environmental site assessments and studies; voluntary remediation
program; FEMA grant and EPA revolving loan program; cost benefit ratios, and drainage paths
and flood control measures for the Capitol Basin, 19 and 26 St. drainage projects and the
th
th
downtown area.  The following expressed concerns with the Civic Center Commons Project,
including problems that had been raised relative to the use of federal funds and effect of reduced
parking for Civic Center patrons:  Barbie Harrington, Major Dorr and Brad Harrington.
Speaking in favor of the project involving redevelopment of the area and addressing flood
control problems were:  Dean Dexter and Dave Teubner, Warehouse 21; Carrie Hufney; Howard
Rodack; Tony Lucero, A & C Feed, and Joe Dougherty.  Reporting for Public Services
Committee, Mr. Roybal moved to approve on third and final reading, seconded by Mr. Case.
Mr. Shanor moved to amend by substitute, seconded by Dr. Rinne.  Discussion included content
of the proposed substitute, which would create a Civic Center Commons Project Oversight Team
(CPOT), instead of a Project Management Team, to oversee the administration of the project;
provide disclosure of documentation to FEMA and an amended scope of services to be provided
by DHM Design Corporation.  Commenting in support of the amendment, including
redevelopment of the area and implementing flood control measures were: Mr. Speight, Jordan
Dean, Danielle Marks, Tom Dickson and Glen Garrett.  Discussion followed and included the
following topics: Lack of timeliness in receiving the proposed substitute prior to the meeting;
transparency; FEMA involvement, and the importance of flood control in the downtown area.
Upon inquiry to City Council members and there being no objection voiced, Mayor Orr

expressed her concern with the lack of timeliness in receiving the proposed substitute for review;
provided information on project funding sources and status of the FEMA grant, and posed
questions with regard to whether the substitute contained a sunset clause, whether DHM had
been contacted concerning a change in scope of work under their contract, and whether it was
applicable to name a specific design company by name in an ordinance.  In response, Mr. Shanor
stated a sunset provision was contained in Section 4(A) of the substitute; that DHM has been
contacted and they are willing to look at additional services, and that DHM has a current contract
with the City specific to the project.  Upon inquiry, Sylvia Hackl, City Attorney, advised that if
there is further amendment made to Section 4(A) clarifying an ordinance “sunset” date, there
shouldn’t be a major concern with referencing DHM in the ordinance.  Motion to amend carried.
Voting “yes” -- all members of the governing body with the exception of Mr. Johnson and
Mayor Orr voting “no”.  Mr. Shanor moved to amend by adding the following sentence at the
end of Section 4(A), seconded by Mr. Cook:  “This ordinance shall sunset upon completion of all
project activity and shall be of no further force and effect”.  Motion to amend carried.  Voting
“yes” -- all members of the governing body.  Dr. Rinne moved to amend Section 4(C) by adding
“and any other department that the project oversight team deems necessary” to the end of the
first sentence, seconded by Mr. Shanor.  During brief explanation of the intent of the proposed
amendment, Ms. Hackl offered to provide different wording on the topic.  Motion to amend
failed.  Voting “no” -- all members of the governing body.  Mr. Shanor moved to amend Section
4(C) by revising the first sentence to read as follows, seconded by Mr. Roybal: “The CPOT and
DHM will work directly with any City departments and employees deemed necessary for the
completion of the project.”  Motion carried.  Voting “yes” -- all members of the governing body.
Main motion as amended by substitute, as twice amended, carried.  Voting “yes” -- all members
of the governing body with the exception of Mr. Johnson and Mayor Orr voting “no”. (#4170)
