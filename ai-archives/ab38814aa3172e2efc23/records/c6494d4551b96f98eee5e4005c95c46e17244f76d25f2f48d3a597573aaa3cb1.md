WHEREAS, the State of Wyoming, through its State Loan and Investment Board, has
received Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security Act (CARES Act) funding from the
Federal Government; and
WHEREAS, the application process for CARES Act grant funding for municipalities and
counties has been a very time-consuming and confusing process; and
WHEREAS, many local governments are struggling to determine what information is
required in order to obtain CARES Act funding from the State of Wyoming; and
WHEREAS, CARES Act funding must be obtained and spent by December 30, 2020 or it
reverts to the Federal Government; and
WHEREAS, CARES Act funding is critical for local governments as revenues have been
decimated by the economic impacts ofthe COVID- 19 pandemic, compounded by the fact that local
governments have assumed the “first responder” role for protecting Wyoming citizens from the
ravages of this pandemic; and
WHEREAS, critical services of local governments have been curtailed, or eliminated, as a
result of the economic impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic and CARES Act funds are essential to
trying to maintain critical services; and
WHEREAS, massive investments in personal protective equipment, sanitizing, adapting to
telework environments, and other necessary steps have been undertaken by local governments to
respond to the pandemic and these investments were never contemplated in existing local
government budgets; and
WHEREAS, the current “first-come, first-serve” process for obtaining CARES Act grant
funding is fundamentally inequitable, particularly for those local governments struggling to
understand the required application information; and
WHEREAS, switching to a formula-based allocation model for distribution of CARES Act
funding eliminates the inequities of the current approach and provides a much more equitable and
efficient approach; and
WHEREAS, the proposed allocation model has only one serious flaw in the view of this
Governing Body and that is the 50/50 split between municipalities and counties; and
WHEREAS, the impacts of the COVID pandemic are felt most acutely in Wyoming’s
municipalities as that is where most people reside, it is where nearly all citizens obtain local
governmental services, and it is where citizens gather to interact with one another; and
WHEREAS, according to the Governor’s data, Wyoming’s population is 563,775. Of that
total figure, 387,978 (69%) of Wyoming’s citizens reside in recognized municipalities; and
WHEREAS, a far more equitable approach to the allocation model would be to apportion the
allocation based upon this population distribution. A distribution of 2/3 to municipalities and 1/3 to
counties would accomplish this end; and

WHEREAS, an additional consideration is how the CARES Act funds are distributed. The
current application process through the Office of State Lands, the Attorney General’s office, and
finally, the State Loan and Investment Board has proved to be too cumbersome; and
WHEREAS, an approach where the allocated CARES Act funding to each local government
was able to be distributed to each upon execution ofa “hold harmless” agreement whereby the local
government agrees to be financially responsible for any CARES Act reimbursements which are later
determined by the Federal Government to be inappropriate and need to be paid back allows a much
more efficient distribution of the available funding; and
WHEREAS, upon receipt of its allocation, each local government would be required to
deposit the funds into a governmental “special revenue fund,” withdrawals from which could only
occur upon provision of documentation satisfactory to the chief executive officer of the local
government (or that officer’s delegee) that the reimbursement meets the requirements ofthe CARES
Act and the Wyoming legislature; and
WHEREAS, any funds not drawn upon by the local governmental entity by December 30,
2020 would then be remitted back to the State ofWyoming for refund to the federal government; and
WHEREAS, records are to be kept by the local governmental entity and are then subject to
any future audit which may occur by the State of Wyoming or the Federal Government.
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE GOVERNING BODY OF THE CITY
OF CHEYENNE, WYOMING, that the Governing Body of the City of Cheyenne supports and
recommends that the Governor’s proposed allocation model for distribution ofCARES Act funding
to local governments be adopted by the State Loan and Investment Board with the caveats and
changes referenced in this resolution.
