Summary:

The Cheyenne Historic Preservation Board over the last two years has worked on creating a demolition
by neglect ordinance that seeks to have owners of historic properties maintain their properties and not
allow them to fall into disrepair. The requirements of the ordinance only apply to structures that have a
contributing status. A ‘contributing structure’ means a building, structure, site, feature, or object found
in local or National Register historic district that contributes to the historic character of the district.

The Cheyenne Historic Preservation Board presented the proposed ordinance at a City Council Work
Session in February of 2021, a public forum in March of 2021, and a Downtown Development Authority
Work Session in April of 2021. Following those meetings, the Historic Preservation Board met with the
Planning and Development Department, City Attorney’s Office, and the Building Department to discuss
the feedback from the meetings listed above and to make comments on the ordinance. The ordinance
was then edited by the Historic Preservation Board to incorporate the feedback received. Following
those edits the ordinance was given to the Planning and Development Director and Chief Building
Official for final revisions.

15.50.070 – Neglected structure.

A. Purpose–Authority–General.

1. Owners of certain historic properties are required to maintain their properties and
not allow them to fall into disrepair and to prevent gradual deterioration. The
requirements of this section are applicable only to certain properties, termed
"contributing structures" in this section. That term as used in this section is defined
to include designated historic structures and properties identified as "contributing"
in designated historic districts or individually listed.

2. It is a further purpose of this article to protect the health, safety, and welfare of the
citizens of the city by reducing the risks associated with neglected and deteriorating
buildings or structures, especially during the process of gradual deterioration.

3. It is the further purpose of this section to provide a just, equitable, and practicable
method to require the owner of record of the real property on which the qualifying
building is located or other person or persons who may have legal possession,
custody, or control of such building or structure (hereinafter the "owner") to
commence and complete necessary corrective action to repair and stabilize the
qualifying building and to authorize the city to perform such corrective action if the
owner fails to commence or complete such corrective action and recover the costs
of such corrective action from the owner.

B. Definitions.

1. "Abate" shall mean to bring into compliance, halt, eliminate, or remedy.

2. "Agent" means and includes any person acting on behalf of or in place of a property
owner, including tenant, manager, or lessee.

3. "Neglected Structure" means imminent total or partial destruction of or damage to
a historic structure or any portion thereof, due to the failure of the owner(s) to
adequately maintain or repair the structure, excepting acts of God or natural or
manmade disasters. Exposure to the elements and seasonal weather shall not be
considered an act of God or natural disaster.

4. "Director" means the Planning and Development Director or their designee.

5. "Hearing Officer" means an independent hearing officer appointed by the mayor
and confirmed by the city council in accordance with Chapter 2.92 of this code.

6. "Historic structure" means all structures contributing to the city's Contributing
Structures List and structures listed on the National Register of Historic Buildings.

7. "Maintenance and repair" means any work, for which a building permit is not
required by law, where the purpose and effect of such work is to correct any
deterioration, decay, or damage to a structure or any architecturally significant part
thereof and to restore or replace, as nearly as may be practicable, to its condition
prior to the occurrence of such deterioration, decay, or damage.

8. "Owner" means and includes:

a. Any owner or holder of any legal or equitable estate in real property, including
a dominant or servient tenement, the interest of a public trustee, lienholder,
mortgagee, or beneficiary of a deed of trust; or

b. The owner of record as reflected by the records of the office of the county
assessor, or county clerk.

9. "Person" means and includes a natural person, association, corporation, partnership,
limited liability company, and any other legal entity capable of owning, using, or
occupying real property.

10. "Preservation" means the act or process of applying measures necessary to sustain
the existing form, integrity, and materials of a contributing structure.

11. "Undue economic hardship" means the inability of the owner and parties in interest
to obtain a reasonable return or a reasonably beneficial use from the contributing
structure that is the subject of demolition by neglect, or the inability of the owner
and parties in interest to finance the required repairs to the contributing structure.

12. "Review Committee" means a group of people that are appointed by the CHPB
which investigate petitions filed with the Director alleging that a violation this
Chapter has occurred and recommend to the CHPB if further action shall occur.

1. Duty to maintain. Any historic structure shall be preserved against material
deterioration of exterior features and structural elements, as identified by the factors
set forth in subsection 3. below, by the owner of such qualifying building, and it
shall be the duty of the owner to maintain and repair the qualifying building in a
manner that prevents such deterioration.

2. Duty to repair. The owner of a historic structure shall, upon written notice of
violation from the city as described herein, repair such exterior features or structural
elements of the historic structure if any such exterior features or structural elements
are materially deteriorating or if the condition of any such features or elements is
contributing to material deterioration of the qualifying building including, but not
limited to, any of the conditions as listed in subsection 3. below.

3. All historic structures shall be preserved against decay and deterioration as set forth
by the International Property Maintenance Code adopted by the City of Cheyenne
Ordinance No. 15.24.010 to prevent being a neglected structure.

D. Monitoring.

1. Any Historical Register nominations or surveys, photographs, and county assessor
data shall serve as the baseline from which any decay and deterioration outlined in
subsection C.3. above is determined. For the purposes of this section, completed
plans not approved by the city shall be allowed to serve as a baseline.

E. Process for Submitting a Petition Alleging Neglected Structure.

1. The initial determination that there is a condition of neglect shall be made by the
Director after an investigation that is initiated by a petition from any person who is
familiar with the subject property, which may include, but not be limited to, a city
employee, the Cheyenne Historic Preservation Board, or any citizen.

2. Filing of Petition.

a. Petitions charging that a structure is a neglected structure, shall be filed with
the Director. The city may charge a reasonable fee to cover costs associated
with investigation, processing, and research regarding the petition. Fees shall
be set administratively by the city and shall be made available to the public.

b. The Cheyenne Historic Preservation Board (CHPB) may file a petition on its
own initiative. Petitions by the CHPB shall be exempt from fees.

c. Any official, commission, or department of the city, any state agency, or any
local or state historical, preservation, or business association may request in
writing to the CHPB that it make a preliminary investigation of a structure to
determine whether a basis exists for a determination of neglected structure.

the following:

regardless of whether that return represents the most profitable return
possible;

b. The qualifying building cannot be adapted for any other use, whether by the
current owner or by a purchaser, which would result in a reasonable return;
and qualifying building and preserving it have failed;

c. Diligent efforts to find a purchaser interested in acquiring the neglected
structure.
