RECORD OF PROCEEDINGS FOR THE GOVERNING BODY
OF THE CITY OF CHEYENNE
Office of City Clerk
January 28, 2013
The Governing Body of the City of Cheyenne convened in a special meeting on this date
beginning at 4:30 p.m. in Council Chambers for the purpose of holding a public hearing.  Present
were:  MAYOR: Richard L. Kaysen; COUNCIL MEMBERS - Mr. Brown, Ms. Broyles, Mr. Cook,
Mr. Luna, Dr. Rinne (arriving at 4:59 p.m.), Mr. Shanor, Mr. Valdez and Ms. Williams.   Absent:
Mr. Allen.  Also present were: Carol Intlekofer, City Clerk, and Dan White, City Attorney.  The
pledge of allegiance was recited.
Mayor Kaysen opened a public hearing to receive public comment and information on
violations of City ordinances and state laws pertaining to the sale of alcoholic beverages, which
violations have occurred at Mingles, 1618 Stillwater Avenue, Cheyenne, Wyoming, operated by
Stillwater, Inc., which holds City Retail Liquor License No. 39.  Officer Steve Niesen, alcohol
enforcement officer, Cheyenne Police Department, reviewed elements of the department’s alcohol
compliance checks program involving liquor licensees; City ordinance provisions relating to
violations by licensees resulting in convictions within a 12 month period, and advised failed
compliance checks conducted at Mingles bar in 2012 resulted in three convictions involving Mingles
bar staff within a one-year time frame.  He reported the failed compliance checks, resulting in the
issuance of citations and subsequent convictions, related to alcohol service to underage individuals.
Officer Niesen reviewed communications and discussions held with Owen Groshart, manager of
Mingles, after the first two citations were issued to offer assistance and suggestions on what could
be done to remedy the situation, noting that the third citation/conviction occurred after discussions
with Mr. Groshart.  Upon inquiry, Officer Niesen confirmed the dates of convictions (i.e. June 22,
2012; June 26, 2012, October 5, 2012) through municipal court involving the violations, and that the
compliance check failures occurred at the bar’s package store as well as the adjacent retail dispensing
room portion of the building.  Tanvir Dhami, member of the CLICK (traffic safety) program at
Cheyenne’s South High School, explained the CLICK program is dedicated to such issues as youth
traffic safety; expressed concerns with the violations incurred by Mingles relative to underage
drinking/driving, and stated that business operators need to be responsible and held accountable for
their actions if they do not abide by the law.  Lisa Ammons, Wyoming Prevention Management
Organization - Laramie County, provided a “snapshot” of data on juvenile liquor violations, and
reviewed issues surrounding underage drinking and drivers who are under the influence.  Anthony
Reyes, attorney representing Stillwater, Inc., clarified Stillwater, Inc. is not taking the convictions
lightly and are willing to do whatever is needed to remedy compliance issues.  He advised of
Mingles’ action involving the employees affiliated with the violations, and explained employee TIPS
(Training for Intervention Procedures (a recognized alcohol server training program)) training that
is provided, noting that Mingles is willing to provide additional training and is open to
recommendations that may be offered.  Upon inquiry, Mr. Reyes provided information on employer
records that are retained for staff who are TIPS trained, and Owen Groshart, manager of Mingles,
stated Mingles currently has 12 employees and discussed high employee turnover issues; staff duties;
