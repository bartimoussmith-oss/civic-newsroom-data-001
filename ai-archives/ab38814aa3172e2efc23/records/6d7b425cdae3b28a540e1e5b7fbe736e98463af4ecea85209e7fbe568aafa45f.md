SUMMARY:
Wyoming State Statutes require that prior to annexing territory to the City certain steps must be
followed, including the governing body conducting a public hearing and finding that the territory
is eligible for annexation. The Statutes list six criteria that must be met for the territory to be
eligible for annexation. As described in detail later in this report, the proposed development does
not meet these criteria.
The main concern with the annexation is that it is a piecemeal annexation that excludes adjacent
property under the same ownership for no apparent reason. The adjacent land is proposed to
serve as stormwater detention for a portion of the proposed development on the subject property.
The property owner submitted concurrently with the annexation a preliminary plat application
and an application to change the zoning from A-1 Agricultural and Rural Residential (County) to
MR-2 Medium-density Residential-Developing. These applications are in process, a
recommendation having been tabled by Planning Commission at its January 7, 2013 meeting due
to the numerous outstanding issues relating to proposed plat.
A brief overview of the concerns identified in the plat review and zone change process is
provided here in summary:
1. The application does not reflect extension of the street connecting Converse Avenue to
Ogden Road. Half of the street has already been platted by the City with the platting of
the parcel to the north. Extending existing platted streets is required (UDC § 4.3.3.f). The
street is also referenced in the transportation master plan and critical for the provision of
a four-way intersection onto Converse Avenue.
2. The application claims an existing detention pond as an exception to the open space
requirement, but the stormwater detention pond in question does not have sufficient
capacity and design standard to serve the proposed development. The open space
provided in the proposal does not appear to meet the open space requirements (UDC §
4.4).
3. Stormwater run-off for part of the proposed development would be addressed with a
detention pond that is outside the City's jurisdiction.
DESCRIPTION OF SITE AND SURROUNDINGS:
The owner of this 8.84-acre area east of and adjacent to Converse Avenue, north of Ogden Road,
Mr. Frank Cole of United Western Corporation, has petitioned the City to annex the land in order
to develop a 28-lot single-family subdivision. Adjacent to the subject property but not included
in the annexation or plat is a 3.35-acre parcel under the same ownership where a detention pond
has been developed. North of that parcel is a 21.32-acre parcel owned by Mr. Cole along with
members of his immediate family. To the east of it, north of Anderson Elementary are two
parcels owned by Freedom Land Limited Partnership, a partnership of Cole family members.
These are the only unincorporated parcels in this area east of Converse.
The proposed plat contains 28 buildable lots. The plat proposes to utilize a city maintained
stormwater facility south of the site and the stormwater facility east and north of the site recently
developed in the County mentioned above. The proposal terminates Apache Street and Pattison
Avenue into a cul-de-sac and proposes no access onto Converse Avenue.

Rather than annexing all of the property in this area and taking a comprehensive approach to

certain conditions in code.
TRANSPORTATION: The Master Transportation Plan establishes a network of collector and
arterial roads and a corresponding traffic model is also developed to plan for a future
transportation system that can be effectively maintained by the City. The transportation and land-
use plans also establish reasonable expectations for urban land-uses and future transportation
improvements by surrounding landowners.
Policy 4.3.d of PlanCheyenne states, in part: "New neighborhoods should contain street systems
that encourage internal pedestrian, bike, and auto circulation." To this end the Cheyenne Unified
Development Code (UDC) requires that "All new streets shall align with any existing or
proposed streets on adjacent property, and shall continue and extend arterial, collector, and local
streets within the proposed subdivision externally to the parcel boundary..." (UDC § 4.3.3.f)
The vacant property to the north has already dedicated half of the street width for a collector
connection in anticipation of this proposal submitting the same. The omission of any right-of-
way along the north side of the proposed Bluffs 9 th Filing is a significant deviation from the
transportation master plan.
