In review of eligible costs, staff looks for consistency with Wyo. Stat. § 15-9-113 and the Wyoming
Constitution (Specifically, Article 16, Section 6 (a) (i) and Article 3, Section 36). The biggest concern is
regarding how the application relates to the State Constitution. Relevant sections of the State
Constitution are as follows:
•
Article 16, Section 6, Loan of credit; donations prohibited; works of internal improvement.
“Neither the state nor any county, city, township, town, school district, or any other political
subdivision, shall . . . loan or give its credit or make donations to or in aid of any individual,
association or corporation, except for necessary support of the poor . . .”
•
Article 3, Section 36, Prohibited appropriations. “No appropriation shall be made for charitable,
industrial, educational or benevolent purposes to any person, corporation or community not
under the absolute control of the state, nor to any denominational or sectarian institution or
association.”

The above-mentioned sections of the State Constitution prohibit a political subdivision from loaning or
gifting funding to a private individual, corporation, or similar entity unless they serve a public purpose or
provide necessary support of the poor. Cheyenne has been careful with the programs within the Urban
Renewal Statutes (Urban Renewal and Downtown Development Authority) to ensure that all funds
passing through the programs have a public purpose. Staff has taken the stance that use of public funds
is appropriate for improvements within the right-of-way, and improvements on private property that
have a direct relationship to the public realm such as publicly accessible plazas/arcades and façade
improvements, or areas of private property publicly controlled by easement or other instrument.

Staff believes that most of the costs proposed are eligible and will meet the intent of Wyoming’s urban
renewal statutes and the State Constitution upon further clarification; however, staff expresses concern
with assisting with land acquisition and improvements that could be considered not of public benefit.

Leveraged Return Metrics
EPS conducted an analysis of the leveraged returns for the project to assess the impact
of the loan terms on its financial performance. Leveraged analysis examines a project’s
financial outcomes after the Developer has fulfilled all financial obligations, including debt
service. The key assumptions used in this analysis are outlined below:
•
Escalation Rate: Both revenues and expenditures are projected to grow at an
annual rate of 2.0 percent.
•
Capitalization Rate (Cap Rate): The terminal cap rate, or the rate applied upon the
sale of the property, is estimated at 6.25 percent for each project.
•
Disposition Year: It is assumed that each project will be sold in year 10. While the
Developer may opt to hold the property for a longer period, using a 10-year
disposition horizon provides a standard framework for evaluating the project’s
