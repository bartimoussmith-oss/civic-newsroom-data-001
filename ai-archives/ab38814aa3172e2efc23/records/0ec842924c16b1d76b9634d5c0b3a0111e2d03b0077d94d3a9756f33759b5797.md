RECORD OF PROCEEDINGS FOR THE GOVERNING BODY
OF THE CITY OF CHEYENNE
Office of City Clerk
August 28, 2017
The Governing Body of the City of Cheyenne met in regular session on this date beginning
at 6:00 p.m. in Council Chambers.  Present were:  MAYOR – Marian J. Orr; COUNCIL MEMBERS
– Rocky Case, Bryan Cook, Richard Johnson, Pete Laybourn, Mike Luna, Scott Roybal, Dr. Mark
Rinne (arriving at 6:03 p.m.), Dicky Shanor and Jeff White.  Also present: Carol Intlekofer, City
Clerk, and Sylvia Hackl, City Attorney.  The pledge of allegiance was recited.
Consent Agenda.  (All agenda items listed with the designation of [CA] are considered to be
routine items by the governing body and will be enacted by one motion. There will be no separate
discussion on these items unless a member of the governing body so requests and support by two
other members is received.  Any item removed from the Consent Agenda will be considered in its
normal sequence on the agenda.)  Upon request by Mr. Johnson, and pursuant to support received
by Mr. Cook and Mr. White, Agenda Item #22 was removed from the Consent Agenda.  Mr. Shanor
recused himself from voting participation on the item due to an affiliation with the State Land and
Investment Board (Consent Agenda Item #18), and vacated Council Chambers.  Mr. Luna moved
to approve remaining Consent Agenda items, seconded by Mr. Johnson.  Motion carried.  Voting
“yes” -- all members of the governing body present in Council Chambers.  Following announcement
of the vote, Mr. Shanor returned to Council Chambers.
[CA]
Minutes from Regular Meeting of the Governing Body on August 14, 2017.  Motion (per
Consent Agenda) carried.
Mr. Johnson moved to suspend the rules to change the order of the agenda by moving
Agenda Item #22 (Resolution proclaiming “Purple Heart Day” and the City as a Purple Heart City)
to be acted upon prior to Agenda Item #7 (Vouchers), seconded by Mr. Roybal.  Motion carried.
Voting “yes” -- all members of the governing body.
RESOLUTION – Proclaiming ‘Purple Heart Day’ and the City of Cheyenne, Wyoming, to
be a Purple Heart City.  Speaking in support of the resolution were:  Brian Olson, Commander, local
chapter of the Military Order of the Purple Heart; Retired Lt. Col. Barry Gostas; Larry Barttelbort,
Director, Wyoming Veterans Commission; Ted Gostas; Floyd Watson, Save American Veterans;
Major General Luke Reiner, Wyoming Military Department; Lisa Olson Snyder and Peter Howard.
Topics discussed included: History of the Purple Heart medal; recipient qualifications; recognition
of the City as the first city in Wyoming to be designated as a Purple Heart City; importance of the
City’s military support in Cheyenne, and consideration of veterans for positions of leadership and
service on boards and committees.  Ted Gostas commented on his military experience, including
his time as a POW (prisoner of war) which resulted in his receiving two Purple Heart medals.  Mr.
Luna moved to adopt, seconded by Dr. Rinne.  Discussion included expression of appreciation to
those serving in the military and particularly to Mr. (Ted) Gostas for his leadership and courage.
It was noted that community events will be planned to celebrate Purple Heart Day in 2018.  Motion
carried.  Voting “yes” -- all members of the governing body. (#5872)

Announcement was made that due to the Labor Day holiday on Monday, September 4, 2017,
regularly scheduled City Council Committee meetings will instead be held in the Municipal
Building, Room 122, 2101 O’Neil Avenue, Cheyenne, Wyoming, as follows: Finance Committee
will meet Tuesday, September 5, 2017 at 11:00 a.m. and Public Services Committee will meet
Wednesday, September 6, 2017 at Noon.
Under other business, Jeff Dierksen discussed traffic issues on Pinto Lane; referenced a
petition he filed last fall regarding mitigation of traffic conditions in the area, and requested an
update on whether WYDOT had been contacted regarding interpretation by previous City
engineering staff of the City’s authority to change the speed limit to 25 m.p.h. in residential
neighborhoods.  Mr. Dierksen voiced concerns that conditions have deteriorated for years due to
development in the area increasing the traffic volume and speeding by drivers accessing the area.
Upon inquiry, Jim Voeller, advised he was unaware if WYDOT had been contacted; stated the City
has done traffic studies in the neighborhood with law enforcement involvement, and noted the
position of traffic engineer is no longer under the Engineering Department.  Discussion included:
Potential options to reduce speeding on Pinto Lane; street classification, history of development in
the area affecting traffic conditions, and state law and jurisdictional issues concerning changes to
speed limits in neighborhoods.
There being no further business to come before the governing body, the meeting was
officially adjourned at 7:03 p.m.
