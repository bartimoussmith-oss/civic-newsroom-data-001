RECOMMENDED MOTION:

Move to recommend the Governing Body approve text amendments to the Unified Development Code
to create a common landscaping process and a definition of development footprint as outlined in this
staff report, noting the request meets the review criteria of UDC 2.4.1.d.

BACKGROUND:

The City’s Unified Development Code (UDC) was adopted by the Governing Body on January 23, 2012, by
Ordinance Number 3943 with an effective date of April 30, 2012. The Unified Development Code is the
regulatory document carrying out the vision of Plan Cheyenne and is reviewed annually pursuant to UDC
1.1.7 [Annual Review Requirement] to determine if any particular standard or regulation is timely,
relevant, and adequately addresses current market conditions. In addition to the annual review, the
Planning and Development Director and City Engineer are authorized under UDC 2.4.1.b.3 to bring
forward text amendments on their own initiative.

The intent of this amendment is to create flexibility in landscaping options to allow an administrative
option for a common landscape plan for Light Industrial (LI) and Heavy Industrial (HI) zoning district
developments that are over 100-acres.   The goal of the common landscape plan is to create overall
plans that would contribute to uniformity of a development’s collective appearance, place landscaping
in areas that are more beneficial to the public, and promote water conservation.

DISCUSSION:

City staff has been working together with Parks and Greenway Planning and the City Forester to allow
for alternative landscape requirements in areas where strict adherence to the landscape ordinance may
not be in the best interest of the community.  Specifically, issues with our current landscape code have

landscaping standards large lots are required to calculate landscape requirements on the entirety of the

Summarized below are highlights of the proposed amendment.

•
A new definition, “Development Footprint,” is proposed to allow developments to utilize
their disturbed area for the purpose of calculating internal landscaping points and tree
requirements, instead of the entirety of the lot.
•
The Common Landscape Plan will only apply to developments comprised of 100-acres or
more that are zoned Light Industrial (LI) and Heavy Industrial (HI) zoning district.
•
Common Landscape Plans may be initiated by all landowners or an organization authorized
to act on their behalf, like a property owners’ association. Approved conceptual plans are
recorded with County Real Estate Records on the individual properties.
•
Common Landscape Plans are required to be prepared by a licensed landscape architect.
Plans may estimate future development footprints and revise them as necessary.
Subsequent plans for an individual site may be prepared by a design professional, but are
required to comply with the approved concept plan.
•
The internal landscape point calculations are based on the development footprint, instead
of the entire lot area.  The calculation is based on a 90% impervious coverage of the
development footprint.  Current LI levels are 85% of the entire area, and HI are 90%.
•
A Common Landscape Plan allows for transfer of landscaping elements to other areas of the
plan. The process encourages transferred elements to be located along public rights-of-way
or parking areas. Transfers are required to be identified in the plan.
•
The street tree standards of UDC 6.3.6 are modified to require street trees at the rate of one
tree per 60 linear feet of street frontage instead of one per 40 feet. The street trees, as well
as any transferred landscaping points may be clustered and are required to be located
within 75’ of the property line adjacent to the right-of-way to allow for more creative design
that may better harmonize with the surrounding area.
•
The ability to seek administrative relief of tree planting requirements is allowed. This could
include up to a 20% reduction of the required internal or street trees. Approval is based on
specific site conditions and to ensure that the reduction does not negatively affect the
development’s collective appearance. If a tree reduction were to be approved, the applicant
would be required to pay a cash in-lieu fee equal to the cost of the tree and installation.  In-
lieu fees would be deposited into a specific fund for use in purchasing trees for the Greater
Cheyenne Greenway and other City lands. The in-lieu fee is presently calculated annually by
the City Forester for similar relief of street tree requirements.
•
Specific review criteria are incorporated to aide in the administrative decision-making
process.  Key components of the review criteria include demonstration that adequate
infrastructure exists to serve the plan area, the proposed plan does not put an undue
burden on the City or other public utilities, and that an entity responsible for ensuring
maintenance, such as a property owners’ association, has been identified.
•
Provisions for plan modification and plan abandonment administratively are included.
