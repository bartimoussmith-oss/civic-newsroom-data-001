WHEREAS, the City of Cheyenne (“City”) requests assistance in financing the movement
of three railroad vehicles located in the Union Pacific Railyard and Lions Park to City owned
property on 15th Street to fully renovate them for display as historic artifacts or use as space for
retail, dining, or small business use.

WHEREAS, Cheyenne approved a new Tourism Master Plan that includes the
development of a new railroad attraction as one of its top goals and this project will be a catalyst
for future development along 15th Street; and

WHEREAS, the City is applying for an Economic Development Administration Grant in
partnership with Visit Cheyenne for the movement and renovation of three railcars; and

WHEREAS, the total estimated cost for this project is $773,000.00

WHEREAS, a grant in the amount of eighty percent (80%) of that cost, or $618,400, is
being requested from the Economic Development Administration; and

WHEREAS, the required match, or $154,600 will be paid from the City of Cheyenne’s 6th
Penny Grant Match account.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE GOVERNING BODY OF THE
CITY OF CHEYENNE, WYOMING, that

1) the Governing Body of the City of Cheyenne, Wyoming supports an EDA American
Rescue Plan Travel, Tourism, and Outdoor Recreation grant application for 15th Street
Railcar Renovation Project;

2) the Mayor of the City of Cheyenne or other City official designated by the Mayor, is
authorized to sign an application for a grant for the project; and

3) that the Mayor of the City of Cheyenne is hereby designated as the authorized
representative of the City to act on behalf of the Governing Body on all matters relating to this
grant application, and in the event grant funds are awarded to the City of Cheyenne for this project,
the Mayor is authorized to execute the grant agreement and associated documents.

B. Project Information

B.1. Provide a geographic definition of the region to be served by the investment (project), including
the specific geographic location of the project within the region.

The City of Cheyenne (City), with a population of 64,822, is located at the northern end of the Rocky
Mountain Front Range Urban Corridor in Laramie County, Wyoming. The county covers 2,688 square
miles in the Southeast Region of the state and has a total population of 100,512 (2020).  It is the crossroads
of two major interstate highways and two major railroads, hosting an estimated 1.1 million overnight
visitors in 2019. 15th Street is located in Census Tract #56021000700.

Cheyenne is home to two SBA Opportunity Zones located south and northeast of downtown which seek to
encourage investment in economically-disadvantaged and distressed areas. In 2021, the City created the
Urban Renewal Authority as a state-enabled economic development tool to identify deteriorating or
underdeveloped blighted areas in the community to focus revitalization efforts and funding mechanisms.
Two downtown areas within our project zone have been designated as “blighted” and appropriate for
urban renewal actions as describe in the State of Wyoming Urban Renewal Code (Wyoming Statute 15-9-
103).  A portion of the 15th Street Railroad Attraction is located in the Reed Avenue Rail Corridor blighted
area and the Hynds/Hole blighted area is only one block away from the Attraction in the lower downtown
district, both of which have been included as significant project goals in the Tourism Master Plan -
Downtown Experience and Development recommendations. The Cheyenne Railcar Renovation Project is
a catalyst for the economic vision and revitalization efforts in these blighted areas and the greater
downtown district.

B.3.b. Describe the economic conditions of your region. Define the economic development needs to
be addressed by the proposed EDA investment and explain how the proposed investment will
address that need.

The COVID-19 crisis has created economic hardships throughout the State of Wyoming; Cheyenne and
Laramie County in Southeast WY are no exception. As is widely known, Wyoming’s economy lacks
diversification and is overly dependent upon the energy sector. The 12018 WY ENDOW PLAN states this
fact clearly in its name - Economically Needed Diversity Options for Wyoming – and in its preamble:
 We must evolve beyond the boom-bust cycle of our economy, wherein 70% of state revenue comes from
the energy and mineral industry. We must facilitate diverse, broad, and lasting growth so that the state can
prosper no matter the economic climate or status of commodity prices.
The COVID crisis continued this decline in energy sector revenue and employment and forced the state to
pivot and actively attack its job losses and lack of economic diversification.  Significant fluctuations have
destabilized this regular source of state income, creating a level of uncertainty for the future. Areas in the
lower downtown area of Cheyenne, while ripe for redevelopment, remain depressed despite some forward
progress.  Economic activity is minimal on 15th Street where five of the eight storefronts along the critical
two blocks of the attraction site are vacant and have been for years.
Wyoming’s 2018 ENDOW Plan named Tourism and Recreation as one of the top economic engines to
create economic opportunity, job growth, and diversification. According to Visit Cheyenne, if the travel
industry were a single business, it would rank as Laramie County’s third largest employer behind Frances
E. Warren Air Force Base and The State of Wyoming.
Tourism experienced the sharpest decline in jobs among the state’s economic sectors as a result of the
COVID-19 pandemic. International tourism has come to a standstill, and stay-at-home orders limited
travel and spending significantly. Wyoming’s leisure and hospitality industry suffered a 16.4% Gross
Domestic Product decline in 2020.  Overall, Wyoming’s GDP dropped by 8.3% in 2020, ranking WY as
one of the states with the largest GDP declines: Hawaii’s - 9.7 %, Alaska -8.7%, Wyoming -8.3%,
Oklahoma -7.7%, Louisiana -7.5%. (Source: Economic Analysis Division of the Wyoming Department of
Administration and Information)

In 2019, the community gathered to complete a Downtown Economic Assessment which was compiled
into the 2Forward Greater Cheyenne Community & Economic Development Strategy.  The
community believes that “a vibrant downtown offers residents and families of all ages and backgrounds a
gathering place and an economic and social center of gravity – a destination for shops, restaurants, and
other businesses, and a place where people can socialize.  Such a center could serve as a quality of life
amenity for the entire community – not only to attract new residents, but to also retain existing residents
and give them an alternative to travelling to Fort Collins and other Colorado communities to have fun.”
The goal is to create a downtown experience built on the talents and entrepreneurial spirit of Cheyenne’s
residents. Cheyenne offers unique opportunities within a favorable tax and regulatory climate and low cost
of doing business.  This project will introduce new business opportunities for women or people of color to
set up their own businesses and create a lasting footprint on 15th Street.

B.4.  Applicant’s capability

The City of Cheyenne has the capacity to manage a project of this nature with systems, policies, and
procedures in place to ensure compliance and fiscal integrity of the project. The City uses a cross
departmental and highly skilled team approach and follows strict adherence to the policies and procedures
as approved by the City of Cheyenne Governing Body to include: the City of Cheyenne Accounting
