OF TITLE 9, PUBLIC PEACE AND WELFARE, OF THE CODE OF THE CITY OF
CHEYENNE PERTAINING TO THE FEE FOR A FIREARM DEALER’S BUSINESS.” John
Fafoutakis questioned the amount of the current license fee and spoke in opposition to municipal
government regulating sales of firearms, which are licensed at the federal level.  Upon inquiry,
Carol Intlekofer, City Clerk, advised the current license fee is $85 annually and that Wyoming
Statute, Title 15 provides for the City to license and regulate businesses.  City Attorney Dan White
confirmed Wyoming Statute §15-1-103 provides authority for municipalities to license, tax and
regulate any business whatsoever conducted within the city limits. Bob Fecht, Cheyenne Police
Chief, clarified that federal regulation is through Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms rules, which states
that applicants for a federal license to sell firearms must go through an approval process through the
Police Dept.  Duncan Philp, Carpenter, Wyoming, Director of Wyoming Tyranny Response Team,
spoke in opposition to a firearm dealer’s business license being required at the local level in
addition to a federal license.  Mayor Kaysen clarified the ordinances involving proposed fee
increases are to help offset licensing/permitting administrative costs, and that some fees have not
been adjusted since 1997 or 2006.  Mr. Valdez moved to approve on second reading, seconded by
Ms. Broyles.  Motion carried.  Voting “yes” – all members of the governing body present.

“A RESOLUTION AUTHORIZING THE MAYOR TO EXECUTE A LETTER OF
INTENT WITH FIELD HOUSE DEVELOPMENT GROUP, LLC, FOR PURPOSES OF THE
DEVELOPMENT OF AN INDOOR SPORTS COMPLEX IN THE CITY OF CHEYENNE.”
Offering public comments were John Fafoutakis, Mark Shubert and Eric Miller, who spoke in
opposition to the resolution and urged caution in proceeding forward with a field house
development.  Topics presented included: Field house facility vs. recreation center and public
sentiment expressed through a ballot election question on the latter; future costs and concerns,
including use of city-owned property; field house project process proposed by the developer;
utilization of current facilities and rental revenues; project financing; current economic conditions,
and prioritizing of City projects.  Dave Zyzda, Director of Leasing, Lake Barrington (IL) Field
House, Field House Development Group representative, explained the Group’s letter of intent will
provide them the opportunity to visit with local sports teams/groups to determine whether there is
support through commitment of facility rental use/fees to consider development of an indoor
sports complex.  He stated if there is enough interest expressed then the Group may proceed with
discussions with the City with regard to a site involving donated land and land lease arrangements.
He provided information on potential formation of a non-profit charitable organization; facility
funding mechanism and debt payments; facility management time frame by the Group; property

(ground) lease, and eventual ownership of the facility by the City.  Dirk Miller, Cheyenne Soccer
Association Board of Directors member, spoke in support of the resolution and clarified there

rent facilities in Colorado for use.  Comments followed on utilization of City soccer fields and

Collins, CO area, where competitive soccer programs lease the facilities to enable year round play
(i.e. indoor turf), explained team needs and volumes and the unavailability of facilities locally
through the community college and schools affecting player use.  Major Dorr spoke in support of
the letter of intent and giving youth a chance to compete and prove themselves.  During public
comments, points of order were raised with Mayor Kaysen requesting speakers to remain on topic
with their comments, maintain respectfulness, and to offer comments, not enter into debate, with
other speakers.  Upon inquiry, Mayor Kaysen clarified the letter of intent authorizes the Group to
visit with sports teams and if, based on their findings, the Group determines a facility is feasible
for the community, then discussions will be held involving a definitive development agreement.
He stated any final agreement proposed will come before the governing body for review and
approval.  Mr. Valdez reported there was no recommendation from Finance Committee on the
item.  Mr. Collins moved to adopt, seconded by Dr. Rinne.  Comments followed and included the
following topics: Shortage of space for sports leagues to practice; providing athletes with a facility
for year round use to build and enhance athletic skills and maintaining a competitive training
environment for players; aspects of partnering with the private sector to build a community field
house; local community college and school policies affecting facility rental availability; e-mails
received by Council members in support of the letter of intent; possible land lease with the City
for a nominal amount, and non-commitment on the part of the City at this time.  During comments
and inquiries, Mr. Zyzda provided information concerning potential ground lease/sales options
and financial bonding, and confirmed the letter of intent involves no obligation by the City.  Upon
inquiry regarding potential parcels of City land that could be utilized for a facility, Parks and
Recreation Director Rick Parish stated parcels of land would need to be reviewed for
requirements, options and availability if it is decided the feasibility study warrants moving
forward. Concerns were expressed with budgetary issues, other ongoing city projects, and contact
with citizens who have expressed a desire for a recreation center in the future instead of a field
house.  Motion carried.  Voting “yes” – all members of the governing body present with the
exception of Ms. Broyles voting “no”. (#5135)
