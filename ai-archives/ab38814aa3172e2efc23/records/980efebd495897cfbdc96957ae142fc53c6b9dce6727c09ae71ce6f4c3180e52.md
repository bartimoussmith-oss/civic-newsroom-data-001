approval. Townhomes would require plot plan review

PLANCHEYENNE: The application is in accordance with the development policies and physical patterns

The application is in accordance with the development policies and physical patterns identified in the
PlanCheyenne. In general it will provide a mix of housing choices (ref. Foundation 1, PlanCheyenne).
The project, again by way of the Greenway, provides opportunities to support bicycle and pedestrian
mobility (ref. Foundation 4, PlanCheyenne).  The project also promotes elements of fiscally responsible
development, such as infill development (ref. Foundation 7, PlanCheyenne).

This proposal contributes to the mix of housing types and appropriately locates higher densities near
activity centers with the commercial zoned property to the north which are zoned to accommodate
businesses that serve the neighborhood.  This site also is within walking distance of a Greenway
Connector.

PLANNING. The application reflects sound planning and urban design principles with respect to
existing and potential future development in the vicinity, including consideration of current and future
streets, open spaces, blocks and lots, and public utilities or facilities.

This infill proposal is a replat of a single vacant lot within a previously platted and completely built-out
high-density residential block.  The MUB district is intended for higher intensity development to help
assure compatibility between adjacent uses, such as between the residential and the commercial uses
adjacent to this site. The current proposal will improve the site and its relationship to the public street
and water and sewer exist immediately adjacent to the site.

Community Facility Fees (Park Fees)
Land Acquisition and Infrastructure Fees in the amount of $250 (minimum fee) will be due before
signatures are affixed to the Final Plat map.  An additional Residential Enhancement Fee of $400/ unit
and Commercial Enhancement Fee of $250 will be due at the time of building permit application.

Transportation Networks and Street Designs
This is a replat of a single lot within an existing block.  There is no change in the surrounding street
network.  The block that this replat lies within is under the maximum block perimeter requirements and
the surrounding street network is already established, providing a clear picture of the transportation
network for this subdivision.  This block is located along Bluegrass Circle, classified as a collector
street, and Edgewater Ave., classified as a local street. While the streets have been constructed, the
street-sections will be required to be built to current design standards and deviations from these
standards will require a subdivision standards waiver or adjustment (2.3.1).

Open Space Systems
The Greenway system is accessible within 800’ feet of the southeast corner of this plat. Generally,
given the context (mixed-use commercial) and zoning (MUB), required open space is 2% of the
building footprint for lots less than 2,500 sqft and 5% of the building footprint for lots greater than
2,500 sqft.  In this case, in addition to being exempt according to Section 4.4.2.b.1, UDC, this proposal
can be described as residential infill development less than 10 acres and exempt according to Section
4.4.2.b.3, UDC, as well.

PHASING: Any phasing proposed in the application is clearly indicated and demonstrates a logical and
well-planned development pattern. This may include consideration of amenities and public facilities of
the subdivision within each phase compared to the anticipated timing or the likelihood of future phases
being constructed as planned.

As the Note 3 on the Final Plat indicates that no phasing is proposed.  In general the expectation is the
all sidewalks and curb repairs (if needed) will be fully constructed.

STUDIES:  The impacts identified in specific studies or technical reports that were prepared or requested
to be prepared in association with the application, and whether the findings and recommendations of
these reports, or any modifications or conditions on approval of the application, can mitigate these
impacts.

A Transportation Worksheet was submitted pursuant to 3.1.1.c.1, UDC.  While no worksheet is
required for residential projects proposing 25 dwelling units or less, this project involves the potential
for access changes on Bluegrass Circle (a collector).  Thus, the worksheet was forwarded to the Traffic
Engineer for his consideration. After review, the worksheet was approved and signed.

As the project site is less than 20,000 sqft, an Abbreviated Drainage Study was submitted pursuant to
3.2.1.c.2, UDC, and forwarded to the Drainage Engineer for his consideration. His concerns have been
addressed.
