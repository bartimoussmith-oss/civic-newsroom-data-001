MEMORANDUM
TO:

The Honorable Mayor Patrick Collins and City of Cheyenne Council Members
FROM:

Robin Lockman, City Treasurer
DATE:

September 6, 2023
SUBJECT:
Re-Appropriation to be considered for approval

On September 11, 2023, the Governing Body will be referring a resolution to the Finance
Committee to re-appropriate funds for the Fiscal Year (FY) 2024 City of Cheyenne Budget per
W.S. § 16-4-112 and 16-4-113.  Below is more detailed information regarding this re-
appropriation request.

ALL FUNDS - ENCUMBRANCES AND ROLLOVER ACCOUNTS (RESERVES)
Each September my department prepares a large budget re-appropriation request that includes
carryover balances and encumbrances from the previous fiscal year for various City funds,
including the One Percent Optional Tax Fund, Specific Purpose Tax Fund, and others.
When the annual budget is approved each June, you may have noticed that numerous city funds
are not included.  This is because funds available to carry over are not known until after the fiscal
year ends.  Therefore, I have included carryover balances in this reappropriation request.
I am also including General Fund line items that will have unspent balances from FY 2023 to be
carried over to FY 2024.  This includes parks and recreation projects in the amount of $2,961,
Belvoir Ranch planning/development in the amount of $40,904, and county pocket annexation
funds in Planning and Development’s budget the amount of $169,351 for a total of $213,216.
Finally, I am including more detailed information on the purchase order (PO) encumbrances (see
attached report) from FY 2023 for a total of $606,918 in the General Fund and $7,474,005 in all
other city funds.  Encumbrances are simply purchase orders that were created for goods and
services ordered prior to, but not received until after, June 30, 2023.

GENERAL FUND – ADDITIONAL REVENUE AND RESERVES
In addition to the encumbrances and rollovers listed above, there are six General Fund requests
included for your consideration:

1. City Council cell phone.   A request was made to increase the telecommunications line by
$720 to pay for a city issued cell phone for a member of City Council.  This increase will
be paid for from reserves.

2. Janitorial and HVAC contract increases.  There are increases to City contracts for
janitorial services (contract #7538, #7539, #7540, and #7541) and HVAC maintenance
services (contract #7785) that were not known at the time of the FY 2024 budget
preparation.  This includes janitorial services conducted at the Municipal Building,
Municipal Court Building, Public Safety Center, and Youth Alternatives Building, as well
as HVAC maintenance services at the Aquatics Center, Depot Plaza, Kiwanis Community
House, Municipal Building, Municipal Court Building, Pioneer Park Building, Public
Safety Center, and Youth Activities Center.  The total General Fund increases for janitorial
services is $16,023 and for HVAC maintenance services is $22,327, both to be paid from
reserves.

3. Investment Advisor Fees.  An increase of $16,529 is proposed to pay for investment
advisor fees and costs to maintain the City’s investment custodial account. In the past, these
fees have been paid through offsetting investment revenue, but for better tracking and
transparency, we have separated them into an expenditure line item.  This is paid for by
increasing the investment interest revenue budget.

4. Attorney Fees for Property Litigation.  The City Attorney’s Office has entered into a
contract with Davis & Cannon LLP (contract #7780) for real property litigation
representation.  The attorney fees line item has increased $107,115 as a result and will be
paid for from reserves.

5. Additional Transfer to Civic Center and Ice & Events Center Request.  Jason Sanchez,
Community Recreation and Events (CRE) Director, is requesting an additional $350,000
General Fund transfer to the Civic Center, and an additional $119,270 transfer to the Ice &
Events Center, due to $714,884 in FY 2023 CRE Department General Fund budget
reversions.  Please see attached memorandum from Mr. Sanchez for further information.

6. Additional expenses related to moving donated rail cars.  The economic development
line item increased in the amount of $95,550 as the result of ground operations and crane
expenses for moving the four (4) rail cars donated by the Union Pacific Railroad for the
15th Street Rail project.

In addition to carrying over numerous existing grants, the City of Cheyenne has received the

MEMORANDUM
TO:

The Honorable Mayor Patrick Collins and City of Cheyenne Council Members
FROM:

Jason Sanchez, Community Recreation and Events Director
DATE:

September 1, 2023
SUBJECT:
Civic Center and Ice & Events Center Requests from General Fund Reserves

Last fiscal year, our team managed over 100 large-scale events within the Community Recreation
& Events Department. Nearly half of these events, including Fridays on the Plaza, Superday,
Culture X, Cheyenne Hispanic Festival, Brew Fest, Cheyenne Frontier Days (CFD) Pancake
Breakfast, and Cheyenne Day, were hosted outside of the Cheyenne Civic Center and were directly
managed or facilitated by Civic Center staff.
The Civic Center staff consists of five full-time employees who are critical to the operations and
success of these outside events due to their specialized skills in technical production, event
management, booking, marketing and advertising, ticketing, and safety. With this team, we have
experienced our most successful Fridays on the Plaza to date, built stronger partnerships with third-
party promoters and agents, cultivated deeper relationships with sponsors, and served as a critical
resource for local groups and community organizations in the surrounding area.
As we have continued to expand the caliber of events being brought to the downtown community,
we have garnered stronger support from businesses who recognize this as a catalyst for tourism,
economic growth, and overall vitality for the City of Cheyenne.
The Cheyenne Civic Center holds a special place in the hearts of our citizens, serving as a home
for a wide range of events, from performances by local artists to nationally renowned acts,
community events, conferences, training sessions, and so much more. Its diverse offerings
contribute significantly to the City's cultural fiber, vibrancy, and economic prosperity.
However, like any facility, the Civic Center requires ongoing investments to maintain its
infrastructure, attract top-tier talent, and provide the best experience for both performers and
audiences. It is my belief that redirecting funds toward the Civic Center is a strategic move that
will yield substantial returns in terms of community engagement, economic activity, and overall
quality of life for our residents.
