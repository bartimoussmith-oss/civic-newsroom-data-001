1.0INTRODUCTION
This Sanitary Landfill Environmental Monitoring Plan(SLEMP) has been prepared to address groundwater, leachate,
and methane monitoring requirements at the City of Cheyenne Sanitary Landfill, alsoknown as the Happy Jack
Landfill (Landfill). The plan has been prepared as part of the facility's 2017 lifetime permit application, primarily to
fulfill monitoring requirements contained within the Wyoming Department of Environmental Quality/Solid and
Hazardous Waste Division(WDEQ/SHWD) municipal solid waste landfill regulations. Additional monitoring
requirements have also been incorporated pursuant to the WD EQ November 25,2015Location Standards Variance
Approval for the expansion area.This SLEMP has been prepared in accordance with revised Chapter 2 of the
WDEQ/SHWDRules and Regulations dated January 3, 2017.
The primary objective of the environmental monitoring program at the Landfill is early identification of impacts to the
subsurface as aresult of landfill activities.Additionally, the hydro geologic and analytical data generated during routine
monitoring maybe used for assessment and/orremedial design purposes, if impacts are identified in subsurface soils or
groundwater beneath the landfill.
The remainder of the SLEMP is organized into the following sections:
Section 2.0-Groundwater and Leachate Monitoring Plan: provides a description for each of the aquifers beneath
the landfill leach ate monitoring points, different phases of monitoring, frequencyof sampling, aswellas luid
gauging and groundwater sampling procedures. This section also addresses the off-site water well monitoring
program for wells within a half mile of the expansion area boundary that are permitted or certificated for domestic
or stock use.
Section3.0-Groundwater Data Quality Assurance/Quality Control: presents the quality assurance objectives for
this project and the methodology for reviewing field and laboratory data.
Section 4.0-Groundwater Data Statistical Analyses: includes a description of the statistical methods used to
evaluate the groundwater analytical results from each monitoring event.
·Section 5.0Landfill Methane Monitoring Plan: describes the monitoring system, frequency, and procedures for
monitoring methane in land fil structures and in the subsurface.
：Section 6.0-Notification Requirements: outlines the notification requirements and follow up actions to
statistically significant increases in contaminant concentrations in groundwater, detections above groundwater
protection standards, and methane concentration measuring more than 25% or 100% of the lower explosive limit
(LEL).

A transducer study was then completed to shed more light on thelocal subsurface hydrology as a condition of the

2.2.6 LEACHATE SUMPS
There are five (5) sumps installed to monitor and evacuate leachate generated beneath the northern trenches at the
landfill. Figure 1 shows the locations of each of the leachate sumps installed at the landfill. Fluid levels in sumps
NT-1 and NT-2 are manually gauged semi-annually in concert with the routine sampling events to the nearest 0.1-foot

fluid levels and leachate pumping volumes read from the electronic display on a monthly basis. The pressure
transducers in Sumps NT-3 through NT-5 will be verified every two years. The pressure transducers will be verified to
the nearest 0.1-foot utilizing a water level indicator. If the measurement between the water level indicator and the
pressure transducer differ by greater than 0.3 foot, the transducer willbe re calibrated.The sumps are evacuated using
dedicated submersible pumps programmed to prevent more than 12-inches of leachate from accumulating above the
cell liner.Grab samples will be collected annually from the NT-5 leachate sump (if sufficient volume if present) and
analyzed for the Appendix B (VOC) and C constituent lists in order to quantify constituent concentrations detected in
the leachate (Table 3). Sump NT-5 was selected because it collects leachate from the most recently constructed cell
and will produce leachate for the longest future time period.In addition, sample collection can be accomplished easily
because of a sample port location on the discharge line and no confined space entry is required. Again, constituent
concentrations are primarily for information purposes to characterize the leachate quality and are not related to any
compliance criteria.As new landfill cells are constructed, the City proposes to sample one leachate collection area for
thenew cells for Appendix A and C constituents annually.

2.3.1FLUID LEVEL MONITORING
Upon arriving at a wellocation, sampling personnel will check the wellfor damage and remove the lock and well cap.
Any damage to the wellhead or casing, or any items requiring maintenance will be noted in the field sampling record
and reported to the project managerso that the necessary repairs canbe scheduled.Field personnel will wear new,
sterile gloves during all preparation and sampling activities.
Fluid levels within each water bearing zone will be measured within a 24-hour period during the two scheduled
sampling events (April and August).Fluid level measurements will be performed using a water level indicator accurate
