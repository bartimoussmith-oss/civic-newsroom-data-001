districts, however, on the south and east, the future land use plan’s optimal zone is CB
Community Business. Except in cases of a hard barrier (such as a railroad or interstate, the
future land use map should be viewed as using ‘fuzzy’ lines, that is, areas should gradually
transition from one future land use to another.

When the future land use map was created, many previously-developed areas were given future
land use designations based on current zoning or current ownership. Areas exempt from
property taxes were given the ‘public quasi-public’ future land use designation, even if they
were private entities. This resulted in HUD homes in the middle of neighborhoods being
designated ‘public’ despite being surrounded by the Urban Residential category. Churches also
received this designation, including the subject property. With the Connect 2045 comprehensive
plan update, any privately-owned ‘public’-designated parcel was assigned a new future land use
designation based on existing zoning. As the subject parcel was zoned residential, it was given
the ‘Urban Residential’ future land use designation.

The current use of the parcel is nonconforming (prohibited) in the LR zone and would require
commercial zoning by the current zoning code. Any expansion of the use would require a zone
change or conditional use approval.

Based on the policies and future land use map, CB zoning is appropriate for this location.

The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 1.

2) The application is compatible with nearby property, specifically considering existing zoning,
the Comprehensive Plan, and any other official plans and policies created under the guidance
of that plan for these areas; (UDC 2.2.1.d.2)

Surrounding properties are zoned CB, P, and LR. The LR zoneis described as low density
residential. The P zone allows a variety of public uses, which are all non-residential in nature..
The CB zone is to the east and south, and is the requested zone for this parcel. Generally, the
code encourages buffering between the CB and LR zones.

The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 2.

3) The suitability of the subject property for development according to the standards and uses of
the existing zoning district, compared to the suitability of the subject property for
development according to the standards and uses of the proposed zoning district (UDC
2.2.1.d.3)

The current use of the parcel is nonconforming (prohibited) in the LR zone and would require
commercial zoning by the current zoning code. Any expansion of the use would require a zone
change or conditional use approval.
The LR, as put forward in the UDC, is intended for larger-block, front-loaded, suburban style
development patterns.  CB zoning is intended for Retail, Employment, Service and Civic uses that
may serve a number of residential neighborhoods or other districts in meeting daily needs,
discretionary needs, or other business functions.  The current function of the property is better
suited to the CB zone, such as the other business to the east.

The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 3.

The zone change is occurring in a pre-existing neighborhood. No changes to the street network
are proposed. Accesses will not be changed with the zone change. Infrastructure (streets and
utilities) exist in the area.

The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 4.

5) The ability of the application to support the Intent and Applicability statements of the
proposed zoning district and achieve the Design Objectives of any specific standards. (UDC
2.2.1.d.5)

The CB District is intended for Retail, Employment, Service and Civic uses that may serve number
of residential neighborhoods or other districts in meeting daily needs, discretionary needs, or
other business functions.  The current function of the property is better suited to the CB zone,
such as the other business to the east. Due to the nature and users of CB-zoned property, the CB
zone has a much more flexible signage allowance. The impetus behind the request is to allow
the current site more signage options.

The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 5.

6) The ability of all potential future development to meet all applicable standards for
development in the proposed zoning district, including other development options if any
specific development proposed in association with the application is not executed after
approval. (UDC 2.2.1.d.6)

The CB zone allows a variety of commercial uses including retail, employment, service, and civic
uses. The applicant intends to install a new LED sign into the existing brick signage of the church.
Changing from LR zone to CB zone would enable this. Should the existing use cease and the
property need redevelopment, the options presented by the CB zone are more logical and
feasible than those of the LR zone.

The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 6.

7) The review and recommendations of other reviewing bodies. (UDC 2.2.1.d.7)

Review comments discussed how the proposal meets comprehensive plan principles. The parcel
should be this zone based on the physical form of the area to the north, east and southeast, and
the neighboring business.

The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 7.

8) Whether the application will harm the public health, safety, or general welfare. (UDC
2.2.1.d.8)

City staff is satisfied that this proposed zone change would not adversely affect the public
health, safety, or general welfare.
