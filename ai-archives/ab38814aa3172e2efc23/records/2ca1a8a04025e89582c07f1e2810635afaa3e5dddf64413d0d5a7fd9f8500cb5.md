Section 3.  That on September 3, 2024, the Planning Commission conducted a public
hearing, reviewed the proposed text amendment, and recommended approval of said text
amendment.

Section 4.  That Sub-section 1.4.3, Defined Terms, in Section 1.4 Interpretation, of Article
1, General Provisions is amended to create a new definition "Caretaker Unit. One or more living
quarters or lodging facilities located within a non-residential building for the use of employees of
the use. A Caretaker Unit shall not be considered a 'residential use' or 'residential unit' for the
purposes of this code but shall be considered part of the non-residential square footage for the
purposes of the code. The combined square footage of all Caretaker Units shall not comprise more
than ten (10) percent of the principal use's building square footage (should dwelling area exceed
this amount, the living area shall be determined to be a "Mixed-use Dwelling" and a residential
use for the purposes of this code). A Caretaker Unit is allowed as part of the approval of the
principal use without any more restrictive approval process than that warranted for the principal
use."

Section 5.  That Sub-section 5.1.2, Zoning Districts Established, in Section 5.1 General
Provisions, of Article 5, Zoning Regulations is amended to create a new row "BP | Business Park"

BACKGROUND:

The City’s Unified Development Code (UDC) was adopted by the Governing Body on January 23, 2012, by
Ordinance Number 3943 with an effective date of April 30, 2012.  The Unified Development Code is the
regulatory document carrying out the vision of PlanCheyenne and is reviewed annually pursuant to UDC
1.1.7 [Annual Review Requirement] to determine if any particular standard or regulation is timely,
relevant and adequately addresses current market conditions. In addition to the annual review, the
Planning and Development Director and City Engineer are authorized under UDC 2.4.1.b.3 to bring
forward text amendments on their own initiative.

The proposed text amendments are to create a new zone district “BP Business Park” in the UDC. The
impetus behind the proposed text amendment is to create a zone district which closely matches the
current County PUD for North Range Business Park. North Range Business Park is outside of City limits but
uses City water and sewer with an Outside User’s Agreement. The Outside User’s Agreement requires the
business park to annex once it becomes eligible (adjacent to City limits), and the park was made adjacent
to City limits by a separate annexation earlier in 2024.

The BP zone is a combination of light industrial and community business zoning, allowing a mix of
industrial, service, retail, and office uses. All but the most intense of non-residential uses are allowed. The
business parks located on the edges of the community are often located along regional transportation
routes. This location is attractive to regional-serving commercial uses, along with industrial uses. The
proposed zone district was created with input from Cheyenne Leads on what types of businesses are
locating in business parks.

There are areas of the City where parcels flip back and forth between commercial and industrial zoning
depending on the specific project proposed. There is even a recent example of this occurring on a zone
change request that came to planning commission in the last month. The BP zone could not only apply to
North Range Business Park, but also other existing business parks in the City.

With the creation of a new zone district, multiple sections of code referencing zone districts by name also
need updating. When the SS zone was established, not all of these sections were updated, so while these
sections are opened to include the BP zone, the SS zone is being added at the same time.

The scale and location of business parks warrants some special consideration for design standards. The
code amendment has BP adopt the design standards of 6.9 for non-industrial development, and gives
some special design standard considerations for parking, landscaping, and neighborhood identification
signage. A universally-applicable definition was added for ‘caretaker unit’ to give a path forward for a
small living quarters on the same site as a industrial or commercial facility.

amendments are as follows:
-
Amend 1.4.3 to create a definition for ‘caretaker unit’, allowing said units to exist on properties

forward for sites which desire to provide this type of accommodation
-
Amend 5.1.2 to include BP and SS on the ‘established zones’ list

REVIEW CRITERIA:

Review of a UDC text amendment shall be measured by the following three (3) criteria of Section 2.4.1.d:

1. COMPREHENSIVE PLAN:
“The amendment is in accordance with general goals and policies of the Comprehensive Plan, or
is necessary to address an emerging issue not anticipated in the Comprehensive Plan and is not
inconsistent with the plan.”

This proposed text amendment is in accordance with the goals and policies of the Comprehensive
Plan.

The Comprehensive Plan has a point designation entitled “Community/Regional Activity Center”.
This designation is often found at interstate interchanges and are expected to “serve Cheyenne
residents as well as residents of the surrounding region”. They are expected to “typically include
large-scale retail and employment uses, mixed-use development and residential”.

The Comprehensive Plan also has a future land use designation “Mixed-Use Employment
Campus”. This is anticipated to develop with “a variety of uses, with a primary focus on
employment designed in a business campus setting”. While PlanCheyenne does encourage these
areas to develop with a campus-like development (including plazas and pedestrian-friendly
amenities), the examples given of existing Cheyenne ‘business campuses’ are really more
‘business parks’:  Swan Ranch, North Range Business Park, and Cheyenne Business Parkway. None
of these Cheyenne business parks have a true campus-like atmosphere like those found at places
like the Denver Tech Center, the Anschutz Medical Campus in Aurora, or the Interlocken park in
Broomfield.

The City does have a zone which is intended to catch the Comprehensive Plan’s vision for “Mixed-
use Employment Campus”: the MUE zone. The current MUE Mixed-use Employment zone, as
found in the zoning code, contemplates a very specific type of campus-style development. The
type of development encouraged is small-scale industrial and commercial uses, with a significant
office presence. Higher-intensity versions of retail, service, or industry are prohibited. Most of the
City’s business parks are looking for higher-intensity uses, including specifically the higher-
intensity commercial uses anticipated at major interchanges and encouraged by the
comprehensive plan’s “Community/Regional Activity Center” designations at these interchanges.
MUE design standards for the current MUE zone are centered around a unified, campus-like
atmosphere, and is not as applicable to the disjointed, large-distance type of development often
found in current Cheyenne business parks. There is a place for campus-style zoning, so Staff did
not propose removing or replacing this zone district to accommodate the intent of the BP zoning.

The large swaths of “MUE” as designated in the Comprehensive Plan on the edges of the
community could have a campus-style development, but to expect all such areas to develop with
campus-style development is probably unrealistic. The BP zone is proposed to accommodate a
mix of commercial and industrial uses in a business-park-style development pattern instead of a
more compact business-campus-style development pattern.

quantities of dense residential development may not be as necessary when applied to the large
