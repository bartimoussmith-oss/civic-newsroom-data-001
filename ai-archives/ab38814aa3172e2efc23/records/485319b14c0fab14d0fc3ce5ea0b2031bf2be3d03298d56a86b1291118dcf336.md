Dear Mayor and Council Members,
Please accept this letter as an application on behalf of High West Energy Inc.for an electric
utility franchise from the City of Cheyenne.We believe this would be a good thing for Cheyenne
for several reasons:
We are a qualified and reputable provider of power.HWE is price competitive, offers
reliable service and hashigh customer satisfaction levels.We are focusedon meeting the
growing demands of the areas surrounding Cheyenne, and are investing $8.5 million in a new
substation, transmission lines and a new customer service center by Cheyenne.
We are a part owner of a Tri-State Generation & Transmission Company, which is a multi-
state power generator with its headquarters in Westminster, CO.Tri-State, in turn, is a part
owner of Basin Electric, which is also a multi-state power generator, and the owner-operator
of Laramie River Station in Wheatland and Dry Fork Station near Gillette.
HWE owns, operates and maintains more miles of electric line and covers a larger
geographical area, sometime over very rough terrain, than does CLFP. We are safety
accredited by our national organization and our insurance provider, and enjoy high
satisfaction from our membership.
Benefitsof the cooperative business model.A key difference between a co-opandan
investor-owned utility is our business model. We return our profits to our customers at the
end of the year. High West returned $275 in the form of a dividend, on average, to each of
our10,000 customers in November2011.
As the city grows, more people are choosing to live in High West's territory where we have
lines and substations established to serve our customers.We believe these individuals will
enjoy not only becoming customers of High West Energy, but also members of our
cooperative family, with the ability to share in these dividends and also have a real voice in
the management of the company through democratically elected board members from each
member district.

Mayor and City Council

High West Energy is not seeking to grow its service territory.Rather, as Cheyenne grows, the
city's boundaries havebeen moving into areas where our co-op has been providing electricity for
75 years. Unfortunately, our company cannot continue to serve those businesses and residents
without thecity's approval.
All three Ward II1 Council Members -Jim Brown, Georgia Broyles and Don Pierson, are
sponsoring this ordinance.HWE currently provides service to 14 accounts in our certificated
area in Ward IIl.Almost all of these accounts wish to remain customer-owners of High West
Energy.
The key provisions of our proposed franchise are:
HWE's certificated area.We are not interested in taking customers or service territory from
CLFP.We believe both utilities can thrive in the greater Cheyenne community and provide
quality service to their respective customers.
2.We will pay a franchise fee of 3% of electric sales, but will establish a floor of at least
$10,000 per year.
3.We are committed to make financial contributions to community-based organizations in
Cheyenne equal to $2.50 per HWE customer. This equates to approximately $25,000
annually since we have 10,000 total accounts. The $2.50 corresponds to the level of
contributions made by CLFP divided by the number of gas and electric accounts they serve.
In other words, we are committed to the same level as CLFP on a per customer basis for the
term of the franchise.
Thanks for your consideration.
