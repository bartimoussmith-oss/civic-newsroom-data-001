RESOLUTION NO. __________

ENTITLED: "A RESOLUTION REQUIRING THE STUDY AND SUBMITTAL OF A
REPORT TO THE GOVERNING BODY ANALYZING THE BEST
AVENUE FOR TRANSITIONING THE DOWNTOWN PARKING
PROGRAM TO A CONTRACT OR A PROFESSIONAL SERVICES
MODEL."

WHEREAS, in 2015, the Cheyenne Metropolitan Planning Organization engaged Kimley-
Horn and Associates to study, assess and identify the short and long-term goals for the
development of an efficient, well managed public parking system;

WHEREAS, through Resolution No. 5854 (May, 22, 2016), the Governing Body thereafter
acknowledged receipt of the final report prepared by Kimley-Horn and Associates
(the "Kimley-Horn Report"), with the report's stated purpose of being "a guide for decision makers
on topics such as governance, technology, enforcement, as well as facility and parking asset
management[;]"

WHEREAS, the Kimley-Horn Report provided several recommendations on how the City
of Cheyenne could better develop and manage the City's parking program for the downtown and
following receipt of the report, the City established a transition team to review the Kimley-Horn
Report;

WHEREAS, based on this review, the transition team identified three (3) primary areas of
focus for the City's administration of its parking program in the downtown: 1) the sale of parking
permits; 2) parking enforcement; and 3) the collection of parking fines;

WHEREAS, upon examination of these areas of focus, the transition team identified three
(3) avenues through which to modify and improve the City's parking program. Option No. 1 was
to place the entire parking program under the supervision of the Cheyenne Police Department,
Option No. 2 required that the police department take over only the enforcement of parking
regulations, and Option No. 3 involved contracting with a professional parking management firm
to develop a stand-alone parking program for the downtown;

WHEREAS, based upon the avenues available, the City determined at that time that Option
No. 2 was best suited to improve the City's parking program. As a result, parking enforcement was
placed under the Cheyenne Police Department and, while briefly assigned to the City Clerk's
Office, parking permit administration was also ultimately placed under the police department.

WHEREAS, while the Cheyenne Police Department is responsible for parking permit
administration and parking enforcement, the collection of fines associated with parking violations
was initially to be administered by the Municipal Court. However, in 2020, the Governing Body
directed that parking citations be changed from a criminal offense to a civil fine administered
through the police department, in part, to alleviate the heavy workload placed on the Municipal
Court;

WHEREAS, while the Cheyenne Police Department currently administers all aspects of
the program, it is also charged with a multitude of more traditional responsibilities associated with
the law enforcement objectives of the department. This reality has resulted in a parking program
with limited staffing resources, and combined with other logistical constraints, the department may
not have sufficient resources to administer the downtown parking program without some form of
additional assistance;

WHEREAS, because the current organizational structure of the downtown parking
program is self-managed, it can be categorized as "vertically integrated" with a department head
having complete responsibility for the management of all parking related program elements;

WHEREAS, as a vertically integrated system, the City's downtown parking program is
currently self-managed, but management can be outsourced and contracted with a professional
parking management firm to develop a stand-alone parking program;

WHEREAS, one of the recommendations made through the Kimley-Horn Report was for
the City of Cheyenne to pursue contracting with a professional parking management firm. As
stated by the report, "[u]nder this approach, the current City staff would be retained (although their
specific roles and duties may be modified) and a private parking management firm would be
engaged to manage day-to-day operations with the key goals being to infuse the operation with a
greater depth of parking management/technology expertise, increased management and
enforcement resources, revenue enhancement and program/policy development expertise[;]"

WHEREAS, improving the City’s parking system in the downtown is a critical element for
enhancing the overall downtown experience as well as being an important component in
community vitality and economic development;

WHEREAS, transitioning to a contract or a professional services model should bring
unrealized efficiencies to parking downtown and to the downtown parking program, notably,
through enhanced enforcement resources, which may assist the City in better collecting fines
imposed for parking violations. Regrettably, the collection of parking ticket fines has proved
challenging with the outstanding balance of unpaid parking tickets for fiscal year 2023 equaling
approximately two hundred fifty thousand dollars ($250,000.00); and

WHEREAS, because the Cheyenne Police Department currently administers the
downtown parking program, the Governing Body believes the department is best suited to study
and analyze the best approach for transitioning the program to a contract or a professional services
model; and

WHEREAS, as part of this study, the Governing Body seeks information not only on the
best way to transition, but recommendations on best qualified professional parking management
firms for the City and any associated costs.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE GOVERNING BODY OF THE CITY
OF CHEYENNE, WYOMING, that the Cheyenne Police Department begin analyzing the process
for City transitioning the administration of the downtown parking program to a contract or a
