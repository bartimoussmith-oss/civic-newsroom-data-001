SUBJECT:
Consideration of RFP #9-24 for a Professional Service Agreement between the
City of Cheyenne and TPT Trails, LLC design and construction services for the
Belvoir Ranch Phase 2, Design-Build Trail project.

Proposals were solicited for the Belvoir Ranch Phase 2, Design-Build project. This project is for
the design services and construction of approximately 6.5 miles of a single-track trail system for
multi-use, nonmotorized use. One written technical proposal, with a sealed price proposal, was
received from TPT Trails, LLC.

A review committee comprised of City Staff from Public Works, Community Recreation and
Events, Planning and Development, 1% Construction, and Engineering evaluated the submitted
proposal for a qualification-based selection. The price proposal was opened following the ranking
of the submitted proposal. The price proposal appears appropriate for the scope of services
described in the Request for Proposals and the submitted written technical proposal.

The fees include services design, construction, and GIS data for this project. Funding for this
project will come from the Belvoir Recreation Fund, GL 015-21-2111-45-21301. The total
professional and construction services will be a cost not-to-exceed $219,672.93. We respectfully
request your approval of this professional services agreement.

CITY ENGINEER’S OFFICE

Scope of Work

Design
The Consultant/Contractor shall design a trail system consisting of approximately 6.5 miles of new
natural tread multi-use nonmotorized trails designed for Hikers and Mountain Bikers. The trail width
will be 24 to 30 inches with a low maintenance sustainable tread design. The trail system shall be
configured, developed, and constructed as a stacked loop system. Trail design and construction
shall adhere to the IMBA Trail Solutions standards. Trails will be designed and built to accommodate
users with an intermediate fitness level and beginner to intermediate mountain bike skills. This
project does include a short segment of trail that will require advanced mountain bike skills. The
project will also include signposts and navigational sign placement at all trail intersections.
Directional/location signs will be supplied by the City of Cheyenne.

Trail design must incorporate adequate grade reversals for trails to properly drain and shed water.
Wherever possible, trails should be located and constructed on rock exposures and soils that
support sustainability and reduce maintenance. Sustainability components should include:
•
Support of current and future use with minimal impact to the area’s natural systems.
•
Produce negligible soil erosion, loss, or movement while allowing vegetation to inhabit the
area.
•
Does not adversely affect the area’s animal life.
•
Accommodate existing use while allowing only appropriate future use.
•
Careful trail design that eliminates or minimizes future trail rerouting.
•
Minimizes long-term maintenance.

Construction
Trail construction will be a combination of hand and machine-built tread. It should be anticipated
that much of the trail alignment is not conducive to machine use due to rock outcroppings and other
natural features that should remain undisturbed and minimally impacted for aesthetic value and
preservation of the natural resource.

Construction considerations include:
•
The tread of any trail section steeper than 8% and longer than 25 feet is to be armored with
stone. The same requirement holds for any section longer than 10 feet where the grade
exceeds 10%, and for the entire length of any section steeper than 12%.
•
Full bench cut construction technique wherever possible, otherwise partial bench cut with
large rock edge stabilization. Drainage features should be incorporated into the edge
stabilization.
•
Minimum finished tread width of 24 inches where side slopes are 40% or less. Where side
slopes exceed 40%, or where downhill exposure exceeds 3 feet, the finished tread width of
30 inches, minimum.
•
Average tread out-slope of 3% to 5%.
•
Trail tread surface will be a smooth finish except for unavoidable obstacles or trail
stabilization features measuring 8 inches or less in height. Any rock ramp or step features
incorporated into the trail tread should be shaped or built in a manner that does not create a
trail feature that exceeds an intermediate difficulty rating.
•
Shaped back slope no steeper than 1:1 ratio except where impractical due to terrain
features.

TRAIL GRADE/WIDTH: Trail width will be 24 to 30 inches.  The average trail grade should be 8% or

where in-slope is needed for safety and usability. Back-slopes will be cut/shaped to maintain stable
conditions but in general around a 1.5:1 slope ratio.

DRAINAGE: Drainage structures will be incorporated into the trail tread and trail layout to minimize
the effects of water flow and prevent excessive erosion. Trail shall follow the contour of the terrain
but remain at least 35 feet from stream banks or obvious surface water flow lines except at
crossings. Alignment shall take advantage of natural drainage to minimize the need for major
drainage modifications. In areas where there is a potential for trail erosion, grade reversals shall be
incorporated into the trail tread to create natural appearing drainage dips. Grade reversals can
include both grade dips and rolling grade dips. More grade reversals will be needed on steeper
sections (over 7%) and less needed on flatter sections (under 4%). Water bars will not be used in the
construction of the trail.
