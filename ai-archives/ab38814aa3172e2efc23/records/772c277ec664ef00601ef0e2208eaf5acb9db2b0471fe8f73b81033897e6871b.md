MEMORANDUM
TO:
The Honorable Mayor Patrick Collins and City of Cheyenne Council Members
FROM:
Robin Lockman, City Treasurer
DATE:
August 19, 2024
SUBJECT:
Reappropriation to be considered for approval
On August 26, 2024, the Governing Body will be referring a resolution to the Finance Committee
to reappropriate funds for the Fiscal Year (FY) 2024 City of Cheyenne Budget per W.S. § 16-4-
112 and 16-4-113.  More information is detailed below regarding this final FY 2024
reappropriation request.
GENERAL FUND RESERVES REQUEST
The following General Fund reserve requests are included for your consideration:
Vacation and Sick Leave Accrual Payouts for Terminated Employees
Throughout FY 2024, sick and vacation accrual leave balances were paid out once an employee
terminated their employment with the City.  Generally, accrual payouts are not budgeted for unless
a retirement is anticipated.  If the position is replaced immediately, the budget is usually not enough
to cover the accrual payout.  I am requesting a total increase of $280,973 from reserves to cover
these expenses.
Increase in Natural Gas and Electricity and Fleet Fuel, Labor, and Parts Costs
The City Treasurer’s office estimates natural gas and electricity costs for City facilities during the
annual budgeting process using historical information and price trends.  As a result, departments
are not allowed to use the utility budgets approved by Council for any other purpose.  However,
they also are not required to cover their utility budget shortcomings.  Therefore, I am requesting
$34,639 from General Fund reserves to cover any shortages.
Additionally, the City’s expenditures for fleet fuel, labor and parts have exceeded the approved
FY 2024 budget for several General Fund departments.  These budgets are handled the same way
as utility costs.  As a result, I am requesting an increase of $205,796 from General Fund reserves
to cover this budget shortfall.

GENERAL FUND NEW REVENUE REQUEST
Last year a new governmental accounting rule was promulgated by the Governmental Accounting
Standards Board (GASB) that requires the City to determine the true cost and value of
subscription-based information technology (IT) services, or SBITAs such as software
subscriptions.  GASB 96 was created to provide clear guidelines for accounting and financial
reporting of SBITAs, which are essentially contracts where a government pays to use an IT service
or software for a period of time, similar to leasing an asset.   This includes the City’s OpenGov,
New World, and Microsoft subscriptions, among others.  If the SBITA meets the criteria, we are
required to amortize them, or expense them, over the contract term.  Please note that this expense
does not come from reserves, but rather an increase in revenue in an equal amount.  This is an
accounting entry, and no cash is involved.  As a result of this rule, I am proposing to add $760,719
to the budget.
