INTRODUCTION

Federal Transit Administration (FTA) requires that all direct and primary recipients document their
compliance with USDOT’s Title VI regulations by submitting a Title VI Program to their FTA
regional civil rights officer once every three years or as otherwise directed by FTA.
The City of Cheyenne, Wyoming (the City), is the designated recipient of FTA funds on behalf of
the Cheyenne Transit Program (CTP).  The Cheyenne Metropolitan Planning Organization (MPO)
is the designated Title VI officer for the CTP.
To fulfill the general reporting requirements of Title VI, the CTP has developed a Title VI program
to ensure supported transit services and related benefits are distributed by applicants, recipients,
and sub-recipients of FTA assistance in a manner consistent with Title VI.

PROVISION OF TITLE VI ASSURANCES

In accordance with 49 CFR Section 21.7(a), every application for financial assistance from FTA
must be accompanied by an assurance that the applicant will carry out the program in compliance
with DOT’s Title VI regulations. The CTP Certification and Assurance is Attachment “A”.

INTRODUCTION
The purpose of this limited English proficiency policy guidance is to clarify the responsibilities of
recipients of federal financial assistance from the U.S. Department of Transportation (USDOT)
and assist them in fulfilling their responsibilities to limited English proficient (LEP) persons,
pursuant to Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and implementing regulations. It was prepared
in accordance with Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, 42 U.S.C. 2000d, et seq., and its
implementing regulations provide that no person shall be subjected to discrimination on the basis
of race, color, or national origin under any program or activity that receives federal financial
assistance.
EXECUTIVE ORDER 13166

Executive Order 13166 "Improving Access to Services for Persons With Limited English
Proficiency," reprinted at 65 FR 50121 (August 16, 2000), directs each Federal agency that is
subject to the requirements of Title VI to publish guidance for its respective recipients clarifying
that obligation. Executive Order 13166 further directs that all such guidance documents be
consistent with the compliance standards and framework detailed in the Department of Justice's
(DOJ's) Policy Guidance entitled "Enforcement of Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964-
National Origin Discrimination Against Persons With Limited English Proficiency." (See 65 FR
50123, August 16, 2000 DOJ's General LEP Guidance). Different treatment based upon a person's
inability to speak, read, write, or understand English may be a type of national origin
discrimination.
Executive Order 13166 applies to all federal agencies and all programs and operations of entities
that receive funding from the federal government, including state agencies, local agencies,
Metropolitan Planning Organizations (MPOs), governments, private and non-profit entities, and
subrecipients.
PLAN SUMMARY
The Cheyenne Transit Program (CTP) has developed this Limited English Proficiency Plan (LEP)
to help identify reasonable steps to provide language assistance for LEP persons seeking
meaningful access to transportation services as required by Executive Order 13166. A Limited
English Proficiency person is one who does not speak English as their primary language and who
has a limited ability to read, speak, write, or understand English.
This plan details procedures on how to identify a person who may need language assistance, the
ways in which assistance may be provided, training staff, how to notify LEP persons that
assistance is available, and information for future plan updates. In developing the plan CTP
undertook a U.S. Department of Transportation four factor LEP analysis which considers the
following:

FOUR FACTOR ANALYSES
1. The number or proportion of LEP persons eligible to be served or likely to be
encountered by Cheyenne Transit Program.
According to the 2010 Census the Cheyenne Metropolitan Statistical Areas population
is 14.5% Hispanic, which is the largest minority population. According to the 2010-
2014 ACS 5-Year Estimate from US Census for Laramie County, of the total Hispanic
population (11,627) only 2.1% has Limited English Proficiency. See Attachment “H”.
2. The frequency with which LEP individuals are exposed to the CTP services.
The 6 transit routes cover approximately 80-85 percent of the urbanized area. Using
federal funding through ARRA, CTP created accessible paths to the nearest corner,
placed concrete standing/lift pads at the boarding areas and installed 59 shelters on 62
sites. Currently one third of CTP boarding areas have recognizable bus shelters and all
boarding areas are delineated by two CTP signs on either end. Route and schedule maps
in both Spanish and English are affixed inside the panels of each shelter with a notice
of a number to call for non-English speaking individuals. Transit maps can be found at
most public places and almost all human service agencies counters or brochure racks.
The frequency that LEP persons contact the transit dispatch or enter the CTP office to
purchase passes is undocumented.
3. The impact of the CTP to members of the Cheyenne community.
The CTP system is designed to serve persons who do not have driver licenses, seniors,
the disabled and low income individuals. If LEP individuals are included in one of
these categories, then public transit is very important to them. Most human services
agencies are aware of the Cheyenne Transit program and can provide information to
their clients in need of transportation. List is Attachment “F”.
4. The resources available to CTP for LEP outreach, as well as the costs associated
with that outreach.
a. The US Census American Fact finder website is the primary source of identifying
the location of LEP populations within the Cheyenne area. The State Department of
Education, the local school district and the Department of Family Services are
additional sources of LEP data. The ACS 2010-2014 five year estimate for LEP for
the Asian population and the proximity of those individuals to transit is shown in
Attachment “I”.
b. The FTA and FHWA planning funds are the source for 90.49% of the CTP planning
budget. Conducting LEP research and outreach is done in-house by planning staff.

LIMITED ENGLISH PROFICIENCY PLAN

1. How to Identify an LEP Person who Needs Language Assistance - Below are tools
to help identify persons who may need language assistance:
a. Examine records requests for language assistance from past meetings and events to
anticipate the possible need for assistance at upcoming meetings;
b. When CTP-sponsored workshops or conferences are held, post on the public notices
the contact information for people with special needs;
c. Have the Census Bureau's "I Speak Cards" be made available (see attachment “J”)
when needed and have the cards available at the CTP office; and
d. Frequently survey first line staff of any direct or indirect contact with LEP
individuals.
2. Language Assistance Measures - CTP has or will implement the following LEP
procedures. The creation of these steps is based on the very low percentage of persons
who are LEP in the Cheyenne metropolitan area:
a. The CTP website has installed a translation application.
b. When the CTP hosts public meetings or conferences and a special need is identified
in advance, every effort will be made to have a translator available at the meeting.
c. Census Bureau's "I Speak Cards" are to be located at the CTP office at all times.
d. When an interpreter is needed, in person or on the telephone, an available bilingual
CTP or close proximity staff member will attempt to assist. If a bilingual staff
member is not available, staff will first attempt to determine what language is
required. A telephone interpreter service is available at a reasonable charge online
at Language Line Services - http://www.languageline.com. If CTP finds in the
future the necessity for this service is necessary, then the City may sign up for
Language Line Services. On the Language Line home page, the staff will select the
“Need an Interpreter Now” link and follow the directions to receive an access code.

on procedures to follow. Training topics are listed below:
a. Understanding the Title VI policy and LEP responsibilities;
