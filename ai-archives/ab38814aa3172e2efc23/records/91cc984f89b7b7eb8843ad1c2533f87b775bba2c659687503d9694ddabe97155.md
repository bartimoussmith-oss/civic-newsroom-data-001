RECORD OF PROCEEDINGS FOR THE GOVERNING BODY
OF THE CITY OF CHEYENNE
Office of City Clerk
February 14, 2011
The Governing Body of the City of Cheyenne met in regular session on this date
beginning at 6:00 p.m. in Council Chambers.  Present were: MAYOR - Richard L. Kaysen;
COUNCIL MEMBERS - Mr. Allen, Ms. Ash, Mr. Brown, Ms. Broyles, Mr. Collins, Dr. Rinne,
Mr. Spiker and Mr. Valdez.  Absent: Mr. Pierson.  Also present: Carol Intlekofer, City Clerk;
Barb Dorr, City Treasurer, and Dan White, City Attorney.  The pledge of allegiance was recited.
Presentation of Wyoming Engineering Society Presidential Project Award for 2010,
Grand Prize - Engineering Category, to the City of Cheyenne for the Norris Viaduct Project.
Nathan Beauheim, Assistant City Engineer, advised the City was honored with the grand prize in
the Engineering category at the recent annual convention of the Wyoming Engineering Society.
He presented the plaque that had been received to Mayor Kaysen, on behalf of the governing
body, and thanked those businesses, agencies and staff who assisted with the project.  Mayor
Kaysen advised this was the largest public works project the City has ever undertaken, and
expressed appreciation to the Engineering Society for recognition of the project and to everyone
involved.
Consent Agenda. (All agenda items listed with the designation of [CA] are considered to
be routine items by the governing body and will be enacted by one motion.  There will be no
separate discussion on these items unless a member of the governing body so requests and
support by two other members is received.  Any item removed from the Consent Agenda will be
considered in its normal sequence on the agenda.)  Upon request by Mr. Collins, and pursuant to
support received by Ms. Ash and Dr. Rinne, agenda items #14 and 20(c) were removed from the
Consent Agenda.  Mr. Collins moved to approve remaining Consent Agenda items, seconded by
Mr. Brown.  Motion carried. Voting “yes” – all members of the governing body present.

“An Ordinance amending Section 9.16.100 (B),  Open House Assembly, of Chapter 9.16,
Offenses By or Against Minors, of Title 9, Public Peace and Welfare, of the Code of the City of
Cheyenne, Wyoming, pertaining to the definition of ‘Adult’.”  Speaking in support of the
ordinance were:  Matthew Bowen, who explained his reasons for support included empowering
18-20 year olds to be responsible and to understand the consequences of breaking the law;
Debbie Taylor, spokesperson and volunteer victims advocate affiliated with the Wyoming
Chapter of Mothers Against Drunk Driving (MADD) organization, who discussed strengthening
penalties for minor in possession violations, parental perception, and sending a clear message to
youth that underage drinking is not acceptable, and Lisa Scholz, Cheyenne Police Dept.,
manager of a Core Prevention Grant that the Department has received that involves a “Parents
Who Host Lose the Most” campaign, provided statistical information affiliated with underage
drinking and reviewed addiction factors involving youth drinking.  During comments, Cheyenne
Police Chief Brian Kozak reviewed Police Department statistics from recent years involving
underage drinking, officer time and resources involving response, safety and control measures,
and stated the proposed ordinance would hold 18-20 year olds accountable if they were involved
in hosting a drinking party.  He advised a violation of the proposed ordinance would be a
criminal offense and would require the cited individual(s) to appear in Municipal Court.  He
provided brief information on a community educational program, through grant funding, the
Police Department will be offering in the near future called “Parents Who Host Lose the Most”,
and noted the Department is also working with the local military base on underage drinking
issues.  Reporting for Public Services Committee, Mr. Collins stated there was no
recommendation on the item from Committee.  Mr. Collins moved to approve on second
reading, seconded by Ms. Broyles.  Discussion included the following topics:  Information on
underage drinking and teen hosting of parties involving alcohol; public education; parental
providers of alcohol to youth; methods being utilized by youth to acquire alcohol; whether the
proposed ordinance will make a difference; past ordinances approved to assist with training of
alcohol servers in businesses licensed to serve alcohol, and keg registration control;
inconsistency relative to 18 year olds being able to serve alcohol in restaurant liquor licensed
