RECOMMENDED MOTION:

Move to recommend the Governing Body approve text amendments to the Unified Development Code
Sections 1.4.3 and 5.8.8 as outlined in this staff report and recommended by staff, noting the requests
meets the review criteria of UDC 2.4.1.d.

BACKGROUND:

The City’s Unified Development Code (UDC) was adopted by the Governing Body on January 23, 2012, by
Ordinance Number 3943 with an effective date of April 30, 2012.  The Unified Development Code is the
regulatory document carrying out the vision of PlanCheyenne and is reviewed annually pursuant to UDC
1.1.7 [Annual Review Requirement] to determine if any particular standard or regulation is timely,
relevant and adequately addresses current market conditions. In addition to the annual review, the
Planning and Development Director and City Engineer are authorized under UDC 2.4.1.b.3 to bring
forward text amendments on their own initiative.

The proposed text amendments are to make it clearer as to the definition of a detached building and how
close an accessory building can be to other buildings on a residential property before they are considered
attached. The reason for this text amendment is due to several issues that have arisen with structures
being built adjacent to the principal building and then later being attached.

Currently the definition for a detached building states that there is no common wall connection with
another building. There is nothing in the UDC that states how far an accessory building must be from
another building or the principal building on a residential property. This means that you can place an
accessory building within inches of another building or even the home. Due to this, there have been
buildings built and then later attached to the principal building which creates many issues with setback
requirements and even building code and fire code requirements.

Staff proposes changing the definition of a detached building slightly. The change would add that a
detached building also does not have any structural attachments to another building. The full definition
will state that there is no common wall or structural attachment, which will make the definition even
clearer for what a detached building is.

Staff is also proposing adding a requirement for accessory buildings to be at least 1’ from other buildings
on the property to be considered detached or a separate building. This requirement makes a clear
requirement for how far a building needs to be from another to be considered detached or separate. This
will allow staff to determine setbacks easier and it will reduce the number of accessory buildings that
become attached to the principal building.

Attached to this staff report are examples of some of the issues staff have run into. This is not to call out
these property owners or point blame, but to show why staff has brought forward this amendment to
make things clearer within the code.

PROPOSED AMENDMENTS:
Amend UDC Section 1.4.3, Defined Terms, specifically Building, Detached.

Building, Detached. A building having no common wall connection or structural attachment with another
building.

Additionally, amend UDC Section 5.8.8, Accessory Buildings to add the following:

j. To be considered a separate building, the structures must not share any structural attachment and no
part of either building may be within 1' of the other when viewed from plan view. Two structures closer
than 1' or with a structural attachment shall be considered one building for the purposes of setback, area,
and number.

REVIEW CRITERIA:

Review of a UDC text amendment shall be measured by the following three (3) criteria of Section 2.4.1.d:

1. COMPREHENSIVE PLAN:
“The amendment is in accordance with general goals and policies of the Comprehensive Plan, or
is necessary to address an emerging issue not anticipated in the Comprehensive Plan and is not
inconsistent with the plan.”

This proposed text amendment is necessary to address an issue not anticipated in the
Comprehensive Plan when the Unified Development Code (UDC) was adopted. This issue was also
not addressed when staff did a previous text amendment for accessory buildings. This
amendment does not change the use of the property but provides clearer standards for what a
detached / accessory building is.
