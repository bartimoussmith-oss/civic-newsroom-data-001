32. Presentation of Wyoming Horse Racing "Horse Palace Swan Ranch
Master Plan."
24 - 553
Peter Liguori, Wyoming Horse Racing, gave an update on the project and future
expansion of the facility. He said the project which opened in June 2024 has taken two
years for planning and construction. Liguori said the tax benefits to the county, city,
and state are roughly a million dollars a year. Liguori noted that 70% of the business
comes from out of state. Liguori spoke about their philanthropic mission.
Liguori explained about phase two of the project, includes restaurants, a theater, a
hotel, and a larger space to accommodate additional historical horse racing
machines. The cost would be around $76 million, and 450 team members would be
hired. He noted that the potential financial impact to the county would be around $2
million a year in revenue.
Liguori said the city has agreed to give a letter of support. Liguori said his
organization will go before the gaming commission in January or February, and he
requested a resolution from the county before then. Commissioner Malm thanked him
for his presentation and investment in the community. He said the next step would be
a formal request for a resolution and work with the county attorney's office.
The confidential master and site plans were received and filed.

36. PUBLIC HEARING regarding a site plan for the Whitt's End
Farmstand located in a portion of Sect 30, T14N, R68W of the 6th
PM, Laramie County, WY.
24 - 546
Whitt's End Farmstand Site Plan
Attachments:
Justin Arnold, Planning, said Sarah Lene and Augustus Whitt, and Kenneth Ball
submitted a site plan application for Whitt’s End Farmstand, located at 1249 Happy
Jack Rd. He said the application was submitted to expand the existing farm stand with
a 360 sq ft addition, install RV electrical connections, and plan future livestock
shelters.
Arnold said based on evidence provided, staff recommends approval with the
adoption of findings of fact a and b, with the condition that all agency
recommendations, including public improvements deemed necessary, are addressed
prior to the issuance of a certificate of review.
Commissioner Thompson questioned Arnold about hiring a surveyor to establish the
right-of-way. Arnold deferred the question to Lene Whitt, the applicant. Whitt said the
right-of-way is still being worked on. Commissioner Thompson further questioned
bringing the road on the southern property line to county standards. Molly Bennett,
Public Works, said the agency comment refers to the States Rd east of the Whitt
property line to the Steiner property line. Bennett noted there is potential to create a
hammerhead turnaround or something similar to accommodate emergency services.
Commissioner Thompson asked if the condition would include establishing the
easement and bringing the road to county standards. Arnold said the condition
addresses all agency review comments and incorporates roadway improvements.
Commissioner Thompson asked if the farm stand was within the right-of-way. Arnold
said they would have to verify the structure and a number of variables would need to
be addressed during the building permit process.
In response to Commissioner Thompson’s question about running water and the plan
for black and gray water from RVs, Whitt stated there is no running water for the farm
stand and it is dry camping for sewer. She explained that hand sanitizer and
porta-potties are used.
Commissioner Thompson then asked if there is an appropriate ingress and egress
for the volume of vehicles planned for the site. Whitt explained the use of T-posts and
caution tape to direct vehicles to the parking areas, with possible use of volunteers.
Commissioner Thompson discussed the agricultural exemptions and asked if the
condition would address public safety concerns and require construction of buildings
appropriate for the volume of people visiting the area. Arnold discussed what
constitutes agricultural and commercial use.

Cowboy Kids at Ridley Ranch, 2nd Filing, located in a portion of Sect

24 - 543
Cowboy Kids at Ridley Ranch, 2nd Filing, Subdivision
Permit and Plat
Attachments:
Justin Arnold, Planning, said AVI, PC, on behalf of Cowboy Kids Land Co, LLC,
submitted a subdivision permit and plat application for Cowboy Kids at Ridley Ranch,
2nd Filing, located southwest of the I-25 and Ridley Rd (Road 220) intersection. He
said the purpose of the application is to divide a 283.1 gross acre parcel into 45
tracts. Arnold said the subdivision will have 41 developable lots and four open-space
lots. The open-space lots will be owned collectively and controlled by the subdivision’s
HOA. He said the open space lots can never be developed. Arnold said the proposed
subdivision will remain zoned as LU-Land Use, and any commercial development will
require a site plan and possible zone change.
Arnold said based on evidence provided, staff recommends approval of the
subdivision permit and plat with the adoption of findings of fact a and b with no
conditions.
Chairman Lovett opened the hearing for PUBLIC COMMENT. Cory Isaacson,
resident, spoke about preserving the quiet neighborhood and expressed concern
about the developer reserving the right to put commercial use there, well water usage,
traffic and potential crime. Hearing no further PUBLIC COMMENT, Chairman Lovett
closed the hearing.
A motion was made by Commissioner Holmes, seconded by Vice
Chairman Malm, to approve the subdivision permit and plat for Cowboy
Kids at Ridley Ranch, 2nd Filing, located in a portion of Sect 11, T15N,
R67W, of the 6th PM, Laramie County, WY, with no conditions and adopt
the findings of fact a and b of the staff report.
Commissioner Malm questioned Arnold whether the subdivision would fall under the
new land use rules and regulations. He noted the possibility that the outbound lots,
which are larger and dedicated for residential use, may in the future become
commercial. Arnold said multiple guardrails would be in place governing that situation-
- a zone change application and a conditional use permit to act as a new zoning with
conditions placed by the pertinent body. Arnold noted the area has a major
interchange and commercial development is a likely possibility.
Commissioner Thompson spoke about placing a note on the plat prohibiting further
subdivision. He said he was concerned about preventing future subdivision of the
property and pointed to the tracts greater than 5.2 acres.  He referred to the AMEC
memo and the goal of protecting the aquifer.
