1.The vacation meets the purposes, intent, and applicable standards of Article 4.
The proposed vacation of portions of Cheyenne Street and Van Buren Avenue will allow better
use of the property as developable land rather than unbuilt and unnecessary rights-of-way.This
is because the streets are not necessary considering the context, character and intensity of
expected development in this area.Furthermore, the streets vacation will not reduce the ability of
the adjacent properties tobe developed according to their respective zone district and future land
use plan.The applicant will be coordinating the development of their property and utilities with
the construction of Polk Avenue and the greenway path in an efficient manner.
2.The ability of all existing and potential future development on adjacent lots tomeet all
applicable standards for development in the existing zoning district, including other
development options in the event thatany specific development proposed in association
with the vacation is not executed after approval.
The rights-of-wayvacation will not inhibit the ability for adjacent lots to develop according to
their respective zone districts.AR Agricultural Residential zoning resides to the north and east of
the proposed streets vacation.AR is intended for agriculture and residential uses on a minimum
of % acre lots that typify a rural lifestyle and generally require little or no public infrastructure.
To the south and west of the proposed vacation an LI Light Industrial zoning district is proposed.
In this district, there are no lot area, lot frontage or building height zoning requirements and 25'
setback requirement for all properly lines adjacent to streets and a maximumlot coverage
requirement of 85%.The streets vacation would not restrict either property from developing
according to either theAR or LI zone district regulations.The vacation of Cheyenne Street will
take away a formal street access for platted lots adjacent to the north, however, a 30’access
easement is being provided in place of the right-of-way will allow access.
3.The vacation reflects sound planning andurban design principles with respect to existing
and potential future development in the vicinity, including consideration of current and
future streets, open spaces, blocks and lots, and public utilities or facilities.
Vacating both Cheyenne Street and Van Buren Avenue will not impact the City's street network.
Its important to note that a portions of Van Buren Avenue has already beenvacated and that this
terminated the option for Van Buren Avenue to connect north or for Cheyenne Street to connect
to Van Buren.Furthermore, the streets vacation should have minimal impact on thefuture
development of adjacent properties as these properties will still be able to plat appropriate block
and lot arrangements and have provisions for access, utilities, water, sewer, storm drainage and
open space systems.
4.The application meets the public interest, and is not otherwise prohibited by other city,
state or federal laws.
Because the City does not intend to construct either of these rights-of-way, vacatingthe street to
the adjacent land owners will put platted yet inbuilt streets, which are not planned tobe
constructed, into private property that can be developed.This vacation will also release the City
from any responsibility for maintenance and construction of the rights-of-way.Furthermore, it
would generate property tax dollars rather than sitting fallow asunbuilt streets.It is the City's
understanding that the abutting County land owners are aware that they are appropriating land
that is under City jurisdiction.Should they choose these property owners may pursue de-
annexation of the acquired rights-of-way.
