Community Business.

SUMMARY:

The applicant is requesting a zone change from CB Community Business to HI Heavy Industrial for a
vacant 2.15 acre site located at the eastern edge of the City in the Cheyenne Business Parkway. The
applicant is proposing to expand an existing data center over two parcels which have different zone
district designations; One being HI and the other CB. In order to enable the data center expansion, the
applicant needs to obtain a consistent zoning designation across the two parcels. The subject parcel is
located within an HI district however it is surrounded by a CB zone district to the west, south and east.
Nearby land uses consist of a distributor and manufacturer of agricultural chemicals, an industrial parts
distributor, a satellite internet dish site and a satellite uplink and broadcast center. While the future land
use plan indicates an HI zone district for this parcel, the property is situated within an area that could
support a CB zone district for the following reasons: The subject property is located adjacent to a CB
zone district on three of four sides. Existing land uses in the vicinity such as manufacturing, distributing
and satellite services are compatible with the proposed data center expansion and lastly, the parcel is
large enough to accommodate any necessary buffer and setback requirement between it and the HI zone
district located to the north.

RECOMMENDATION:  The Planning and Development Office recommends approval of the zone change
request from HI Heavy Industrial to CB Community Business pursuant to the review criteria outlined in
Article 2, Section 2.2.1.d of the Cheyenne Unified Development Code (UDC).

ZONE CHANGE DATA:

The applicant is requesting a Zone District Map Amendment (Zone Change) from HI Heavy Industrial
to CB Community Business for a vacant 2.51 acre site. The zone change is being pursued in order to
enable Green House Data Center, located south of the subject property, to expand their operations.

ZONE CHANGE ANALYSIS

The subject property is a vacant 2.51 acre parcel of land which resides west of and adjacent to Progress
Circle. The parcel has four sides but resembles the shape of a pie. The vertical side adjacent to Progress
Circle measure approximately 160’ while the parallel side measures 500’. The horizontal sides measure
approximately 425’and 428’. The parcel is located at the eastern edge of the City, north of Interstate 80
between the North College Drive and Interstate 80 interchange and the Campstool Road and Interstate
80 interchange. To the north of the subject property is the Union Pacific railroad which cuts across the
area at a 45 degree angle from the southwest to northeast. The railroad track acts as a barrier to the
residential land uses to the north. The parcel is not located in a floodway or floodplain.

The subject property is part of the Cheyenne Business Parkway, a 900 acre master planned business park
which is overseen by Cheyenne LEADS. Cheyenne LEADS is a private, not-for-profit economic
development organization serving as the economic development entity for the City of Cheyenne and
Laramie County with a goal of bringing progress and prosperity to the entire community of Laramie
County.

Adjacent to the subject property to the north is Van Diest Supply Company which is a distributor and
manufacturer of agricultural chemicals (www.vdsc.com). Adjacent to the west, south, and east is vacant
land. Other land uses in the near vicinity are the Green House Data Center which resides two lots to the
south and Motion Industries, an industrial parts distributor which resides to the southeast. To the
northeast reside a Wildblue satellite internet dish site and an EchoStar uplink and broadcast center.

The subject property is currently zoned Heavy Industrial (HI) as is the property located to the north,
however,  to the west, south and east resides a Community Business zone district (CB).

The HI zone district is intended for uses of very high intensity which should be isolated and buffered in
order to protect the community and to ensure unimpeded operation of the use. The HI zone district is
applicable to larger land areas that are remote from other differing or incompatible uses to minimize the
potential for incompatible and negative impacts of uses in the district. The HI zone district is appropriate
in areas with access to major regional transportation infrastructure, specifically areas designated
Industrial in the Comprehensive Plan.

The property owner is pursuing a zone change in order to enable Green House Data Center to expand
their operations. The Green House Data Center proposes to develop a data center which will provide
space, power, cooling, and physical security for computer server, storage, and networking equipment of
businesses and to connect them to a variety of telecommunications and network service providers.
Additionally, Green House Data Center is completely powered by wind energy
(www.greenhousedata.com).

The data center expansion is proposed to be developed across two different vacant lots; the subject
property and the adjacent property to the south. Currently, the subject property is zoned HI while the
adjacent property to the south is zoned CB. The applicant needs to obtain a consistent zoning
designation across the two parcels in order to develop the intended expansion of Green House Data
Center.

Data centers such as these are considered office-general uses and  permitted by right in the CB, CBD,
LI, HI, MUB and MUE zone districts. In order to enable the development of this use the applicant is
requesting a zone change from HI to CB.

The CB zone district is intended for Retail, Employment, Service and Civic uses that may serve a
number of residential neighborhoods or other districts in meeting daily needs, discretionary needs, or
other business functions. The CB zone district is applicable to areas where moderate- or large-scale
business uses are appropriate such as areas designated Community Business in the Comprehensive Plan.

ShapeCheyenne, the City’s future land use plan, identifies this area as Industrial. The Industrial category
encompasses the heavier and light industrial areas and generally provides a location where less
restrictive regulations are applied. Appropriate uses for this category are offices, distribution and
warehouses, and manufacturing and fabrication. Industrial areas should be located with access to major
transportation facilities, such as interstates and railroads. Appropriate zone districts for the Industrial

While CB is not an applicable zone district in the Industrial category, the property is situated within an

Pursuant to Article 2, Section 2.2.3b1 and Article 3, Section 3.1.1c1, due to the new use and building
associated with this application a Site Plan and a Transportation Worksheet, at a minimum, shall be
required at the time of development. In addition, right-of-way improvements along Progress Circle will
also be required, the details of such would be determined during the site plan review process.

Pursuant to Article 5, Sections 5.4.2d and 5.5.2d, there are no lot area, lot frontage or building height
zoning requirements in the CB or HI zoning districts. However, the CB zone district has a 25’ setback
requirement from all properly lines adjacent to streets while the HI zone district has a 40’ setback
requirement. Additionally, the maximum lot coverage requirement for the CB zone district is 85% while
the HI zone district is 90%. Given the location, size and shape of the subject parcel, the zoning
regulations for either zone district are capable of being met.

Pursuant to Article 6, Section 6.3.5a (Table 6-10), a level 2 screening and buffering requirement
between the common property lines of a CB and HI zone district is required. If this zone change request
is approved it would create dissimilar zone districts between the subject property (CB) and the property
to the north (HI) which did not exist before. Pursuant to Article 6, Section 6.3.5d2 the applicant would
be responsible for providing and installing the required screening and buffering elements between the
two zone districts. Given the subject property’s size, it is expected that the application would be able to
meet the landscape width, plant materials and the intent of the design standards associated with the level
2 design standards outlined in Article 6, Section 6.3.5b (Table 6-11).

Pursuant to Article 6, Sections 6.9.2a and 6.9.3a-b, in the CB zone district, where site development
occurs without subdivision of land and where the lot does not have access to the Civic Open Space
systems, open space shall be provided on the lot. Given the lot’s square footage and proposed zone
district, open space in the amount of 8% of the building footprint would be required. Furthermore, on-
site open space shall meet the Type and Design Standards suitable for different zone districts and
contexts outlined in Article 4, Tables 4-15 and 4-16. It is expected that the application will be able to
meet the on-site open space requirement and will be addressed with the site plan submittal.

four sides. The existing land uses in the vicinity such as manufacturing, distributing and satellite
services are compatible with the proposed data center expansion. In addition, the proposed data center
expansion would help further economic development in the City of Cheyenne. Lastly, the parcel is large
enough to accommodate any necessary buffer and setback requirement between it and the HI district to
the north.

2. The application is compatible with nearby property, specifically considering existing zoning, the
Comprehensive Plan, and any other official plans and policies created under the guidance of that
plan for these areas.
The proposed data center expansion is largely compatible with nearby property. Nearby property consist
of a distributor and manufacturer of agricultural chemicals, an industrial parts distributor, satellite
internet dish sites and vacant CB zoned parcels. Additionally, a site plan for the proposed use would be
required at the time of development which would require design elements to mitigate any potential
conflicts between adjacent CB uses and HI uses.

3. The suitability of the subject property for development according to the standards and uses of
the existing zoning district, compared to the suitability of the subject property for development
according to the standards and uses of the proposed zoning district.
The parcel is able to meet the lot and building standards of either the HI or CB zone districts. In
addition, the proposed use is permitted in either the HI or CB zone district.

4. The ability of the subject property and surrounding vicinity to be developed according to the
Subdivision Standards in Article 4 considering the proposed zoning.
The subject property and surrounding vicinity are already platted and established with streets, lots and
blocks and are largely served by necessary infrastructure such as utilities, sewer and storm drainage.
Furthermore, the subject property is capable of meeting the required Civic Open Space associated with
the CB zone district by providing on-site open space. As such a zone change to CB would be able to
meet the Subdivision Standards outlined in Article 4, Section 4.1.3.

5. The ability of the application to support the Intent and Applicability statements of the proposed
zoning district, and achieve the Design Objectives of any specific standards.
The proposed data center facility is able to support both the intent and applicability statements of the CB
zoning district as it represents an office use which serves regional business functions. Additionally, the
site is capable of achieving both the site design regulations of Article 6 and the more specific open-space
and building design objectives associated with the General Commercial Design Standards.

6. The ability of all potential future development to meet all applicable standards for development
in the proposed zoning district, including other development options in the event that any specific
development proposed in association with the application is not executed after approval.
The CB zone district provides for a range of development opportunities including Retail, Employment,
Service and Civic uses. It is expected that the data center as well as any other permitted uses will be able
to meet all applicable standards for development in the CB zoning district.

7. The review and recommendations of other reviewing bodies.
No comments were offered by reviewing bodies.

8. Whether the application will harm the public health, safety, or general welfare.
Granting the zone change request from Heavy Industrial (HI) to Community Business (CB) will not
harm the public health, safety, or general welfare.
