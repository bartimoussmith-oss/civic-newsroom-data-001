professional services exposes our firm to an uninsured liability for which we have no coverage. If we
are found liable for professional services, our insurance provider will not defend our clients.
However, if we are ultimately found to be liable for a claim or loss with respect to our professional
services, Felsburg Holt & Ullevig can recover, on behalf of our Client, reasonable attorney fees and
costs from our insurance provider, but only to the extent we are liable.

additional insured and this provides for defense coverage on those types of claims.  Based on this we

The Snapshot task will provide a picture of existing and future traffic and travel conditions along
8th Avenue and set the stage for development of the corridor plan.
Review Existing Documents

We have downloaded or obtained from you copies of the relevant documents listed in the RFP
and familiarized ourselves with the existing conditions and plans in the area. Furthermore, BHA
Design, who produced the Lions Park Master Plan, will assist us in this effort, and the
institutional knowledge they gained during the development of that plan will further strengthen
our understanding of the area.

We will also compile from the MPO all available GIS layers pertaining to the study area,
including but not limited to zoning, land use, topography, water and sewer, crash data, storm
sewer system, transit routes and flood areas.

Finally, will conduct a site visit to the corridor during Cheyenne Frontier Days to observe traffic,
parking and pedestrian operations during that event.  As part of that effort we will coordinate
with MPO staff to collect 24-hour traffic volume data on 8th Avenue, east and west of Carey
Avenue, and on Carey Avenue north and south of 8th Avenue.

Analysis of Existing and Future Data

The existing documentation provided in the RFP appears to provide an excellent breadth of
information on which we can base our analysis. The NTMP in particular provides the existing
traffic, pedestrian, bicycle and crash data, while the Lion’s Park Master Plan and the On Street
Bicycle Plan and Greenway Plan Update provide input on the vision for the corridor.  From the
available data, the traffic volumes would suggest that the everyday travel demand on the
roadway could be accommodated by a two-lane facility, but one area where there is somewhat
of a data void is traffic volumes during Frontier Days, so we will look to Frontier Days staff and
City Public Works staff to provide input on traffic volumes during that event. Such conditions
may warrant the current four lane cross section east of Carey Avenue, but if that is the case, it
would appear possible to develop a plan wherein the roadway is striped for one travel lane, bike
lanes and parking in each direction for everyday conditions, and a event traffic management

Alternatives Development and Screening

We envision that MPO and Technical Advisory Committee input will drive the development of
roadway and intersection design options for 8th Avenue, and that up to two options would be
considered for the roadway and up to two options would be considered for the Carey Avenue
Intersection.

For the roadway design, we will analyze two potential design options. As noted above,
preliminary review of traffic conditions would suggest that reducing the number of travel lanes
east of Carey would be one feasible option for 8th Avenue, and doing such would allow for
provisions of bike lanes and on-street parking (that could be used as a second travel lane during
Frontier Days and major events in Lions Park) and possibly wider sidewalks. The median could
initially be striped as a two way left turn lane, as it is west of Carey and later converted to a
raised median with left turn channelization the entire length of the roadway. Together with
marked pedestrian crossing at key locations along the road, such a design would accomplish
the Complete Streets goal for the project.  We will also explore one other alignment option
based on TAC and MPO input.

For the intersection design, we will also analyze two potential alignment options.  The
constraints created by the park entry pillars, the skewed angle of Carey and the proximity of the
Snyder and Reed Avenue intersections will present challenges, but the current roadway width
created by the existing alignment intersection may provide sufficient room for a roundabout to
replace the current traffic control.

A second option would be to narrow the approaches on Carey to a single lane to help address
sight line issues for northbound and southbound vehicles, and provide channelization and
crosswalks across 8th Avenue on either side of the intersection.  Such an option would also
create additional space on the southbound approach to extend the sidewalk around the pillar
there, where it could continue it up Carey to the Frontier Park parking area by replacing one of
the two travel lanes there.

2. Develop conceptual roadway cross sections with bike lanes, parking/transit pullouts,

3. Develop three intersection design options for Carey/8th Avenue that address
operations and safety issues, and analyze them using Synchro and SimTraffic under
everyday and event conditions.
4. Develop preliminary design plans for each intersection option to determine impacts
to the surrounding environment
5. Evaluate the roadway and intersection options based on a predetermined list of
performance measures
6. Recommend an alternative to be carrier forward into the preliminary and draft
corridor design plans.
Coordination with Staff for Technical Review and Analysis

FHU will coordinate and conduct three meetings with a Project Technical Advisory Committee to
solicit input on the corridor vision and develop the corridor plan.  We envision that the first
meeting would occur prior to the first public involvement activity, so that TAC input on the
corridor vision can be incorporated into the material presented to the public.  The second
meeting would occur after a preliminary plan has been developed, but prior to presentation of
that plan to the public, to provide the TAC an opportunity to comment on and revise the initial
concepts for the corridor.  The final meeting would occur after the draft plan has been
developed, and provide a final opportunity for TAC comments and revisions on the plan.

Tasks
7. TAC Input meeting
8. TAC review of preliminary plan meeting
9. TAC review and comments on draft plan

session with important stakeholders such as property owners, impacted agencies and
developers, which would be followed by a open house-style meeting and presentation session
for the general public.  The first of these meetings would present the project and solicit input on

Draft and Final Plan and Report

After the preferred alternative has been identified and evaluated, we will prepare a draft report
that documents the study process, key decisions, public process, analyses, impacts and
infrastructure recommendations that form the corridor plan.  The plan will include a summary of
existing traffic conditions and future conditions with the improvements, as well as plan sheets
showing both the near-term and long-term improvements for the corridor and the Carey Avenue
intersection.  All technical data will be included in appropriate appendices for that document.
Comments from MPO staff and the TAC will be incorporated into a final version of the
document.

and recommendation for the corridor, including the design plan sheets and
preliminary opinions of probable costs for short term and long term improvements,
from Task 7, below.
2. Prepare a final report that incorporates MPO and TAC comments on the draft plan.
