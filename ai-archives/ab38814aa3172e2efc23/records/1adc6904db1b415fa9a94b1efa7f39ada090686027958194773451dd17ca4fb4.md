Mayor Spiker opened a public meeting to discuss the submission of a grant application to the
Federal Transit Administration, an operating administration of the United States Department of
Transportation, for federal transportation assistance as authorized by 49 U.S.C. §5307, for
approximately $1,083,348.00 in federal funds.  Joe Dougherty, Transit Director, explained how funds
would be utilized and local funding matches through the City, contracts and grants, and program
income, resulting in a total income amount of $1,966,993.00.  There being no further comments or
input and purpose of the public meeting having been met, Mayor Spiker closed the public meeting.
Mayor Spiker opened a public hearing to elicit public comment on a proposed First Mortgage
Revenue Bond to be issued by Laramie County, WY, in an amount not to exceed $4,150,000.00 on
behalf of Peak Wellness Center, Inc., a Wyoming nonprofit corporation, for the purpose of
refinancing an outstanding loan, providing funds for additional remodeling and furnishing of a facility
located at 510 W. 29 St., Cheyenne, WY, for use as a clinic to house the Youth Services Division
th
and administrative offices for Peak Wellness Center, Inc., and paying certain costs incidental to the
authorization, issuance and sale of the bond.  Barbara Bonds, Freudenthal and Bonds PC (law firm),
reviewed past actions before the governing body relative to the proposed bond and explained
circumstances that have delayed the project. She advised although the County, not the City, will be
the bond issuer, the Internal Revenue Service requires the City, as the primary governmental entity
having jurisdiction over the area, be notified of and provide approval for issuance of the bond. She
reviewed the status of bond actions before the County Commissioners and clarified a proposed City
resolution does not obligate the City legally or financially.  Dr. David Birney, Executive Director,
Peak Wellness Center, reviewed the project generally and stated bond issuance will allow Peak to
expand and provide more services to the community. Upon inquiry, Mr. Birney advised demand for
community youth services continues to increase, and discussed issues affecting services. There being
no further comments or input and purpose of the public hearing having been met, Mayor Spiker
closed the public hearing.
Mayor Spiker opened a public meeting to discuss the Cheyenne Transit Program’s boarding
transition to signed “Designated Boarding Areas,” amending the fixed routes of Cheyenne transit
buses, and recognizing the Five-Year Transportation Development Plan, as a flexible planning tool.
Joe Dougherty, Transit Director, provided a report on the updated routes and changes to the routes
involving a transition to designated boarding areas. He explained that currently Transit utilizes a
“wide open boarding” system which allows individuals to board the bus anywhere along the route that
is determined that safe boarding could be provided, and stated as the system has grown there have
become more and more demands for stops.  Mr. Dougherty reviewed a map of Transit service areas,
proposed changes, routes and number of designated boarding areas assigned to each route, and stated
the proposed changes are posted on the City’s website and in various community locations for the
public’s information.  He noted the proposed changes are still open to recommendations. Upon
inquiry, Mr. Dougherty provided information on curb-to-curb service for individuals with special
needs, potential future pilot programs, number of diesel vs. gasoline buses in service, and ridership
levels and affiliated costs.  There being no further comments or input and purpose of the public
meeting having been met, Mayor Spiker closed the public meeting.
