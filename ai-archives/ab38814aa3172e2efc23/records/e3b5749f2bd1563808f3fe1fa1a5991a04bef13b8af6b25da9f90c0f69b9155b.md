Single tier blocks are generally prohibited, but may be allowed with specific City Council
approval. Single tier blocks contain lots known as ‘double frontage lots’ or ‘though lots’ and are
lots with frontages on two parallel streets – one street in the front and one in the rear of the lot.
Lots of this nature can create issues with access, addressing, setbacks, and maintenance. In this
plat, there are lots along York Avenue which fit this description (Block 3). The applicant has
proposed that a Greenway be located along York Avenue. A Greenway is generally maintained
by the City, so this may mitigate some of the potential maintenance issues. To mitigate some of
the other issues presented by single-tier blocks, the applicant should provide a note to the plat
prohibiting lots from being accessed off of York Avenue. The City Council may also “require
additional design elements either in the right-of-way or easements to mitigate the adverse
impacts of double frontage lots and blocks” (UDC 4.3.3.b.2).

Community Facility Fees:
Community Facility Fees are due with this plat. Plat fees will be due on the land that was
originally platted as Interior Heights 4th Filing (no fees charged at the time of that plat due to AR
zoning). This was roughly 20 acres ($14,000 per UDC 4.2.5.b), but areas platted for streets and
open space may be exempted (4.2.5.c), if the applicant can provide a calculation for rights-of-
way within that area. For areas outside of Interior Heights 4th Filing, plat fees paid with
Harmony Valley, 1st Filing can be credited to those due with this plat (and since plat fees have
not changed since the 1st filing, no fees will be charged for that area).For fees based on acreage,
the area of the plat is pro-rated to the nearest tenth of an acre. The required fees for the 20

4) Any phasing proposed in the application is clearly indicated and demonstrates a logical and
well planned development pattern. This may include consideration of amenities and public
facilities of the subdivision within each phase compared to the anticipated timing or the
likelihood of future phases being constructed as planned. (UDC 2.1.2.d.4)

Phasing is proposed with this subdivision. The first phase largely just comes of off York Avenue.
The second phase would connect into Grand Harmony, and possibly into Allison. Phase 2 may be
required to provide an Allison connection for circulation purposes. Phase 3 is on the north side
and still appears to in design. The phasing scheme proposed is appropriate and logical,
especially considering that the developer is actively constructing the development to the
southwest.

The application complies with this review criterion.

5) The impacts identified in specific studies or technical reports that were prepared or requested
to be prepared in association with the application, and whether the findings and
recommendations of these reports, or any modifications or conditions on approval of the
application, can mitigate these impacts. (UDC 2.1.2.d.5)

The applicant submitted a drainage report and a traffic impact study. The applicant also
submitted preliminary engineering plans (showing utility layouts), which prompted some
discussion and comments that generally do not require modifications to the plat application.
No other studies were provided with the Preliminary Plat application.

Drainage:
The review of the drainage report submitted with the preliminary plat generally found the
drainage acceptable for this stage of review. Staff had specific comments regarding storm sewer
inlets and the discharge rate, which will need to be addressed with subsequent submittals.

Traffic:
The traffic study submitted with the application identified a few off-site improvements which
should be considered with this development, including turn lanes on College and the
construction of a York-Cribbon connection. The review of the study also indicated that Grand
Harmony and York should be designed and built as collector streets.

The application complies with this review criterion, with conditions.
