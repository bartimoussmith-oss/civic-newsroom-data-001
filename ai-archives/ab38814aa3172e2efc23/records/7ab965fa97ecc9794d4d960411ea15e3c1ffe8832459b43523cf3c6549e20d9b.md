MASTER PLAN & OBJECTIVES
MASTER PLAN
Introduction
The master plan is the result of a design charette held at the High Plains Grassland Research
Station on September 24, 2002.  Charette participants included representatives of EDAW, a
planning and landscape architecture ﬁ rm; Friends of the High Plains Arboretum; Grasslands
Research Station staff; and regional representatives.  The master plan encompasses a total
of 48.7 acres and takes into account existing land use and character, and responds to siting
requirements agreed upon during the charette.
Mission
The stated mission of the High Plains Arboretum is “to preserve, restore and enhance the
species at the Arboretum, in an effort to educate and encourage the planting and conservation of
adaptable trees and other plants.”
Goals
In order to realize the above mission, the following goals and time lines have been established:
Short-Term Goals (1-3 years)
Preservation:  The ﬁ rst step is to preserve the plant material that exists.  Pruning and removing
debris is paramount in importance.  The preservation stage also includes taking an inventory of
the plant species on the grounds.  This stage requires assessing the situation and getting a clear
picture of what exists to work with.  Current CHFS woody plant trials are included in this stage.
Medium-Term Goals (3-5 years)
Development:  Move the Arboretum in the direction of becoming an educational center.  This
process involves clear labeling of plant species, developing paths (not necessarily concrete)
for visitors, and writing historical and educational information about the Arboretum and the
plant species.  This stage is focused on making the Arboretum accessible to the public and an
attractive and/or educational for the public.  Publicity will begin to take a primary role, as the
Arboretum will need to have interest from the community to move forward.
Long-Term Goals (5-10 years+)
Educational:  The Arboretum has the potential of becoming a positive learning center for the
community.  Besides providing the living classroom of the Arboretum, the Learning Center
can be expanded to teach children and adults about the plant and animal sciences around the
Arboretum.  This focus would draw the interest of schools and other educational groups,
increasing the possibilities of expansion.  The Learning Center can be an educational asset to
the community while preserving and expanding the woody plant treasure of the Arboretum.  It
would prove to be a worthy project.
Long-Term Goals (10+yrs)
Expansion: Expansion of the arboretum to include new groupings of trees and shrubs suitable for
the High Plains.
In addition to these goals, the following are to be pursued:
•
Highlight existing operations
•
Develop relationships with nurseries, agriculture and universities
•
Explore regional connections
•
Remain compatible with the lease
•
Encourage research
•
Develop connections between Roundtop Park & Reservoir and the Research Station
Visitor Experience
The main visitor activities will include:
•
Learning about the history of the Research Station and Arboretum
•
Learning about High Plains horticulture
•
Getting ideas for High Plains home and community landscaping
•
Finding and observing historic plants
•
Exploring High Plains ecology through bird watching and wildlife observation
•
Enjoying passive recreational and social activities in beautiful gardens and wooded settings
•
Learning about the role of the CHFS in establishing communities in the interior west and its
contribution to the plant industry
•
Celebrating Arboretum activities

Organizing Elements
CHFS Woody Plant Trials
The main feature of the Arboretum is 26.6 acres of the remaining woody plant trial plantings of
the former CHFS.  Since these remaining trial plantings have historical importance, the research
and “trial” character of this area is to be retained as a cultural landscape and interpreted as the
main attraction.  Over time, some of the historical plantings will be renewed and rows will
be ﬁ lled out.  Access through the area will be by a 10’ wide universally accessible main path
system that parallels the rows and shelterbelts.  Way stations with shelters, kiosks or interpretive
panels on the main path will direct visitors to speciﬁ c plantings and interesting information
about the collection.  According to their interests, visitors will be invited to leave the main path
in search of subject plants.  At plant locations, labeling and further interpretive information may
be displayed.  Distinct sections of the plantings will be named after individuals who played key
roles in the CHFS history.
Learning Center and Theme Gardens
This 3.5 acre block is located just west of the former CHFS woody plant trials in the Forage
Pasture Block.  It will become the hub of the Arboretum and the ﬁ rst point of visitor contact.
The location was chosen for the following reasons:
•
Central location to the former CHFS woody plant trials and future Arboretum expansion to
the west
•
Visual relationship to the Research Station core
•
Proximity to a suitable parking site
•
Efﬁ cient access to existing utility connections
See the map entitled “Learning Center Sites Evaluated” for a comparison of this site and others
considered.
The Learning Center will include a building with the following spaces:
•
Exhibit hall
•
Meeting/conference room
•
Ofﬁ ces
•
Restrooms
•
Storage and mechanical rooms
•
Snackbar
The building will be designed to allow for phasing and expansion to the east.  The building
will become LEED (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design) certiﬁ ed and illustrate
principles of sustainable design in its materials and siting, such as:
•
Berming on the north elevation
•
South orientation for passive and active solar heating
•
Providing a windbreak to outdoor use areas
•
Skylights and clear-story for “daylighting”/ cooling
•
Collection of roof runoff for irrigation
•
Use of local materials
•
Porous paving
The theme gardens will be adjacent to the Learning Center and will focus on High Plains
horticulture and CHFS introductions and history, such as:
•
Cheyenne Hardy Mum Series
•
Cold hardy fruits
•
Frost hardy vegetables
•
Native fruits
•
Historical plants
•
Ornamental introductions
•
Methods of growing plants in the High Plains
•
Home and garden plant compositions for the High Plains
•
Alternative crops for the future of agriculture
Also around the Learning Center will be an orientation plaza, a small amphitheater sized for
school groups, and a shortgrass prairie turf that can be used for events.  This area is likely to
become a popular spot for weddings and other events and will have to be managed to address
security issues with the Research Station.  A parking lot will be located west, on the windward
side of an existing shelterbelt.  An arbor through the shelterbelt will serve as the gateway to the
Learning Center and Arboretum.
The parking area is about 2.3 acres and is conﬁ gured to be phased, but ultimately will have
spaces for 130 vehicles and 2 buses.  Runoff from the lot will be directed to bioswales and dry
stream channels to demonstrate water harvesting practices.

Pond and Wetland
This 2.5-acre area is south of the parking lot in the vicinity of the pump station.  It utilizes and
enhances the existing irrigation pond as a wetland, providing important wildlife habitat and plant
and landscape diversity to the Arboretum.
Greenhouses and Lath House
These existing structures are southwest of the parking lot and were used by the former CHFS.
Their future use could be shared with the Research Station for propagation and experimentation.
Service access will be from the Station’s secondary access road to the east.
Perennial Plots
These Perennial Plots are south of the Lath House and were also used as part of the former
CHFS operations.  This area, including the Lath House and Greenhouses, occupies 3 acres.
Although currently fallow, the irrigation system remains intact.  These plots will be replanted.
Expanded Arboretum
South of the Perennial Plots are 10.8 acres of unutilized land containing a variety of shelterbelts.
Because of the existing trees, resulting microclimate, proximity to irrigation connections,
availability and adjacency, this area has the best potential for future Arboretum development.
Unlike the row plantings in the former CHFS woody plant trial blocks, new plant groupings in
this section will be informal, natural looking and be dictated by aesthetics.  Plants will consist of
new and otherwise unrepresented materials.
The main path will meander to reveal a variety of landscape effects.  Shelters and seating areas
will add to a parklike atmosphere and create a pleasant landscape for visitors to linger and enjoy
the outdoors.

SIGNAGE
The following sketches show the thematic character of a family of signage elements, including
an entry sign, information kiosk/way station, interpretive panel, pedestrian way ﬁ nding sign and
plant identiﬁ cation label.  In the early phases of Arboretum development, these elements will be
the main items that establish the image of the Arboretum and will need to be mutually supportive
to create an identity.  To this end, the signage system repeats a rural rangeland look made up
of stout poles and timbers.  The systematic use of repetitive horizontal and vertical members,
and the grid on each element, suggests the discipline and order involved in the HPRC scientiﬁ c
process, classiﬁ cation, testing and evaluation of plants.
