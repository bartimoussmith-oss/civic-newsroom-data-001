“A RESOLUTION RATIFYING THE LISTING OF CHEYENNE’S MOORE HAVEN
HEIGHTS HISTORIC DISTRICT TO THE NATIONAL REGISTER OF HISTORIC PLACES
AND AUTHORIZING THE ADDITION OF THE PROPERTIES CONTAINED THEREIN TO
THE CITY’S INVENTORY OF HISTORIC STRUCTURES.” Chuck Lanham, Cheyenne Planning
Office/Historic Preservation, provided information on the purpose of the resolution, which is to
ratify the listing of the Moore Haven Heights structures to the National Register, and to add the
actual listing of properties to the City’s inventory of historic structures.  Mr. Lanham advised there
were some property owners who requested their respective properties be withdrawn from the listing
prior to establishment of the district, and that these would be considered non-contributing structures
without affecting the rest of the structures within the district. He stated once the district is
established by the National Parks Service, it is hard to remove properties from the listing.  Mr.
Lanham discussed the district survey that was conducted; explained property requirements to be
included in an historic district; clarified there are no federal, state or city restrictions other than City
Code addressing demolishing a structure within an historic district (i.e. demolition permits);
commented that properties within an historic district tend to maintain its value longer than
properties in non-historic districts, and noted that sometimes neighborhood residents form their own
association to uphold the historic integrity of the area. Upon inquiry, Mr. Lanham provided
information on notification efforts to property owners to advise of public meetings pertaining to the
proposed historic district designation.  Mr. Lanham and City Attorney Dan White reviewed City
Code demolition application, review and permitting procedures, including the appeal process, and
zoning restrictions.  Matt Ashby, Urban Planning Director, provided further information on the
National Register designation, stating it does not come with restrictions from the federal
government but that local jurisdictions have authority to apply additional restrictions on historic
districts, such as the City’s demolition permit process, and that other elements can be implemented
by City Code to provide property protections.  Upon inquiry, Mr. Lanham commented on positive
aspects to property owners who have structures included in an historic district and a previous grant
request approved by the governing body that assisted with funding to conduct the property survey,
and advised communication with the Building Dept. would be made involving any unsafe
structures.  Speaking in favor of the resolution were:  Kara Hahn, National Register Program
Coordinator, Wyoming State Historic Preservation office; Betsy Bradley, State Historic
Preservation office and property owner within the proposed district; Jeanie Tucker, Rainsford
Historic District resident, and Glen Garrett, property owner within the proposed district.  Topics
presented and discussed included: Letters sent to property owners in the proposed district advising
of the intent to form an historic district; benefits of being a property owner within an historic
district; grant eligibility to owners if their structure is listed on the National Register of Historic
Places; encouragement for creation of more districts within the community; property values;
owners’ responsibility to understand their role and maintain their property within a district, and
designation of districts providing for a community neighborhood identity.  Mr. Brown advised there
was no recommendation on the item from Public Services Committee. Mr. Brown moved to adopt,
seconded by Mrs. Case. Discussion included content of the letter of notification sent to property
owners by the Planning office; concerns with demolition permit applications relative to previous
requests involving the historic Governors’ Mansion protective area; current condition and
maintenance of rental structures located within the proposed district; property owners’ rights; and
future potential transfers of property ownership.  Following a 10 minute recess to allow time to
