“AN ORDINANCE AMENDING SECTION 2.64.090, DEMOLITION PERMIT
REVIEW, OF CHAPTER 2.64, HISTORIC PRESERVATION BOARD, OF TITLE 2,
ADMINISTRATION AND PERSONNEL, OF THE CHEYENNE CITY CODE, PERTAINING
TO APPLICATIONS FOR DEMOLITION AND MOVING PERMITS INVOLVING
CERTAIN BUILDINGS AND STRUCTURES.”  Reporting for Public Services Committee, Mr.
Collins advised there was no recommendation on the item from Committee.  Speaking in
opposition to the ordinance, Sabrina Matthews, shared information on her experience involving
efforts to “save” a house within an historic district by moving the structure to another site, and
the extensive time frame involved to acquire permit approval, indicating a reasonable time frame
should be set for reviewing/processing applications for demolition and moving permits. She
expressed her belief that the Historic Preservation board should not control a personal property
owner’s rights, but should assist and guide what can be done with an historic property. House
District 11 Representative Mary Throne spoke in favor of the ordinance and expressed the intent
of the ordinance is to protect and preserve the character of an historic neighborhood and that
allowing historical structures to be moved out of a district defeats the purpose of the Historic
Preservation board and designation of a specially protected district.  Mr. Collins moved to
approve on third and final reading, seconded by Ms. Broyles.  Discussion followed on the
physical condition of premises within the Governors’ Mansion Historic area involving recent
demolition actions; current process involved in reviewing and approving/disapproving
demolition permits for properties located in an historic district; purpose and authority of the
Historic Preservation board and its ability to deny a demolition permit; initial intent of Historic
Preservation board review of demolition permit applications; concern with property owners’
ability to have control of personal property interests; burden on property owners relative to the
permitting process, and potential expense to property owners involving appeal of a decision by
the Historic Preservation board to deny a permit(s).  Motion failed (due to a tie vote).  Voting
“yes” – Ms. Broyles, Ms. Case, Mr. Collins, Mr. Laybourn and Dr. Rinne.  Voting “no” – Mr.
Brown, Mr. Pierson, Mr. Segrave, Mr. Valdez and Mayor Spiker.

freedom of speech, elected officials’ commitment to office and dedication of time involved;
democratic process; length of meetings; constituent representation, and use of Robert’s Rules of
Order for efficient conduct of meetings.  Steve Freudenthal spoke in favor of a proposed
substitute ordinance, stating his belief that the substitute embodies a “balancing” between the
majority and minority.  He explained the substitute provides that ordinances will not be included
on a consent agenda and that removal of an eligible consent agenda item(s) would require the
concurrence of two other members of the governing body.  He commented that concerns raised
relating to suppression of free speech is without substance; made statements relating to the
democratic process, and encouraged effective meetings involving the use of the public’s time.
Mr. Collins reported there was no recommendation on the item from Public Services Committee.
Mr. Collins moved to approve, seconded by Mr. Segrave.  Mr. Collins moved to amend by
substitute ordinance, seconded by Mr. Segrave.  Upon inquiry by Mr. Halyak concerning the
proposed substitute, Mayor Spiker stated the substitute would require that a request by a Council
member to remove a Consent Agenda item(s) must be concurred by two City Council members;
that ordinances will not be placed on a Consent Agenda, and that a member of the public can
request a Council person to remove an item.  Commenting on the motion to amend by substitute
were:  Louise Waters, Mr. Quirk, Mr. Melko, Pacomio Sandoval, Gloria Milmont, J. Fred Volk

and suggestion of postponing action on the substitute in conjunction with final reading.  It was
noted the proposed substitute was submitted and reviewed at Public Services Committee.
