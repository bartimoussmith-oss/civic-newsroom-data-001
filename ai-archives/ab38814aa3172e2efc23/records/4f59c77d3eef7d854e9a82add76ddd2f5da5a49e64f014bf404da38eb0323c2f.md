Staff Report – Special Use Permit: Oil & Gas Support Services
June 1, 2011
Updated June 8, 2011
Overview:
The recent oil exploration activity that has evolved quickly in our community is resulting in the need for
locations for oil and gas service uses, including water haulers and pipe storage.  Due to the currently
unknown nature of the extent of the oil play in our area, there is some reluctance to develop storage
sites to the full breadth of City requirements until such time as the true extent of the play is better
understood.  The purpose of this Special Use Permit would allow for placement of oil and gas support
uses on properties while deferring specific land development requirements, like landscaping and
connection to water and sewer facilities, until the full extent of the oil play can be determined.

Key Regulatory Provisions:

The site shall be zoned HI, Heavy Industrial.  The purpose of restricting the use of this permit
type to is so that these uses are located at a distance from any residential uses that might be
impacted.

The site improvements that may be deferred by the Governing Body are limited to landscaping
requirements, right of way improvements, parking lot surface paving, or connection to City
water or sewer as required by Code.

The deferral period is limited to 2-years.

A full site plan shall be submitted illustrating the full scope of development requirements as
required by City Code, including those elements that have been requested for deferral.  The
Special Use Permit shall be submitted concurrently to be approved by the Governing Body to
determine if it is appropriate to defer the requested improvements.

The Special Use Permit does require that all outside storage be screened from rights-of-way and
neighboring properties in order to mitigate visual impacts on adjacent properties.

Planning Commission Recommendation: Updated 6/8/11
The City of Cheyenne Planning Commission recommended approval of the ordinance by a unanimous
vote of 6-0 on June 6, 2011, including staff recommendations 1-8 and reflecting key clarifications
discussed during the meeting.  Key discussions included the following:
a. Desire to provide some additional language defining uses allowed.
b. Describing that uses requiring restroom facilities would not be allowed.
c. Some additional clarification and flexibility on the bonding requirement.
d. Discussion related to limiting the use to Heavy Industrial.  General commentary was that the
limitation was appropriate given the expectation of this type of use in a Heavy Industrial Zone
District.  Additionally, there was discussion on the posted notice requirement and that this was
reasonable due to the nature of the uses allowed in the zone district.

Upon review of the ordinance, several formatting and organizational modifications were required.  The
ordinance submitted for Council review reflects these modifications.  Generally, the changes relate the
headings and overall organization of the ordinance.

Staff Recommendations:
Staff recommends adoption of the ordinance with the following conditions #1 through #8:
1.) Prior to submitting the ordinance to the Governing Body, modify the ordinance to address any
concerns from the City Attorney.
2.) Delete the following language in G.3.c.  “and state the specific reasons for denial.”
3.) Delete the following language in G.4.  “A Special Use Permit may not be issued by the City
Council for the same property, nor for the same applicant within 1 year following the expiration
of the previously approved Special Use Permit.”
4.) Add language clarifying that the Site Plan submittal includes standard requirements for traffic
and drainage studies.
5.) Provide a clear definition of what “Oil and Gas Support Services” include specifically.
6.) Exclude “Maintenance” activities from the definition of allowed uses.
7.) Add “Adequacy of infrastructure supporting the site” as one of the review criteria.
8.) Add language requiring a mitigation plan and bond to ensure site is restored following the
Special Use.

Comment Received:

Building Department:  Recommend adding new sections –
I. Responsibility for Compliance.  The permit applicant and the owner of the land shall be jointly
responsible for compliance with the terms of the Special Use Permit and all other applicable City of
Cheyenne regulations.
J. Conclusion of Permit.  At the end of the term of the Special Use Permit, the permit applicant and the
owner of the land shall ensure that the land and all roads adjacent to the property are in the same
condition as when the Special Use Permit was issued.

Urban Planning:
 I recommend deleting the language in G.3.c.  “and state the specific reasons for denial.”  This type of
statement is more appropriate for administrative decisions.  Also, delete the language in G.4.  “A Special
Use Permit may not be issued by the City Council for the same property, nor for the same applicant
within 1 year following the expiration of the previously approved Special Use Permit.”  This language
could be perceived to limit Council’s approval authority inappropriately.

Additional comments received from Engineering include:
1.) Eliminate maintenance from the permissible uses in 17.116.155(c).  With maintenance
operations, there is too much possibility of spilling oil and washing of contaminants off of
vehicles and into the drainage system.  With modern day environmental regulations,
maintenance is an activity best conducted in a permanent location.
2.) As an MS4 community, the City is ultimately responsible to DEQ (and ultimately EPA) for any
contaminants (including sediment) that get into the creeks.  If we start allowing people to start
skipping parts of the system, we need to have a well thought out plan for how we’re still going
to meet our obligations.

criteria.
