Planning Commission Recommendation:
The Planning Commission heard this item at their May 17, 2021 meeting and voted 4-0 recommending
approval of the ordinance. The Planning Commission recommendation included the additions of
reviewing the Right-of-Way size and including provisions regarding electric vehicle charging stations.
Planning and Development staff are addressing the recommendations by:

1. Informing developers and applicants that there may be the ability to vacate some Right-of-Way
and return it to property owners on a case by case basis. Staff opted not to include this
component in the Urban Use Overlay as it is not specific to the district and will need to be
assessed based on individual property characteristics. If staff deems right-of-way vacation
appropriate, the developer will be informed at a pre-application meeting.
2. Electric vehicle charging stations have become more prevalent over the past several years.
Rather than incorporate them specifically into the Urban Use Overlay, staff will assess the use
holistically and develop regulations for charging stations in the UDC. Staff will determine the
appropriate regulations for each zoning district through this effort, rather than drafting a section
specific to the UU overlay.

BACKGROUND:

The City’s Unified Development Code (UDC) was adopted by the Governing Body on January 23, 2012, by
Ordinance Number 3943 with an effective date of April 30, 2012.  The Unified Development Code is the
regulatory document carrying out the vision of Plan Cheyenne, and is reviewed annually pursuant to UDC
1.1.7 [Annual Review Requirement] to determine if any particular standard or regulation is timely,
relevant and adequately addresses current market conditions. In addition to the annual review, the
Planning and Development Director and City Engineer are authorized under UDC 2.4.1.b.3 to bring
forward text amendments on their own initiative.

Planning efforts in the West Edge District have been underway for nearly a decade in the City of Cheyenne.
One of the most consistent and critical components of these planning efforts has been the
recommendation to rezone the area. The West Edge plans of both 2014 and 2016 note that the area
should consider the Mixed-Use Business Emphasis or Mixed-Use Residential Emphasis zones with special
characteristics. Through extensive outreach and comprehensive internal efforts, the Planning and
Development Department has crafted the Urban Use overlay district to fulfill the West Edge vision.
Through the efforts, staff has determined that the use of the district is prudent in the West Edge but may
also be appropriate for other near downtown areas in need of redevelopment options. The Urban Use
overlay district is intended for Urban Development adjacent to the Central Business District of Downtown
Cheyenne. The UU district is intended to transition and step down from the urban core of Cheyenne into
the surrounding neighborhoods. In order to utilize the UU overlay district, the subject properties must
have a base zoning district of MUB, MUR, or P.

DISCUSSION:

Planning and Development staff began working on the Urban Use Overlay in the Spring of 2020. In the
early Spring staff met with several stakeholders in the area to determine what the stakeholders expected
from the rezoning effort. Several stakeholders discussed that they supported a rezoning effort but noted
the need for flexibility in the zoning regulations and consistency in the applicability of the rezoning. Noting
that there appeared to be broad support for the rezoning effort, staff planned an outreach strategy and
steps to begin the rezoning effort. Initially staff anticipated holding a kickoff meeting on March 25, 2020;
however, the meeting was postponed due to the Covid-19 pandemic. As staff realized the pandemic would
be on-going for the foreseeable future, the meeting was transitioned to a web base platform. The purpose
of the meeting was to determine if the content from the West Edge Plans of 2014 and 2016 was still
relevant and resonated with community members. The meeting was available for 2 weeks and had 17
members of the public comment on the information. Although the participation was low, the effort
yielded that the plans still resonated with the community. Through reviewing the plans and assessing
preliminary outreach, staff determined a new zoning overlay should be drafted that can be applied to the
MUR, MUB, and P base districts. This new district would promote redevelopment through flexible
dimensional standards; exceptions for utilizing existing structures and buildings; and promoting uses
compatible with the West Edge plans, such as retail, office, and a variety of residential formats. Staff
anticipated drafting these regulations in the Summer of 2020; however, were unable to due to staffing
shortages in the Planning and Development Department.

Staff rebooted the regulation drafting effort in October of 2020. The drafted regulations reflected the
feedback staff had gauged at the Spring 2020 meeting and the content of the 2014 and 2016 West Edge
Plans. After internal meeting and corrections, staff brought the draft regulations forward at several public
meetings and work sessions. The meetings were presented with the Mentimeter platforms which
promoted the opportunity for live feedback and polling to promote discussion. The results of the meetings
are included as an attachment in this staff report. Staff held the following meetings for public outreach:

•
Reed Avenue Rail Corridor Steering Committee – December 7, 2020
•
Planning Commission Work Session – January 18, 2021
•
Downtown Development Authority Work Session – February 21, 2021
•
West Edge Public Meeting – February 23, 2021
•
West Edge Public Meeting Online Edition – February 23 – March 15, 2021
•
City Council Work Session – March 19, 2021

After the extensive public outreach, staff reassessed the drafted regulations and made targeted changes.
Most of the comments reinforced the regulations as drafted and were in support of many of the concepts
staff has put forward. The proposed amendments are included in the following section of the staff report.
In general, the amendments promote flexibility in uses by permitting uses included in the base zoning
districts of MUB, MUR, and P; allow existing buildings to be utilized noting their likely non-conformities to
promote adaptive reuse; remove parking minimums to allow the developer and their financing
mechanisms to determine how much parking will ultimately be provided; create more bicycle parking and
active street fronts to foster a multi-modal and pedestrian friendly environment; and simplify the
dimensional standards to remove the difficulty in interpretation of lot types.

The drafting and adoption of the Urban Use Overlay district is the first step in rezoning the West Edge.
Once these regulations are adopted, staff will lead a mapping effort to rezone the entirety of the West
Edge to Mixed-Use Business Emphasis with the Urban Use Overlay. This will be a City-initiated rezoning,
consistent with the feedback heard at all outreach meetings. In addition to this, it is important to note

Review of a UDC text amendment shall be measured by the following three (3) criteria of Section 2.4.1.d:

1. COMPREHENSIVE PLAN:
“The amendment is in accordance with general goals and policies of the Comprehensive Plan, or is
necessary to address an emerging issue not anticipated in the Comprehensive Plan and is not
inconsistent with the plan.”

This proposed text amendment is consistent with the principles and policies within PlanCheyenne.
Specifically, the proposed amendment addresses the following policies and principles:

•
Policy 1.1.B: Flexibility to Respond to Market Demands.
The UU Overlay text amendment will provide flexibility with zoning regulations to promote
infill development, adaptive reuse, and redevelopment of an important area in the City of
Cheyenne. The flexibility should incentivize the market to consider developing in the West
Edge.

•
Policy 1.4.A: Infill Opportunities
The Urban Use Overlay text amendment provides the opportunity for infill development
through regulations that promote flexibility, but also consider the character of the area in
which they are located.

•
Policy 1.4.C: Downtown Revitalization and Mix of Uses
The Urban Use Overlay can be applicable to the MUR, MUB, and P zone districts in or adjacent
to downtown. As the Urban Use Overlay is applied to the West Edge it is anticipated to
revitalize the area and further mix uses in the area. Staff anticipates the development of the
West Edge will greatly benefit the downtown as a whole.

•
Policy 1.4.D: Infill and Revitalization Incentives
The Urban Use overlay achieves several items that are mentioned in this policy; such as
reducing parking minimums, providing flexible dimensional standards, promoting higher
densities than currently permitted, and providing flexibility in design requirements for
utilizing existing buildings.

•
Policy 2.3.A: Mix of Housing Types
The Urban Use Overlay will lead to the West Edge being zoned MUB or MUR; each of which
provide ample opportunities for a variety of housing.

2. STATE STATUTORY REQUIREMENTS:
The amendment is consistent with the Wyoming Statutes, and specifically the Municipal Planning
and Zoning statutes. The following Articles are all listed verbatim from Wyoming State Statute.

WYO 15-1-602 (a):
The governing body shall specify how regulations, restrictions and the
district boundaries are to be determined, established, enforced,
amended, supplemented or otherwise changed.
Assessment:
State statute states that the governing body shall specify how regulations
regarding zoning are established and enforced.  The governing body
possesses the authority to amend regulations and therefore can amend
the Unified Development Code to reflect this text amendment.

WYO 15-1-601 (a):
The governing body of any city or town, by ordinance, may:
(i) Regulate and restrict the:
(D) Density of the population
(E) Location and use of buildings, structures and land for trade,
industry, residence or other purposes.
Assessment:
This amendment proposes modifying the Unified Development Code for
the purposes of regulating the location of buildings and density of the
population; thus, fulfilling the requirements of this statute.

WYO 15-1-601(d):
All regulations shall be made:
(ii) With reasonable consideration, among other things, of the character
of the district and its peculiar suitability for particular uses;
(iii) With a view to conserving the value of buildings and encouraging the
most appropriate use of land throughout the city or town.
Assessment:
This amendment proposes modifying the Unified Development Code for
the purposes of encouraging redevelopment and infill development in
the City of Cheyenne with respect to the existing character of the districts
to which these regulations are applied. Furthermore, these regulations
contain provisions for height regulations to preserve views of existing
prominent structures. Regulations of this nature are allowable under
state statue and may be modified by the Governing Body.
