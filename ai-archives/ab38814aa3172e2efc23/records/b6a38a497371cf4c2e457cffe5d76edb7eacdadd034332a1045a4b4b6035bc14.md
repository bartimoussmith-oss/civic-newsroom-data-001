EXECUTIVE SUMMARY:
This Vacation Request isfor4,086squarefeet of Evans Avenue Right-of-Way adjacent to a
single lot, which would then become part of the single property ownership.This Request would
include that ROWup to the attached, paved Greenway, whichwould remain in the public space.
Typically, any property owner is responsible for the maintenance of the sidewalk and Right-of-
Way adjacent to their property.Given the considerable width of the ROW not used for
transportation due to the circumstances related to the closure of 4th Avenue, the applicants have
submitted the request in order to extend their sideyard lot line out(it's currently at or about O'
from the house, like all the homes along Evans Avenue to the north).
In 2005, Evans Avenue was reconstructed with Greenway along the west sideof the road in lieu
of a sidewalk.At that time, it was determined that the problematic intersection that existed at E.
4th Avenue and Evans would be remedied by closing 4"h Avenue, which now ends in the bulb-out
(though emergency equipment can still get to 4" from Evans).The reconstruction included the
new driveway and current configuration of landscaping and street trees along Evans.Parks and
Recreation ended up“inheriting”the maintenance for this pocket area.They extended a metered
irrigation tap and routinely mow, edge, andweed the area.They conduct back flow prevention
and blowouts of the system, as well as a monthly irrigation check. They maintain the area to this
day and fully support this vacation request, as it would relieve them from the responsibility.The
irrigation tapwill either have to be disconnected or transferred tothe property owner should the
vacation request be approved.
VACATION DATA:
Inreviewing, providing comments, and making recommendations and decisions on a proposed
street vacation, the staff, Planning Commission and City Council shall use the following criteria:
The vacation meets thepurposes, intent, and applicable standards of Article4.
While thevacation would not substantially alterthe connectivity in the area, it would reduce the
ROW to a width of about 60', where the rest of Evans Avenue is 80'.Evans is classified as a
collector road, and thebest practice is to reduce the number of accesses onto collectors, due to
the volume.Evans most closely resembles a Type C Collector, which calls out for at least 70’ of
ROW (See Attached Exhibit).Theback of curb roadway width narrows along this stretch from
about 40’to34', and no on-street parking is allowed adjacent to the area that is the subject of this

Current fence regulations allow for property owners tofence along their property lines, except

tall fence could potentially be erected along the new lot line.However, no fence shall be
constructed within 2’of any public sidewalk(or Greenway), irrespective of where the lot line is.
The vacation reflects sound planning and urban design principles with respect to existing and
potential future development in the vicinity, including consideration of current and future streets,
openspaces, blocks and lots, andpublic utilities or facilities.
Thevacation of this ROWwill limit future widening, if necessary, and could impact the
streetscape by reducing the“open space”currently maintained there, especially if the new
ownership decides to fence in the property. Given that this ROW was reconstructed only10
