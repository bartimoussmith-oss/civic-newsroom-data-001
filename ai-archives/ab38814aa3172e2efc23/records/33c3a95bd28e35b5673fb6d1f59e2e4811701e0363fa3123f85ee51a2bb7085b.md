ORDINANCE - Amending Section 9.16.070(A), sale of tobacco to persons under the age
of eighteen years prohibited, of Chapter 9.16, Offenses by or against minors, of Chapter 9, Public
Peace and Welfare, of the Cheyenne City Code, pertaining to electronic cigarettes.  Officer John
Gay, Cheyenne Police Department (CPD), advised CPD supports the proposed ordinance change
and cited benefits of incorporating the state statute restriction within municipal court’s
jurisdiction for officer efficiency purposes; to provide the ability to verify re-occuring offenses;
to have a pre-determined bond/fine schedule, and for any juvenile assistance programs that may
be available through municipal court.  Amy Schicketanz, owner, Straight Line Vapors, and Lisa
Ammons, Wyoming Prevention Management office, each spoke in support of the ordinance to
restrict sales to minors.  Each discussed and provided data and statistical information on e-
cigarettes as well as regular cigarette tobacco products.  Ms. Schicketanz comments included the
following topics: E-cigarettes as a helpful tool for those who are trying to quit smoking; lack of
data reflecting that kids become addicted to e-cigarettes; her opposition to any potential future
ordinance involving e-cigarettes as a tobacco product and the City’s current smoking in public
places regulations; nicotene and e-cigarette (cartridge(s)) ingredients and chemicals, and review
of enforcement measures prior to the ordinance being implemented.  In support of her comments,
Ms. Schicketanz submitted a packet of data on e-cigarettes (on file with City Clerk’s office) to
members of the governing body.  Ms. Ammons’ comments included composition of e-cigarettes
and how they’re used (inhaled); court rulings determining e-cigarettes as tobacco products;
similar laws in other states, and lack of regulation of the products by the FDA.  Mr. Allen moved
to approve on second reading, seconded by Ms. Broyles.  Upon inquiry, John Ridge, Deputy City
Attorney, clarified if a citation is written as an offense against city code rather than state statute
then the fine would go to the City.  Clarification was made that the ordinance would not be
outlawing e-cigarettes but is aimed only at restricting sales of e-cigarettes to minors.  Discussion
included the following topics:   “Mirroring” state statute; preventing youth from gaining access
to these devices; whether fines assessed to be received by the City was overshadowing the
purpose of the ordinance, and procedural benefits for police officers to be able to cite offenders
into municipal court vs. circuit court.  Motion carried.  Voting “yes” -- all members of the
governing body present with the exception of Mr. Luna voting “no”.
