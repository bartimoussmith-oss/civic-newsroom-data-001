Attached is a track changes version of a substitute ordinance providing minor corrections and
incorporating Dr. Rinne’s requested change in height allowances for stan-alone small cell poles.
Staff had stated at the first Public Services Committee meeting that a substitute ordinance
would be present as final review of the ordinance was occurring by the City’s consultant, Bob
Duchen of Rivet Oaks Communications. The substitute ordinance essentially provides
necessary clarifications, renumbers inconsistent numbering, and corrects a conflict between
small cell wireless pole heights in UDC 5.8.1.c.1.(d) on page 5 and 5.8.1.e.1.(f) on page 10.
At Public Services Committee on June 18, 2019 Dr. Rinne asked for an amendment to decrease
the standalone height for small cell towers from 50’ to 40’. That request is reflected in the
substitute ordinance and shown in UDC 5.8.1.e.1.(b) on page 9 and 5.8.1.e.J.(e) on page 10.
Verizon Comments
Upon introduction, Verizon had a variety of comments that were documented in the initial memo
presented to the Public Services Committee.
A majority of those concerns have been
addressed through discussion with the Verizon representatives, however they still request that
the City amend the pole height extension allowances in UDC 5.8.1.c.1.(d) on page 5 and
5.8.1.e.1.(f) on page 10 from 20% to 8’ to allow for colocation.
Staff has not proposed an amendment, however notes that the increase would allow for
colocation of two small cell systems on an existing pole (light and signal) since the FCC typically
requires four feet of separation between technology types (e.g.: 4G and 5G technologies), lithe
City Council elects to keep the ordinance “as is” it could result in new poles being installed for
other antennas; whereas ii amended to eight feet it would allow multiple installations on existing
poles.

Charter Comment
Eric Rasmussen, representing Charter Communications, has requested an amendment to the
ordinance to exempt micro wireless facilities from administrative review. Micro wireless facilities
are a type of small wireless facility that is less than 24 inches in length, 15 inches in width, and
12 inches in height and that has an exterior antenna less than 11 inches in length.
As indicated in Mr. Rasmussen’s correspondence June 27, 2019 email correspondence to the
City Council we did discuss the following modifications:
Page 6
— new subsection 5.8.1 .c.2.(h) under Exempt Facilities on page 5
“Installing a micro wireless facility attached as an accessory to a wireline system(s),
such as conventional cable or telephone strand”
Page 9
— Deleting subsection 5.8.1 .d.2.b.(5) on page 9.
“(5) Installing a micro wireless facility through the use of low-powered
transmitters/receivers attached to existing wireline systems, such as conventional cable
or telephone strand.”
The City’s contract telecommunication attorney, Bob Duchen of River Oaks Communications,
has reviewed the proposed changes and believes the City could carve out an exception for
micro wireless facilities from administrative review as long as Charter’s design is satisfactory to
the City. However, Mr. Duchen recommends that this is not included in the exemption section
because the City may still want to charge a fee for them. He further noted that since they are
smaller than typical Small Wireless Facilities (SWF), they could have a lesser annual fee.
Mr. Duchen further stated that the FCC’s number has stated a safe harbor amount of $270 /
SWF / year for standard SWFs in the ROW. Charging lesser fees for micro wireless facilities
could be charged less could incentivize other providers to deploy smaller facilities.
In the event the City Council wishes to approve the amendments it would:
1.
Allow in-line micro wireless facilities to be processed quicker as the only permit
requirement would be an electrical permit.
2.
Would save staff review time.
3.
Encourage micro wireless facilities on existing wire spans.
4.
Would not allow the City to collect an annual fee for the use of the right-of-way (at least
$270 / year).
5.
Potentially result in a competitive advantage for providers owning existing wireline
systems.
Staff does not recommend incorporation of the amendments at this time. In the event the City
Council desires to approve the amendment, the language above is acceptable.
