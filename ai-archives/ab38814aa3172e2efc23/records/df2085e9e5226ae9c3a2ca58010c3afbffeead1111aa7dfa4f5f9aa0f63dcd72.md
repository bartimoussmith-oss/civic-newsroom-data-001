"A Resolution authorizing the Mayor and the City Clerk to sign a Final Plat for The
Pointe, Fifth Filing, a replat of Lots 13-25, Block 13; Lots 27-69, Block 14; Lots 1-35, Block 16;
Lots 1-22, Block 17; Lots 1-27, Block 18; Lots 1-32, Block 19; Lots 1-19, Block 20 and Lot 1,
Block 21, The Pointe; and Lots 37-38 and 71, Block 14, The Pointe, Second Filing, Cheyenne,
Wyoming (located at the southwest corner of the intersection of E. Four Mile and Powderhouse
Roads).”  Kevin Lewis commented on the drainage report, stating his belief that it was
incomplete and that the final plat should not be approved until a final drainage plan has been
submitted and available to property owners in the area to review and provide input.  He further
expressed his concerns with existing vegetation conditions involving weeds.  Mr. Collins moved
to adopt with staff recommendations #1, 2, 5, 6 and 8, seconded by Mr. Pierson.  Upon inquiry,
Brad Emmons, AVI, agent for applicant, advised the drainage report will reflect areas that would
handle drainage and explained they are working with the City Engineer and the school district on
exact locations where drainage easements will be located and, when finalized, this information
will be included on the school district’s site design.  Upon further inquiry, Mr. Emmons clarified
the construction drawings will reflect re-seeding of areas after grading and explained prior to
signatures being obtained on the final plat, the drainage plan and easements will be approved by
the City Engineer.  Upon inquiry, Dave Royer, project manager, explained as the property is
developed and graded (stripped), re-seeding will be done to assist with weeds/vegetation
concerns.  He further explained water trucks will be utilized during construction to help alleviate
the dust, noting high winds at times are a problem affecting efforts to minimize the effects of
blowing dirt, dust and materials. Discussion included the following topics: Final drainage report
and opportunity for neighborhood review and feedback; maintenance responsibilities of drainage
areas; flooding of areas during past development phases in the area, consistency in standards for
developers, and drainage concerns generally.  Doug Vetter, City Engineer, advised his office has
been working with the developer and the school district on drainage issues; that drainage
easements will be included on the plat; that an appropriate amount of drainage retention for the
area is to be provided, and that the plat will not be signed until a final drainage plan has been

“A Resolution supporting the City of Cheyenne’s application for a Land and Water
Conservation Fund Grant for the Martin Luther King Jr. Park Tennis Court Project (2007-2010
1% Sales Tax and City In-Kind Contribution).” Mayor Kaysen announced this item was being
withdrawn at the request of the sponsor in order to allow interested parties to meet and discuss
current conditions and amenities at the park, including future overall developments or projects,
and specifically relative to the existing tennis court and proposal of 2 new tennis courts.  Mayor
Kaysen noted as the item was being withdrawn there would be no discussion or action
entertained on the item.  Upon inquiry, he confirmed that comments could be made under other
business later in the meeting if desired.  Under other City business, Liz Byrd commented she was
disappointed the resolution was removed from the agenda, and discussed generally repairs that
are needed to the current tennis court due to deterioration.  She noted her concerns with water
run-off and flooding in the park; accessibility of the entire park to residents, including the
ballfield in the northern corner of the park, and future amenities and improvements, and
requested organization of a committee to coordinate plans for park improvements with City staff.
Mayor Kaysen clarified the resolution was withdrawn from the agenda to provide for establishing
a work group involving several interested parties and individuals to discuss projects for the park
with Parks and Recreation staff.  Mayor Kaysen further explained that information presented at
the Finance Committee meeting regarding the grant request (i.e. resolution) being sought
reflected that there was not enough grant funding available to repair the existing tennis court and
the grant request was for landscaping the current tennis court area, and that future development
of 2 new tennis courts in a different area of the park, when funding is available, was discussed.
Ms. Byrd requested a commitment be provided for when new tennis courts will be built and that
communication between interested persons and City staff be provided, and suggested the City
request project funding through the State parks office.

Hailey Ellingham, spoke in opposition to the pending removal of designated trees in
Lions Park due to height concerns affiliated with the air flight path for the airport.  She discussed
positive attributes of the park involving preservation of habitat areas for birds and other wildlife;
trees serving as a sound barrier and a wind break around the lake; wetlands environment; effect

measurements relative to the flight path; whether grand stand structures at Frontier Park violate
