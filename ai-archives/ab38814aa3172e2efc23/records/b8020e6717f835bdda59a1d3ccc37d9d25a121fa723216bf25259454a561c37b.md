Subject: PUDC-24-119 / 3501 Forest Drive / Zone Change
Date:
August 21, 2024

At Public Services Committee on August 20, 2024, there was discussion on the zone change request at
Pershing and Forest. The impetus behind the zone change request was the applicant’s desire for a
larger-than-allowed electronic message center (EMC). There was some discussion on whether or not the
zone change was the correct way to process this request. Public testimony was received expressing
concerns about both the proposed sign and the proposed zoning.

Planning Commission
The Planning Commission was generally sympathetic to the signage request, but due to the potential
impacts of redevelopment on the current neighborhood to the west, recommended denial of the zone
change. Public testimony at the planning commission expressed concerns about both the proposed sign
and the proposed zoning. Some of those commenting were against any increase to signage allowances,
but there were a few who appeared to accept the signage increase, just not the zone change proposal.

Staff Recommendations
Due to the location of the property, current use, and redevelopment options, Staff recommended
approval of the zone change. Should the Governing Body wish to approve an increase to signage
allowances without approving the zone change, Staff recommends the Governing Body direct staff to
craft a text amendment to allow for larger signage for all similarly situated properties.

Background
EMC’s are any portion of a sign that is changed via digital methods, examples include signs like those
found at Applebee’s, Lincolnway Pawn, and Cheyenne Little Player’s Theater. These signs are currently
allowed in residential zones for certain non-residential uses such as schools, churches, and theaters (see
UDC 6.5.4.c.1). However, these are only allowed at 40% of the allowed sign area, and in most residential
zones the allowed sign area for these uses is 48sqft (therefore 19sqft for an EMC).

For a non-residential property owner wanting a larger-than-19sqft EMC in a residential zone, the
following options could allow a larger EMC:  zone change, variance, administrative adjustment, and code
amendment. Attached are a list of some of the pros and cons of the various options.

Comparison of Processes Resulting in an Increase in Signage Allowances

Zone Change
Pros:
•
Recognizes a property is suitable for higher-intensity uses and therefore higher-intensity signage
•
Gives more options for redevelopment
•
It makes it easier to expand a non-residential facility
Cons:
•
Not all sites are suitable for rezoning
•
Sites close to neighborhoods may not cleanly and clearly meet all review criteria
•
Redevelopment near a neighborhood could have a significant impact on the neighborhood

Variance
Pros:
•
Applies to properties on a case-by-case basis
•
Recognizes uniqueness of the property (such as being adjacent to a major street)
•
Does not allow overly-impactful development into (or near to) a residential neighborhood
Cons:
•
Variances are a ‘high bar’ to clear – an applicant must prove:
o Unique property conditions
o True hardship (not just convenience)
o The relief is the minimum necessary
o The relief is in harmony with the intent of the code
•
Potential for consistency issues if only occasionally granted
The best practice in land use law is to change the code if variances are being granted for ‘common
sense’ purposes instead of true variance criteria purposes.

Administrative Adjustment
Pros:
•
Applies to properties on a case-by-case basis
•
Recognizes uniqueness of the property (such as being adjacent to a major street)
•
Does not allow overly-impactful development into (or near to) a residential neighborhood
•
Does not have nearly as high a bar to clear to receive approval (compared to a variance)
•
Allows for ‘common sense’ solutions for circumstances where the proposal is ‘close enough’ to
the requirement
Cons:
•
Limited in scope – this is meant to be minor relief, not a major allowance change
•
Lacks public process
NOTE: for this request, the desired sign area is beyond of the scope of the administrative adjustment.

Code Amendment
Pros:
•
Applies fairly to all properties in the City
•
Allows for a signage increase without the potentially excessive action of a zone change
•
Allows for a signage increase without having to clear the ‘high bar’ set for variances
Cons:
•
Application to the entire City may have unintended consequences
Typically, applicants are not directed to request code amendments so that the code does not become
overly crafted to specific projects and properties.
