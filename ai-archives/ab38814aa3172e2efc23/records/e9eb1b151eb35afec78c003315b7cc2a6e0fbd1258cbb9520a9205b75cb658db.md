MEMO
To:
Honorable Mayor Patrick Collins and Members of the City Council
From:
Lonnie Olson, Planner I
Subject: Resolution for naming the median which contains the Historic Airport Fountain, located to
the east of the intersection of Central Avenue and East 8th Avenue, to be known as Airport
Fountain Park
Date:
April 10, 2023

Background:

The item for consideration is the naming of the median which contains the Historic Airport Fountain as
Airport Fountain Park. The site is a .07 acre +/- parcel located to the east of the intersection of Central
Avenue and East 8th Avenue. The Cheyenne Historic Preservation Board had a landscape study done in
2009 as the fountain was being threatened with demolition. Since the initial landscape study, the
Cheyenne Historic Preservation Board has restored the Historic Airport Fountain over two phases of work
for a total cost of $132,684.08 completed in 2022.

City Naming Policy:

See attached City of Cheyenne Naming and Memorial Policy, Resolution 6139, dated April 26th, 2021.

Threshold: As a guideline but not a limitation, the threshold for naming rights on parks or
buildings would include one or preferably more of the following:

1. Deeding to the City most if not all the land on which the park or building to be
named will be situated,
2. Payment of one-half or more of the capital costs of constructing a park or building
to be named (depending on availability of matching funds or grants),
3. Long-term endowment for the operation, repair and maintenance of the donated
park or building,
4. Provision of significant program costs for facilities that will serve City program and
facility needs.

Procedure:

RESOLUTION NO. __________

ENTITILED:  "A RESOLUTION FOR NAMING THE MEDIAN WHICH CONTAINS
THE HISTORIC AIRPORT FOUNTAIN, LOCATED TO THE EAST OF
THE INTERSECTION OF CENTRAL AVENUE AND EAST 8TH AVENUE,
TO BE KNOWN AS AIRPORT FOUNTAIN PARK."

WHEREAS; the Cheyenne Historic Preservation Board is created by the Cheyenne
Historic Preservation Ordinance, City of Cheyenne Municipal Code, Section 2.64.010, et seq;

WHEREAS; Preserving the historic integrity of Cheyenne, Wyoming is the Cheyenne
Historic Preservation Board’s mission;

WHEREAS; the Historic Cheyenne Airport Fountain was built in 1935 to memorialize the
heritage of aviation in Cheyenne;

WHEREAS; the Historic Cheyenne Airport Fountain was added to the National Register
of Historic Places in 1985;

WHEREAS; the Historic Cheyenne Airport Fountain was neglected for many years and
had its mechanical system turned off for decades;

WHEREAS; the Cheyenne Historic Preservation Board in 2009 initiated a landscape study
on the area surrounding the Historic Airport Fountain due to the threat of demolition;

WHEREAS; the Cheyenne Historic Preservation Board has been awarded grants, received
donations, and held fundraisers to have a complete restoration conducted on the Historic Airport
Fountain for a total cost of $132,684.08 over two phases of work completed in 2022; and,

WHEREAS; the median containing the Historic Airport Fountain does not have a name.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE GOVERNING BODY OF THE CITY
OF CHEYENNE, WYOMING, that the median that contains the Historic Airport Fountain located
east of the intersection of Central Avenue and East 8th Avenue be known as "Airport Fountain
Park" in recognition of the efforts of the Cheyenne Historic Preservation Board in restoring and
preserving the historical integrity of Cheyenne.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that this resolution shall be
effective upon passage and execution by the Mayor and City Clerk.

9:45 am, Apr 05 2023

Naming and Memorial Policy
It is the policy of the City of Cheyenne (City) that the naming of new and renaming of existing
facilities be reserved for exceptional circumstances and that the naming process comply with the
guidelines and procedures set forth in this policy.
A. Purpose:
These policies and procedures are intended to guide a) any individual or community group that is
interested in having a park, building, or major feature named for a significant person, event, or
place, b) any individual, group, or business that is interested in having their significant donation
(park, building, major feature) named, and c) decision makers.
B. Definitions:
1.
Parks. All traditional designed parks, natural open spaces, historic sites, golf courses,
specialized parks (e.g. Botanic Gardens, Skate Park), and trails under the department’s
jurisdiction or management
2.
Buildings. Significant structures that house City functions and programs
3.
Major Features. Major, permanent components of City facilities, (e.g. ball fields,
swimming pools, tennis courts, playgrounds, amphitheaters, fountains, artwork or
physical features lakes). Rooms within Buildings are considered to be Major Features.
4.
Amenities. Smaller furnishings and facilities (e.g. Benches, small fountains, tables, etc.)
Amenities are not formally named. Recognition for donated amenities is covered under
the Gift and Memorial Policy.

permanent identity for a public place. Excessive naming of individual features and
facilities can be confusing to the public.

meeting with the appropriate City Department, petition process and verification, public
meetings held in conjunction with governing body meetings, and resolution approval.

circumstances which tradition and practice have shown to best serve the interests of the
City and assure a worthy and enduring legacy for the City. To this end, the City supports
consideration of naming requests in the following three broad categories:

Gift Policy
It is the policy of City of Cheyenne (City) to actively encourage gifts that reinforce its mission
and core services. Acceptance and recognition of gifts (property, goods or cash) must comply
with the guidelines and procedures set forth in this policy.
A. Purpose:
This policy is intended to help potential donors understand how the City decides whether to
accept a proposed gift and how that donor can be recognized and thanked; to guide decisions of
the City, elected officials, and staff.
The City has benefited from a rich legacy of community generosity. Over the decades Cheyenne
residents and businesses have given gifts of their time, materials, products, and money. Public
and private foundations have invested in the City. A donation can dramatically increase the
quality of a program, park, or building or make the difference during economically lean times.
The City encourages and facilitates public and private gifts, bequests, and such contributions that
enhance and support services, parks, buildings, programs, and goals of equity. The City wants to
assure donors that their gift will be well-maintained and recognized. These policies outline the
criteria for accepting gifts and recognizing donors.
