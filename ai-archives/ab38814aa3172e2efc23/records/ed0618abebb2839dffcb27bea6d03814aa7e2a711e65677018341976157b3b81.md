ORDINANCE NO. ________
ENTITLED: "AN ORDINANCE AMENDING THE OFFICIAL ZONING MAP OF THE
CITY OF CHEYENNE CHANGING THE ZONING CLASSIFICATION
FROM CB COMMUNITY BUSINESS TO P PUBLIC DISTRICT FOR
LAND LOCATED AT LOT 1, CHEYENNE ICE AND EVENTS CENTER,
2ND FILING LOCATED AT 1530 WEST LINCOLNWAY”
BE IT ORDAINED BY THE GOVERNING BODY OF THE CITY OF CHEYENNE,
WYOMING:
Section 1.  That the owner(s) of the property described herein has (have) applied for a
zoning map amendment for said property in accordance with Section 2.2.1, Zoning Map
Amendment, of the Cheyenne Unified Development Code ("UDC").
Section 2.  That on April 1, 2024, the City of Cheyenne Planning Commission conducted
a public hearing, reviewed the proposed zoning map amendment that is the subject of this
ordinance, and recommended approval of said zoning map amendment.
Section 3.  That the Governing Body of the City of Cheyenne finds that the zoning map
amendment that is the subject of this ordinance meets the criteria specified in Section 2.2.1.d of
the UDC.
Section 4.  That, in accordance with Section 2.2.1, Zoning Map Amendment, Section 5.1.2,
Zoning Districts Established, and Section 5.1.3, Official Zoning Map, of the UDC, the
aforementioned application is hereby approved, and the zoning classification of the real property
described as Lot 1, Cheyenne Ice & Events Center, 2nd Filing, Cheyenne, Wyoming, is hereby
changed to P Public District.
Section 5.  That the City Engineer is hereby authorized and directed to alter and amend the
Official Zoning Map as described in Section 5.1.3 of the UDC to comply with the zoning map
amendment as described in Section 4 above when this ordinance takes effect in accordance with
Section 7 below.

publication.

RECOMMENDED MOTION:

Move to approve the rezoning of Lot 1 Cheyenne Ice and Event Center 2nd Filing from CB Community
Business to P Public District, noting that the project meets the review criteria for approval.

BACKGROUND:

This zone change application proposes changing the zoning from CB Community Business to P Public District
for roughly 6.50 acres of land. The land is currently occupied by the City-owned Cheyenne Ice and Events
Center.

SURROUNDING LAND USE AND ZONING:

ANALYSIS OF APPLICATION CONFORMANCE WITH REVIEW CRITERIA:

Should the Planning Commission wish to recommend approval of the Zoning Map Amendment, the
Commission must make the following findings required in UDC 2.2.1.d. A detailed analysis is below:

1) The application is in accordance with the development policies and physical patterns
identified in the Comprehensive Plan.  (UDC 2.2.1.d.1)

PlanCheyenne encourages growth within the Urban Service Boundary and the maximization of
existing infrastructure. This property is within the Urban Service Boundary, in an area with
existing infrastructure.

The Future Land Use Designation for this property and most surrounding properties is Mixed
Use Commercial. Surrounding the area is also the Mixed Use Residential designation. These
designations encourage a mix of residential and commercial uses with associated zone districts.
According to comprehensive plan the optimal City zone for the subject property’s future land
use designation is Mixed-use Business, but a variety of commercial or residential zones may be

PlanCheyenne would suggest a commercial or residential zoning for the property, public uses
such as parks, schools, and recreation facilities can be appropriate in nearly any future land use
designation, serving residents and businesses either nearby or regionally. It is hard to predict
where public uses will be needed or where such public-use development is feasible. Although
there is a ‘public’ designation on the future land use map, it is largely only used for existing
public (or quasi-public) facilities.

PlanCheyenne encourages the measured growth of public facilities to keep up with the needs
and demands of a growing community. Public investment is encouraged along the west
Lincolnway corridor to encourage private investment. The creation of public facilities is one form
of public investment that can assist in this objective. Quality of life amenities (including
recreation-center-like facilities) are encouraged to be competitive with front range facilities.

The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 1.

2) The application is compatible with nearby property, specifically considering existing zoning,
the Comprehensive Plan, and any other official plans and policies created under the guidance
of that plan for these areas; (UDC 2.2.1.d.2)

The requested zone is P Public District.  The property is currently zoned CB Community Business.
The requested zone of P is specifically intended to allow for public recreational development
and to match the public zoning to the west.

The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 2.

3) The suitability of the subject property for development according to the standards and uses of
the existing zoning district, compared to the suitability of the subject property for
development according to the standards and uses of the proposed zoning district (UDC
2.2.1.d.3)

The P Public District zone, as put forward in the UDC, is primarily for public sites, buildings and
uses where activities conducted provide service to the public, including educational facilities,
recreational areas, and community service facilities.  These uses all apply to the event center
making the zone change to P appropriate.

The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 3.

4) The ability of the subject property and surrounding vicinity to be developed according to the
Subdivision Standards in Article 4 considering the proposed zoning (UDC 2.2.1.d.4)

The zone change is occurring in a pre-existing neighborhood. No changes to the street network
are proposed.

Accesses will not be changed with the zone change.

Infrastructure (streets and utilities) exist in the area.

The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 4.

future development or additions within this zone.

No reviewing agencies provided recommendations regarding the proposed zone change.

City staff is satisfied that this proposed zone change would not adversely affect the public
health, safety, or general welfare.

The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 8.
