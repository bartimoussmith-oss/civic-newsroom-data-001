RECORD OF PROCEEDINGS FOR THE GOVERNING BODY
OF THE CITY OF CHEYENNE
Office of City Clerk
March 22, 2010
The Governing Body of the City of Cheyenne met in regular session on this date beginning
at 6:00 p.m. in Council Chambers.  Present were: MAYOR - Richard L. Kaysen; COUNCIL
MEMBERS: Ms. Ash, Mr. Brown, Ms. Broyles, Mr. Collins, Mr. Spiker and Mr. Valdez.  Absent:
Ms. Case, Mr. Pierson and Dr. Rinne.  Also present: Carol Intlekofer, City Clerk; Barb Dorr, City
Treasurer, and Dan White, City Attorney.  Mayor Kaysen recognized members of Boy Scout Troop
216 who led the pledge of allegiance.
Ms. Broyles recognized Lisa Scholz, Executive Director, Cheyenne-Laramie County
Methamphetamine Initiative and expressed appreciation to the Meth Initiative Committee members.
She provided a brief history of the establishment of the meth initiative, how the program has
benefitted the community through the Meth Resource Center and specifically noted Ms. Scholz’
efforts in educating the public on the dangers of using methamphetamine. Ms. Broyles advised the
meth initiative program will continue on a voluntary basis by interested members of the community.
Consent Agenda. (All agenda items listed with the designation of [CA] are considered to be
routine items by the governing body and will be enacted by one motion.  There will be no separate
discussion on these items unless a member of the governing body so requests and support by two
other members is received.  Any item removed from the Consent Agenda will be considered in its
normal sequence on the agenda.)  Upon request by Ms. Ash, and pursuant to support received by Mr.
Collins and Mr. Spiker, agenda item #34(a) was removed from the Consent Agenda. Mr. Collins
moved to approve remaining Consent Agenda items, seconded by Mr. Brown. Motion carried.
Voting “yes” – all members of the governing body present.
[CA]
Minutes from Regular Meeting of the Governing Body on March 8, 2010.  Motion (per
Consent Agenda) carried.
[CA]
Minutes from Vouchers Meeting of the Governing Body on March 15, 2010.  Motion (per
Consent Agenda) carried.
Mr. Brown moved to approve payment of vouchers as presented, seconded by Mr. Spiker.
Upon inquiry, City Clerk Carol Intlekofer explained several business license fee refunds were
included in the voucher listing involving overpayments.  Upon further inquiry, City Treasurer Barb
Dorr, Parks and Recreation Asst. Director Todd Feezer and Engineering Services Director Nathan
Beauheim provided information available for a voucher payable to BOPU ($2,140.88) for water tap
fees at the David Romero Youth Activity Center. Mr. Spiker moved to remove the BOPU voucher
for payment until further clarification could be provided, seconded by Mr. Collins.  Motion carried.
Voting “yes” - all members of the governing body present.  Upon inquiry regarding a voucher
payable to Meridan Fire & Security ($805), Fire Chief Guy Cameron advised services involved
repair of the fire system panel located at the Fire Training Facility.  Motion to approve remaining
vouchers carried.  Voting “yes” – all members of the governing body present.

“A Resolution authorizing the Mayor to offer retirement financial incentives to members of
the bargaining unit of Cheyenne Firefighters Local Union No. 279, International Association of
Firefighters.”  Jon Narva,  President, Firefighters Local Union No. 279, and firefighters Bill Hinz,
and T. J. Haws, spoke in favor of the resolution, advising the proposed financial concessions would
save the city money, assist with reducing potential furlough days and reductions in work force, and
reflect support for other City non-union employees. Mr. Brown advised there was no
recommendation from the Finance Committee.  There being no motion offered to adopt, the
resolution died due to lack of a motion.  Under other City business it was questioned whether there
was a connection between the retirement financial incentives and recent re-negotiation involving
the Firefighters contract.  Upon inquiry, Mr. Narva advised the funding for the financial incentives
was contingent upon City Council approval and was not related to re-negotiation of certain areas of
the Union contract with the City. Upon inquiry regarding whether the City would incur any savings
had the resolution been approved, Fire Chief Guy Cameron reviewed the voluntary Union contract
re-negotiation process initiated by City administration in light of the economy and reduced revenues
the City would be receiving from the state level, and components of topics involved in discussions
relative to cost savings (i.e. manpower levels, elimination of City’s contribution to deferred
compensation program and reduction of City’s contribution to pension, uniform allowance
reduction, medical examination costs, etc.).  He stated calculations involving the resolution and
retirement financial incentives would result in minimal savings to the City.  Ms. Broyles clarified,
pursuant to a previous comment she had made during discussions on potential employee furlough
days, that if any furlough days were necessary as a cost savings measure that she would take a
proportionate cut in compensation relative to the reduced pay employees would receive.
