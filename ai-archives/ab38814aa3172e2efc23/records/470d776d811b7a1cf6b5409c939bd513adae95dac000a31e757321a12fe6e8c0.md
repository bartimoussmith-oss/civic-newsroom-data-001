paragraph B. pertaining to deleting and re-creating Section 102.4.1 wording and which code, the
International Building Code (IBC) or International Fire Code (IFC), would prevail if a conflict
occurred between the two codes.  Bruce Wilson, Chief Building Official, verified the
International Energy Conservation Code would not be excluded in the proposed change as it is
included in the IBC, and that intent of the wording is to clear up any conflict with fire and
building codes.  Upon inquiry, Dan White, City Attorney, explained they wanted to make sure
that the fire code would stand alone as a maintenance code; whereas the building code applies
to new construction, remodels and alterations.  He stated the change will ensure that the IBC
would only supercede if there was a conflict, and, if there was a conflict, he advised that,
generally speaking, then the fire code official, building code official and the property owner
would discuss the matter to work out any areas involved in the conflict.  Upon further inquiry,
Mr. White advised the proposed wording does represent a City policy change.  Further discussion
followed on interaction between fire and building codes.  Upon inquiry, Chief Jim Martin, Fire
and Rescue Department, advised the proposed change was drawn up by the Fire Prevention
Division and that, in the past, if there was a conflict between the codes staff attempted to work
out the differences.  Mr. Shanor moved to strike paragraph B. listed under Section 2., 15.08.020,
Amendments, so that the language from the IBC 2012 code (i.e. Section 102.4.1) would remain

a change for a small group of people (i.e. small businesses); support for providing more

Code (UDC) requirements and financial effect on small businesses; applying for variances;
allowing business owners to have more of a decision; Cheyenne as an urban landscape
environment and implementing more conservation, irrigation and alternative measures to reduce
water run-off; concerns with treating new (big) development the same as small business, multi-
use or non-residential properties; uniform application of the UDC; difficulty of growing trees in
Cheyenne’s environment and safely maintaining larger trees; suggestion that the term
