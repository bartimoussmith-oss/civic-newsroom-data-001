INTRODUCTION
The impetus of the Parsley Boulevard Corridor Study is in response to Wyoming Department of
Transportation’s (WYDOT) need to replace the bridge over Interstate 80 that has been hit by oversized
loads on Interstate 80. The bridge has been hit and repaired numerous times over the last several years.
WYDOT has programed the re-construction of the bridge for the 2022 construction season. As the
Interstate 80 crossing is approximately halfway from the Ames Avenue intersection to the north and
College Drive on the south. The corridor study is needed to create a comprehensive plan to optimize safety
growth, and fiscal responsibility.
The goal of the study is three fold, first to understand the community, businesses and neighborhoods
vision for the roadway, second to improve roadway and intersection safety and function that encourages
and accommodates all forms of transportation (i.e., industrial traffic, motor vehicles, bicycles, and
pedestrians), third to guide the design development for the new WYDOT Interstate 80 bridge structure to
be replaced in fiscal year 2022.
Study Area
Parsley Boulevard Corridor:
1. Ames Avenue to College Drive and
College Drive to Wallick Road.
Key Intersections:
2. 4th Street and Parsley Boulevard.
3. 3rd Street and Parsley Boulevard.
4. Jefferson Road and Parsley
Boulevard.
5. Pinto Lane and Parsley Boulevard.
6. College Drive and Parsley Boulevard.
The study includes 35% design of Parsley
Boulevard from Ames Avenue to College
Drive. A conceptual alignment of Parsley
Boulevard is included from College Drive to
West Wallick Road.

REVIEW OF EXISTING INFORMATION
This section of the plan provides a summary of the existing roadway and planning area.
History
Based on the Cheyenne-Laramie County Cooperative Geographic Information System (GIS) database
search/interactive mapping site, the first recorded plat of Parsley Boulevard is the Interior Heights addition
in 1954 (Figure 2-1). The plat recorded the roadway as Branch County Road No.14.  A 1949 plat by the
Union Pacific Railroad of a portion of SE ¼ and S ½ of NE ¼ Section 1, T13N, R67W, 6th P.M. calls out a
county road on the section line between Section 1, T13N, R67W and Section 6, T13N, R66W. However, there
is no dimension called out for the width of the right-of-way. This same plat depicts a “Road” which would
appear to be the portion of current Parsley Boulevard from Pacific Avenue to Ames Avenue (Figure 2-2).
The Final Plat of David Romero South Cheyenne Community Park which was recorded in 2008 depicts
Parsley Boulevard as 80’ right-of-way from approximately Pacific Avenue to Ames Avenue and a 100’ right-
of-way centered on the section line common to Section 1 T13N, R67W and Section 6, T13N, R66W. There is
also a call out on this plat of 120’ Right-of-Way (Figure 2-4). There appears to be a discrepancy between
the various plats of the width of Parsley Boulevard right-of-way from Unicover Drive to Pacific Avenue
(north). This should be investigated further as part of the improvement design.
Historical Review
The Parsley Boulevard Corridor plan area is not known to be a part of any historic districts at the present
time.  Additionally, the Wyoming State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) website was reviewed for all the
National Register listings in the area of the study and none were found.
Please note that if federal funds are used on any future projects or if a federal agency is part of the
planning and implementation, a Section 106 Study will be required to determine potential impacts to any
historic properties.  Properties in the area of any construction impacts will be identified and evaluated
based on the Secretary of Interior’s Standards and Guidelines for identification.  Several determinations can
be made in the evaluation including the following:

No historic properties affected,

Historic property adversely affected,

Historic property not adversely affected.

Drainage
Most all of Parsley Boulevard is within the Clear Creek drainage basin. Clear Creek crosses Parsley Boulevard
just south of Pacific Avenue adjacent the Dave Romero Park. The FEMA 100-year Floodplain overtops
Parsley Boulevard at approximately one-foot depth. Refer to Figure 2-14. The 1988 Drainage Master Plan,
by CH2MHill called for (3) 10’ x 6’ reinforced concrete box culverts to be installed at the Clear Creek
crossing.
Future improvements to Parsley Boulevard including an urban street section will require significant
drainage analysis and design of storm water conveyance systems to collect and convey storm water to
existing drainage facilities.

Key-Planning Considerations
This phase of the project provides a framework for the future land development and corridor vision of
the various stakeholders.  The Parsley Boulevard Corridor from Ames Avenue to College Drive has been
fully developed over the years with urban density on the east side of the corridor and light industrial on
the west. The Parsley Boulevard Corridor from College Drive to Wallick Road is currently undeveloped.
The entire corridor from Ames Avenue to College Drive is within the city limits with the exception of the
tank farm property on the west side from West Allison to College Drive, therefore improvements to the
corridor will be required it to be constructed to the City of Cheyenne Unified Development Code
Standards including number of lanes, curb & gutter, parking, pedestrian and bicycle facilities. Planning
considerations include:

Provide the Bridge over I-80, with pedestrian and bicycle recommendations;

Provide a safe, accessible and continuous pedestrian connection along the entire corridor;

Provide street lighting at intersections and non-motorized crossings where appropriate;

Provide for expanding transit stops along the corridor;

Provide bike lanes as recommended by the Cheyenne Area On-Street Bicycle Plan and Greenway
Plan Update by Alta Planning + Design in 2012 (Group, 2012).

Review options to expand the Greenway for connectivity to schools and existing greenway
components.
Traffic Study
A Traffic study was performed by Kimley-Horn is to evaluate traffic safety, transportation needs, and
provide recommendations for managing vehicular traffic and promoting a multi-modal transportation
approach along Parsley Boulevard in Cheyenne, Wyoming.
The traffic analysis includes the evaluation of the existing and long term 2040 horizon traffic for the
purposes of this study. In addition to applying a standardized traffic volume growth rate, trip
generation, trip distribution, and project traffic assignment for an adjacent 700-unit residential
development proposed at the northeast corner of Parsley Boulevard and College Drive was also
included within the 2040 total traffic volumes.
Conclusions and Recommendations
Although not found to be needed to provide additional traffic capacity, it is recommended that Parsley
Boulevard be improved to be a three-lane roadway between College Drive and Ames Avenue to provide
left-turn lanes at all major intersections. These left turn lanes will improve safety and facilitate efficient
traffic flow. Likewise, to provide a complete street to serve other modes of travel, it is recommended
that the roadway be constructed with adequate width so that bicycle lanes can be designated on both
sides of Parsley Boulevard. Curb and gutter is recommended on both sides of Parsley Boulevard.

Parsley Boulevard Corridor Study

The Cheyenne Metropolitan Planning Organization (the Cheyenne area long-range transportation
planning agency) in coordination with the City, County and WYDOT have contracted AVI, a
Cheyenne engineering and planning firm to perform a planning study of the Parsley Boulevard
corridor between College Drive and Ames Avenue/Deming Drive.
The roadway condition of Parsley Boulevard is generally acceptable, but it is rural in character and
does not accommodate future traffic volumes or have facilities which provide for other modes of
travel or access per the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA).  The main obstacle to the conversion
of Parsley to an urban street is the narrow bridge structure that spans I-80 and the road does not
have sufficient width for sidewalks, bike lanes, or large vehicles. The primary objectives of the
project are to develop a plan for the corridor which addresses the requirements and vision which
are defined and developed by the current and future users, surrounding neighborhood residents,
property owners, and businesses.   The corridor will need to accommodate future traffic volume,
as well as, multimodal vehicles types (i.e. bus, car, industrial, semi-truck and trailer, bike,
pedestrian, etc.).
There is no funding identified at the present time to reconstruct this corridor, however the bridge
over I-80 is slated to be replaced by the Wyoming Department of Transportation in fiscal year
2022.
