Cover Letter - Executive Summary
Dear Governing Body of the Cheyenne City Council,
My name is Chad Willett. I am currently the Manager
of Get Bent, LLC, the applicant and legal property
owner at 707 W. Lincolnway. I am excited to submit
this application for consideration of award for the
newest available Cheyenne Retail Liquor License.
Located at the corner of Lincolnway and Bent, and
currently occupied by West Edge Collective, this 6,000
sq. ft. creative space was built in 2018 on a lot that
had sat vacant for decades. This Commercial Zoned
building utilizes reclaimed metal structure from Wyoming train bridges, flooring from early 1900’s boxcars,
and shipping containers hand-picked from a Denver shipping yard. Though it’s served as home base for
large street concerts (such as the Sugar Hill Gang and Coolio/Rob Base) and several community-hosted
events, this space is often seen by its visitors as underutilized and under-developed.
In response to the availability of the full retail liquor license (Bar + Package Liquor Store Combo), West
Edge Collective, Micro Pop-Up Concepts, and private investors propose to partner to develop a new legal
entity to operate the license, as well as further develop the space. Both the landlord and tenant would
invest significant dollars to develop the property and concept.
Together, as a joint effort, we are proud to introduce our vision for The Rciilspur. Sure to draw attention and
business with its unique design and premise, The Raiispur will specialize in cocktails, coffee and cuisine, and
its design will enhance both the industrial and railroad heritage of the surrounding area’s buildings. We
anticipate being open to the public before the end of 2022.
In addition to its design, what makes this proposed downtown destination particularly unique is its
offerings. The Ralispur will house a morning coffee bistro, offer hand-crafted cuisine throughout the day
and into the evening, serve specialty drinks, and house a small boutique package liquor store. The Railspur
will be the first facility of its kind in Cheyenne to transform from a family- and business-friendly
establishment during the day to a high-energy 21+ bar after 9 p.m.
You will find that our plan does the following for Downtown Cheyenne:

Property History
This property is a part of the original “West Edge” of
Cheyenne. Prior to expansion of the city, this lot was an
important railroad spur for the Military Department to
access Fort D.A. Russell (now F.E. Warren AFB).
As depicted in this 1912 Sanborn Fire Insurance Map, the
lot was flanked by multiple rail spurs. Large lumber
warehouses on the property were utilized by the Grier
Lumber Co. throughout the early 1900s.
In 2015, this property was a lay-down lot for a local
mechanical contractor; a property seen as blighted and
underutilized. Notice the area to the southwest for the
city-owned lot known as the Pumphouse.
Figure 1
- 1880s rendering
Figure 2- 1886 Sanborn Fire Insurance Map
Home to various businesses and structures throughout its
150 year+ history, there has always been a connection to
the railroad. As seen in this 1886 Sanborn Fire Insurance
Map, the property started with many dwellings.
Figure 3- 1912 Sanborn Fire Insurance Map
Figure 4
- Map from Reed Avenue Roil Corridor Master Plan
Fast-forward 100 years, and the property is now at the
intersection of the southern point of the Reed Avenue
Rail Corridor, the westernmost point of 15th Street, and a
key connection to future Greenway access.
Figure 5 -2015 Satellite View from Laromie County GIS
Figure 6- 2021 Satellite View from Laramie County GIS
In 2021, notice the considerable transformation.
Pumphouse Park has been constructed, this property has
been partially developed, and much (not all) of the blight
has been remediated.
