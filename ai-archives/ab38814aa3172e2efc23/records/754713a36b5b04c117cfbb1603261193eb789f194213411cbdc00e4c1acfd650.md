the past year as well as the new federal health care act have contributed to the 12.6% coverage
cost increase.  He discussed keeping health care premiums and deductible amounts affordable for
employees; different coverage options available under the plan, and the effect of the federal
Affordable Care Act (ACA) on increased provider costs.  Steve Goldstone, WINhealth Partners,
advised WINhealth worked with actuaries and industry experts to project estimated increases due
to the impact of the ACA, noting any time benefits expand there is some cost involved.  Upon
query, Mr. Wiederspahn stated he was unaware of whether City employees were eligible to
participate in the State's health insurance program.  Discussion followed and included the
following topics:  Amount of high medical claims incurred within the past year; degree of staff
due diligence conducted in reviewing and evaluating a health plan; lack of seeking proposals
from other providers; continuing costs of health care coverage and budgetary concerns;
percentage of premium cost paid by the City vs. employees; deductible/co-pay amounts;
differences between shelter, silver and gold plans; stress incurred by employees within the past
year due to budgetary reductions; employee wellness (coaching) program and affiliated costs, and
continuing need to review how to curtail future cost increases.  Throughout discussion, Mr.
Wiedersphahn and Mr. Goldstone provided information available in response to questions.  Barb
Dorr, City Treasurer, advised the estimated cost of implementing a wellness/coaching program
for employees last year was $50,000, at which time the City was offered to be included in a pilot
program through WINhealth.  She estimated the current cost to implement a program would be
double that amount.  Upon further inquiry, Mr. Goldstone clarified that the catastrophic claims
the City had last year would not have been impacted by having a wellness program. Mr.
Wiederspahn verified the Committee did not research plans offered by other health insurance
companies during the plan review/renewal process, and stated catastrophic illnesses and
accidents that occurred last year  has driven up premiums which could also affect coverage costs
by other providers.  He discussed upcoming employee health education sessions; nurse hotline
assistance program currently in place, and other health awareness and training for employees to
hopefully assist in reducing coverage costs.  Upon inquiry concerning the percentage increase of
catastrophic claims incurred in the past year, Mr. Wiederspahn indicated he did not have that
information available at the meeting and would provide it.  Comments followed on continuing
research regarding a wellness program.  Motion carried. Voting "yes" – all members of the
governing body with the exception of Ms. Case voting "no".  (#5614)
