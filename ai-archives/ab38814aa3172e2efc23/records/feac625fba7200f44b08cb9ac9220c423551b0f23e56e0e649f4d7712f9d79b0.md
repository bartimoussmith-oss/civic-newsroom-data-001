SUBJECT:
Professional Service Agreement between the City of Cheyenne and Ayres
Associates, for the design of 19th Street Drainage and Intersection Improvements
Project.

Please find the attached Professional Service Agreement between the City and Ayres Associates
for engineering services related to the design of the 19th Street Drainage and Intersection
Improvements Project. The design will entail a new hydraulic structure in Crow Creek along 19th
Street; 19th Street re-alignment; reconfiguration of the intersections of Missile Drive and 19th
Street, and Missile Drive and Old Happy Jack Road.

Engineering Staff evaluates fee proposals for engineering services based on time and material
estimates for the specific project.  Those costs are readily tracked and verified during design
operations and the City only pays for the actual time expended. We believe this agreement will
provide the required services to complete the design portion of the project.

The fees include services for project management, intersection design, topographical surveying,
geotechnical investigations, utility investigation, structural design, sanitary sewer design, and plan
creation. Funding for this project will come from the 2019 1% Specific Purpose Tax Fund (5th
Penny) and a FEMA grant. The total professional services will be a cost not-to-exceed $712,446.
We respectfully request your approval of this professional services agreement.

CITY ENGINEER’S OFFICE

Cheyenne, Wyoming 82001

A.1 Overview

Consultant services for the 19th Street Drainage and Intersection Improvements Project are the design and
development of a complete set of construction documents, including plans, specifications, and estimates.
Any innovative solutions that are cost effective and meet project goals will be incorporated during project
scoping negotiations and included in the design.  Work required for this project will include project
management; roadway and intersection design; bridge/structural design; hydraulic analysis; geotechnical
investigation; storm water and storm sewer design; right-of-way surveying; and utility location,
coordination, and design.
The project requires a professional design team that includes a Civil Engineer registered in the State of
Wyoming with a minimum of 10 years of project design experience in one or more of the following
disciplines: bridge design; urban roadway design; hydraulic analysis; with the ability to provide in-house
or capacity to sub-consult for survey services, geotechnical investigation and those disciplines required for
the project.

A.2 Project Description

A.2.a Overview

The project is located along 19th Street between Missile Drive and Ames Avenue and extends west of
Missile Drive onto Old Happy Jack Road.
The project will include the design of the intersections of 19th Street and Missile Drive and Old Happy Jack
Road/Washington Avenue and Missile Drive. Four different intersection configurations were presented in
the Metropolitan Planning Organization’s (MPO) Missile Drive Corridor Plan completed in 2010.
Alternative three was selected as the preferred option. This document can be found at:
 https://www.plancheyenne.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/MissileDriveFinalReport_100510_acd.pdf
The use of a roundabout was not evaluated in the 2010 corridor study. The MPO recently evaluated a
merged double roundabout (peanut roundabout) at the intersection of 19th Street, Missile Drive, and Old
Happy Jack Road. This evaluation was presented in the Missile Drive Corridor Concept Plan and is included
with this scope of work. A single roundabout and merged double roundabout will be evaluated as potential
alternatives to the selected intersection configuration in the 2010 study. This will require evaluating future
land uses in the West Edge District of Cheyenne for traffic volume projections and impacts to the 100-year
FEMA regulated Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA). If the floodway is encroached on, a Conditional
Letter of Map Revision and Letter of Map Revision will be required demonstrating a no-rise condition and
documenting changes to the flood plain mapping.
The preferred intersection configuration should be evaluated and designed using traffic projections included
in the Connect 2045 Cheyenne Area Transportation Master Plan.
The project will also include the design of a structure at Crow Creek. Hydraulic analyses will be performed
to determine what type of channel and structure clearance is needed to eliminate overtopping at 19th Street.
Downstream impacts at Lincolnway and Ames Avenue Underpass will need to be evaluated in the analysis
as well. Precast girders, arch structures, and box culverts should be evaluated in the design in order to

A.2.b Project History

Resolution 5933, which was passed in 2018, identified this as a priority project. The intersection has
operational and safety issues due to a severely skewed alignment and also lacks a pedestrian corridor
connection. The current culverts in Crow Creek at 19th Street have inadequate conveyance capacity which
leads to overtopping and storm water inundation in parts of the downtown area during significant storm
events.
 A.2.c Existing Facilities

19th Street, 20th Street, Missile Drive, and Dey Avenue and are classified as minor arterial roadways. Old
Happy Jack is classified as a minor collector. The remaining streets in the area are classified as local
streets.  19th and 20th Streets have two lanes and are configured as a one-way couplet, with a short section
of 19th Street being two-way east of the intersection. Missile Drive has four through lanes, a center
median south of the intersection, an eastbound center left turn lane, and westbound right slip lane north of
the intersection. Old Happy Jack has two through lanes and an auxiliary southbound right turn lane at the
intersection.
An asphalt Greenway path connects to the south side of 19th Street and then leads west to the intersection
and east to a sidewalk. The Greenway terminates at the intersection.
Crow Creek flows through five 14’ X 6’ corrugated metal pipe arch structures under 19th Street. The
structures were installed in 1965. All pipes exhibit moderate corrosion and distortion throughout. The
slope paving at the inlet side has failed at multiple locations and the channel is sedimented which directs
regular creek flows through only one of the five pipe arches.
