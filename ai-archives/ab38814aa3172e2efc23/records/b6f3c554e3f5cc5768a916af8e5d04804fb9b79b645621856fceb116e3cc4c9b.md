BE IT ORDAINED BY THE GOVERNING BODY OF THE CITY OF CHEYENNE, WYOMING:
Section 1.
That the City Right-of-Way (ROW) property described as a 4,784 square foot
portion of the east leg of the Goodnight Trail Roundabout, Sweetgrass, 1st Filing, City of Cheyenne,
Laramie County, Wyoming, is hereby vacated as shown on the attached Vacation Map.
Section 2.
The vacation of the 4,784-square feet of undeveloped ROW is in the best interest
of the City of Cheyenne and its residents.
Section 3.
That said vacated right-of-way shall revert to the adjacent property owners pursuant
to Wyoming State Statutes.
Section 4.
The said vacated land shall have no easements, reservations or restrictions of record
unless shown on the attached Vacation Map.
Section 5.
The City Clerk is directed to record this ordinance, together with the Vacation Map,
with the County Clerk and Ex-Officio Register of Deeds for Laramie County, Wyoming.
Section 6.
That the City Engineer’s office is directed to change the Official City Map to reflect
this vacation and the Official Zoning Map designating the vacation request for the properties as indicated
as zone Sweetgrass PUD (Ordinance 4286 and subsequent amendments).

Sections, paragraphs, sentences, clauses and phrases of this ordinance are

paragraphs and sections.
Section 8.
That this ordinance shall be in full force and effect upon its publication.

The proposed vacation is minor and does not substantively change the public-private interface.
The right-of-way was already included as part of the development plan on the adjacent parcel
(as the right-of-way landscaping and access are typically found on site plans for development at
any location in the City). Any future development on the parcel would be required to address
this area (whether private property or public right-of-way).

Vacation of the property complies with this criterion.

3) The vacation reflects sound planning and urban design principles with respect to existing
potential future development in the vicinity, including consideration of current and future
streets, open spaces, blocks and lots, and public utilities or facilities (UDC 2.1.4.d.3).

The proposed vacation does not eliminate any publicly dedicated connections and all public
travel will be retained in the remaining right-of-way. The area to be vacated has utilities, but a
utility easement is proposed to be included with the vacation.

Vacation of the property complies with this criterion.

4) The application meets the public interest, and is not otherwise prohibited by other city, state,
or federal laws (UDC 2.1.4.d.4).

The portion of the right-of-way proposed to be vacated only services one owner (who has
petitioned for the vacation). All north-south public travel is maintained in the remaining right-of-
way.  Allowing the vacation would allow the landowner greater flexibility in the future and
would relieve the City of some maintenance.

Vacation of the property complies with this criterion.

5) The review and recommendations of other reviewing bodies (UDC 2.1.4.d.5).

The Metropolitan Planning Organization recommended that the entire eastern splinter island be
within right-of-way. However, the proposed splinter island already extends into private
property, and the entirety of the splinter island west of (and including) the public sidewalk will
still be in the right-of-way.

Vacation of the property complies with this criterion.
