Under other business, Bart Stolp referenced comments he had made during an October 2014
City Council meeting concerning traffic, speed limits and his request for safety improvements on
Van Buren Avenue, near Dildine Elementary School.  He noted, pursuant to discussions with the
City Engineer’s office and the school district, plans are in progress to implement speed limit
reductions in the school zone but he had been advised that the rest of Van Buren (outside of the
school zone) would remain a 30 m.p.h. area as it is designated as a collector street.  Mr. Stolp
discussed potential options to reduce the street’s speed limit, outside of the school zone area, to 25
m.p.h.  Upon inquiry, Nathan Beauheim, Acting City Engineer, explained state statutory
requirements that must be followed to change the speed limit from 30 to 25 m.p.h.; advised what
area constitutes the school zone and the City’s signage policy in school zones/crosswalks, and
provided information on the Neighborhood Traffic Management Program (NTMP) adopted, as
updated, by resolution earlier in the meeting.  Comments followed on the City and school district
committee that meets to review traffic issues near schools; composition of committee members; date
of the next committee meeting, and procedural elements of the revised NTMP that need to be
coordinated and possibly adjusted due to different neighborhood specifics and program requests.
There being no further business to come before the governing body, the meeting was
officially adjourned at 7:07 p.m.
