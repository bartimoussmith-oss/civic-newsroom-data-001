28. PUBLIC HEARING regarding the adoption of the 2025 Laramie
County Land Use Regulations.
25 - 348
Adoption of 2025 Laramie County Land Use
Regulations
Adoption of 2025 Laramie County Land Use
Regulations, Revised
Attachments:
Chairman Malm announced there would be one Public Hearing for items 28, 29, and
30. He called on Justin Arnold, Planning, to present the staff report.
Arnold stated he wanted to correct misinformation that the regulations were a land
grab. He informed that the process of updating the regulations began two and a half
years ago and explained his professional background and qualifications working on
land use regulations for 15 years. He said the proposed changes deregulate Home
Occupations and streamline the approval process for businesses, saving thousands
of dollars for startups and expanding enterprises. He said the regulations were written
in-house by an experienced planner with decades of experience in writing regulations.
Arnold noted 75,000 acres were deregulated for home occupations. He detailed what
was changed to make the regulations more effective and to spur economic and
business growth. He said the good neighbor policy under the proposed regulations
allows home occupations, including light industrial. Existing businesses would be
grandfathered in. The county would enforce nuisance statutes.
He said under current regulations, RV living was restricted. The proposed regulations
would allow RV living on a person’s property provided sanitation and health
requirements were met. A property owner would also be allowed to put in a secondary
residence, or accessory dwelling, with a building permit. He outlined other measures
that he said would save property owners time and money.
Arnold also noted that persons whose applications are denied would have seven days
in which to request the board reconsider its decision. He said District Court is the only
option under current regulations.
Arnold said fairness and flexibility were the hallmarks of the proposed regulations.
Chairman Malm opened the meeting for PUBLIC COMMENT.
Some 37 residents representing various home occupations offered comments
opposing the proposed regulations. The majority stated that they did not want
government telling them what to do with their property. Resident Julie Formby said a
county HOA was not needed, and the regulations represented government overreach.
Resident Joseph Kibler said that the few complaints the county received about home
businesses indicated the issue was not a problem requiring enforcement.
