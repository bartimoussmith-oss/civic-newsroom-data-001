October 4, 2023

Background:

The purpose of this MOU is for the city to accept ownership of Lot 11, Block 3, Saddle Ridge 16th
Filing and Tracts 1, 2 and 3 of Saddle Ridge 18th Filing and to outline the design, construction, and
maintenance responsibilities for these areas.
The Developer, WJE LLC., would like to donate Lot 11, Block 2 of the Saddle Ridge 16th Filing
along with Tracts 1,2, and 3 of Saddle Ridge 18th Filing. The Developer has created an HOA, Saddle Ridge
Master Homeowners Association, Inc. to provide all maintenance of these properties and the city will
maintain the Greenway segments as set forth in the MOU.  If the city accepts the donations, the
Developer will construct the final segment of Greenway coming out of this Development on to Pershing
Blvd and connecting into the existing Greenway located on the west side of Christensen Road. (See
Exhibit A).  This represents approximately 1,875 feet of 10-foot width concrete path with an estimated
total cost of $146,000.00.  The city will then reimburse the Developer all Community Facility Fees
collected from both the 16th and 18th Filings. This represents 189 lots at $400.00 per dwelling unit or a
total of $75,600.
       The Developer has already completed the Greenway segments from Three Hearts Trail, south to
Pershing and then the segment along E. Pershing Blvd east to the existing Greenway along Christensen.
It is understood that this agreement for acceptance of land is made based on historical agreements
made for previous filings of the Saddle Ridge development in which previous staff accepted or were
possibly expected by Council to accept these parcels.  Over the years, the donations from the whole of
Saddle Ridge, (approximately 353 acres in total size) to include the proposed donations of Tracts 1,2 and
3, equal 38.91 acres of land.  By comparison, the donations from the whole of The Pointe,
(approximately 304 acres in total size) equal 20.57 acres of land.  City Staff has concerns regarding
future obligations for accepting these donations which include the following:
1. It is common in the City of Cheyenne to see homeowners’ associations go defunct, resulting in
detention ponds being purchased at tax sales and new owners wanting to develop these (South
Park Estates) or residents expecting the city to purchase and maintain detention ponds that are
no longer maintained by an HOA that has gone defunct. (Highland Village)

2. It is the staff’s opinion that the city should not accept donations of local detention ponds as
these are built to detain runoff for a particular development and not the region.  Local detention
ponds should be owned and maintained by Developers or their HOA’s and easements should be
placed on these so that these properties are not purchased in the future at tax sales with
purchasers that are determined to develop them. (South Park Estates).  We will be accepting
donations of two local detention areas in this MOU.
3. The Greater Cheyenne Greenway is a regional transportation alternative and recreational
system that is intended to safely connect neighborhoods to each other and to businesses,
schools, and parks. It is appropriate for the Greenway to run by or through a residential
development to connect it to other residential developments, parks, schools, and businesses but
not to roll in and around the development.  This, in staff opinion, is a benefit to the residents of
Saddle Ridge specifically and not so for the whole of the city.  For comparison’s sake, The Pointe,
a 304-acre neighborhood has 1.4 miles of Greenway and Saddle Ridge, a 353-acre neighborhood
will have 3.4 miles of Greenway upon final construction.
4. Developer proposed park land should only be accepted based on the requirements set forth in
the most current addition of the City’s Parks and Recreation Master Plan and Cheyenne Unified
Development Code.  Minimum requirements include, but are not limited to, a minimum of 50%
of the park site being usable for typical recreation facilities, such as sport fields and courts,
playgrounds, and other facilities. Of this 50%, the property should have an average slope of 1-
5% and all proposed park amenities must accommodate persons with disabilities.  According to
staff calculations, all areas within Tracts 1,2 or 3 represent a minimum grade of 12.5% to up to
50% slope and do not meet these requirements.  The existing Saddle Ridge Park meets these
requirements and is a great amenity for the neighborhood.
5. Staff believes that the donation of Lot 11, Block 2 of the Saddle Ridge 16th Filing, that serves as
property used specifically for the Greenway connection to Christensen is important for the city
to own or to obtain easement on.
This is a lot to think about when considering future maintenance obligations and costs imposed on city
staff.
